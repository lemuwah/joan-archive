# Corrections Log — 2026-09-04: Bates Pronoun + Mark ≠ Illiterate

## What Was Wrong

### 1. Bates Pronoun
Multiple files referred to Louisa Prosser Bates as "his" or "him." Bates is female. Corrected to "her" / "she" throughout.

**Affected files (this commit):**

### 2. "Mark" ≠ Illiterate
The transcription of Image 10 described H. Tibbits's mark as "illiterate witness" and John Green's signature as "literate." Both are narrative smoothing violations:

- **"Illiterate"** is an inference about WHY someone used a mark. The archive does not know the reason. A mark could be:
  - An X (common English practice for those who could not write)
  - An H or other letter (personal initial)
  - A merchant's mark (used by literate traders as a brand)
  - An indigenous mark (pictographic, symbolic, or personal)
  - A symbol from another tradition entirely
  - We cannot tell from the manuscript at this resolution what the mark actually looks like

- **"Literate"** is an inference about John Green's education from the fact that he wrote his name. Observable fact: he signed by hand, not by mark. Whether he was "literate" in any broader sense is unknown.

**The archive's job is to describe what the manuscript shows, not to interpret why people signed the way they did. Calling a mark "illiterate" presupposes English alphabetic literacy as the default and deviations from it as deficiency. That is exactly the colonial framing the archive is trying to avoid.**

**Corrected language:**
- "H. Tibbits his Mark" → described as "witnessed by mark (type and nature of mark unknown at this resolution)"
- "John Green" → described as "signed by hand (not by mark)"
- All instances of "illiterate" removed from transcription annotations
- All instances of "literate" removed from transcription annotations

**Law:** This is a **Law 1 (No Narrative Smoothing)** correction. Inferring literacy or illiteracy from the presence or absence of a handwritten signature is smoothing — it fills in a gap the manuscript does not address.
