# Quest Contract
## Quest for Joan Greene's origins

**Status:** ACTIVE DESIGN CONTRACT  
**Canonical Quest Name:** Quest for Joan Greene's origins

---

## 1. Purpose

The Quest for Joan Greene's origins is the archive's governing research objective for investigating the documentary origins and surrounding context of Joan Greene, wife of John Greene, known in the 1682 record.

The Quest seeks to determine, from documentary evidence, what can be established about Joan Greene's origins, relationships, circumstances, and family/network context.

The Quest does not assume in advance which documentary path will reveal that evidence.

Joan Greene is treated as a findable historical person whose documentary trail has not yet been sufficiently recovered.

The present absence or scarcity of records is a condition of the research problem, not an identity conclusion.

---

## 2. Evidence-First Objective

The governing objective is:

> Determine, from documentary evidence, what can be established about the origins and surrounding family/network context of Joan Greene, wife of John Greene, known in the 1682 record, while preserving competing possibilities, uncertainty, contradictions, and disconfirming evidence.

The system must learn from the evidence where to look.

It must not require the answer to be known before the search begins.

---

## 3. What the Quest Does Not Presume

The Quest does not presume:

- a maiden name for Joan Greene;
- a birthplace for Joan Greene;
- a parentage for Joan Greene;
- a particular ethnicity or community identity;
- a particular religious affiliation;
- a particular marriage pathway;
- a particular place of residence before 1682;
- a particular place of residence after 1682;
- a particular record family as the route to discovery;
- a particular jurisdiction as the route to discovery;
- that every record referring to a John Greene refers to the same man;
- that a John Greene appearing in one record can be identified with another John Greene without evidence;
- that Joan and John met through any particular social, familial, geographic, economic, religious, or legal pathway;
- that a particular candidate relationship is correct;
- that absence from a searched record set establishes absence from historical reality.

No hypothesis, candidate identity, relationship, or documentary pathway is privileged by the Quest contract.

---

## 4. John Greene Is a Research Object

Because the identity of John Greene is necessary context for understanding Joan Greene's documentary environment, the Quest may investigate John Greene as a first-class research object.

This includes, where relevant:

- distinguishing among men named John Greene;
- reconstructing individual John Greene chronologies;
- tracing residences and jurisdictions;
- tracing land transactions;
- tracing associates and witnesses;
- tracing household and family relationships;
- tracing economic, legal, civic, religious, and community records;
- comparing records before, during, and after the 1682 period;
- testing continuity and discontinuity between records;
- identifying documentary networks surrounding candidate John Greene identities.

The commonness of the name John Greene is a reason for stronger identity controls, not a reason to collapse records into a single person.

A record referring to John Greene must not be assigned to the Quest's John Greene merely because the name matches.

---

## 5. Joan and John Must Be Investigated Together Without Being Collapsed

The Quest permits research paths in which evidence concerning John Greene helps locate, distinguish, contextualize, or test evidence concerning Joan Greene.

It also permits research paths beginning with Joan Greene, their household, associates, places, transactions, or surrounding records.

The system must preserve the distinction between:

1. evidence directly concerning Joan Greene;
2. evidence concerning a candidate John Greene;
3. evidence concerning another person with the same or similar name;
4. evidence concerning an associate or related person;
5. inference connecting those records.

A relationship between records must be explicitly established and provenance-preserved.

Name similarity alone is not sufficient.

---

## 6. Search-Path Neutrality

The Quest must permit investigation of every plausible documentary pathway supported by the evidence.

A search pathway may emerge from:

- a person;
- a candidate identity;
- a place;
- a date;
- a transaction;
- a land record;
- a court record;
- a probate record;
- a church record;
- a civil record;
- a tax or town record;
- a military record;
- an economic record;
- an associate;
- a witness;
- a household relationship;
- a family relationship;
- a geographic connection;
- a record-family pattern;
- a negative or contradictory result;
- a newly discovered documentary structure.

The Quest must not encode a preferred pathway merely because it is currently plausible.

Search strategy may change as evidence accumulates.

A newly discovered pathway may generate new search targets without becoming a conclusion.

---

## 7. Temporal Scope

The 1682 record is a known anchor, not the boundary of the investigation.

The Quest may investigate records before, during, and after 1682 when those records are relevant to establishing:

- identity;
- continuity;
- relationships;
- residence;
- chronology;
- associates;
- family/network structure;
- documentary context;
- or competing explanations.

A record's date alone does not establish its relevance or identity connection.

---

## 8. Geographic Scope

The Quest must not assume that the correct documentary trail is confined to the jurisdiction of the 1682 record.

Geographic expansion is permitted when evidence creates a reason to investigate another place, jurisdiction, community, repository, or record system.

Jurisdiction must be established as a research relationship rather than inferred solely from a person's name.

---

## 9. Competing Scenarios

The Quest must preserve multiple plausible scenarios simultaneously when the available evidence does not distinguish among them.

A scenario may concern:

- who John Greene was;
- where John Greene lived;
- where Joan Greene came from;
- how Joan and John became connected;
- which family or community networks connect them;
- which jurisdiction contains relevant records;
- which record family may contain the missing evidence;
- whether an apparent connection is genuine or a same-name collision.

The system must be capable of producing searches that could disprove a currently plausible scenario.

No scenario becomes the Quest's assumed explanation merely because it produces useful search targets.

---

## 10. Negative and Disconfirming Evidence

Searches must preserve negative results, inaccessible sources, conflicting records, identity collisions, and failed pathways.

A failed search pathway is research information.

Negative evidence must remain bounded by its documented search scope.

The Quest must never convert:

- not found
- not yet searched
- source inaccessible
- source not indexed
- source unread
- conflicting evidence

into a stronger statement of historical absence than the evidence supports.

---

## 11. Evidence and Inference Boundary

The Quest distinguishes:

**Documentary observation**
from
**identity assignment**
from
**relationship inference**
from
**hypothesis**.

Search results, snippets, OCR, indexes, secondary references, and algorithmic matches may generate leads.

They do not by themselves establish identity, relationship, parentage, origin, or historical fact.

Original documentary evidence governs conclusions.

---

## 12. Explicit Quest Lineage

Every control-plane object participating in this Quest must have explicit machine-readable Quest membership.

Quest membership must not be reconstructed from:

- question text;
- person names;
- filenames;
- directory location;
- record-family labels;
- human memory;
- keyword matching;
- inferred ancestry.

The intended lineage is:

```text
QUEST
  |
  └── ROOT EVENT
        |
        └── SEARCH TARGET
              |
              └── EXECUTION EVENT
                    |
                    ├── SEARCH ARRIVAL
                    │     |
                    │     └── LEAD
                    │           |
                    │           └── CHILD SEARCH TARGET
                    |
                    └── NEGATIVE / CONFLICTING RESULT
The lineage must remain mechanically traceable.

An empty parent field must not be interpreted as Quest membership or as a semantic root.

13. Separation From Conclusions

The Quest is a research-control object.

It does not establish:

Joan's identity beyond the evidence already admitted;
Joan's parentage;
Joan's origin;
a maiden name;
a John Greene identity;
a relationship between candidate records;
or the truth of any hypothesis.

Those conclusions belong to the evidence, claim, relationship, review, and analysis layers governed by their respective contracts.

14. Governing Laws

The Quest operates under the archive's evidence-first laws, including:

La Mance Law / Follow the Rivers;
No Premature Elimination;
No Algorithmic Contamination;
No Jurisdictional Assumption;
No Centering;
No Trust Without Evidence.

These laws govern how the Quest searches, preserves uncertainty, evaluates evidence, and handles competing explanations.

The Quest does not override evidence or other control-plane contracts.

15. Discovery Principle

The Quest follows this principle:

We do not need to know what Joan's documentary trail looks like before searching for it. We need a control system capable of learning what to look for without turning each new lead into an assumption.

The purpose of the Quest is therefore not to force the historical record into a predetermined identity model.

Its purpose is to preserve enough search breadth, identity discipline, provenance, and disproof capability for the documentary record to determine what can ultimately be established.

16. Scope of This Contract

This contract defines the semantic meaning and boundaries of the Quest.

It does not yet define:

the machine-readable Quest schema;
the Quest ledger;
the Quest identifier format;
Quest creation tooling;
Quest-to-target implementation;
Quest-specific scheduling;
deferred-lead re-entry;
orchestrator changes;
public-page projection.

Those are implementation questions to be audited and designed separately.
