Analysis

Evidence first. Uncertainty preserved. No identity inferred beyond the record.

The current documentary starting point consists of four recorded observations:

  1. Joan is named as wife of John Greene in a March 1682 land deed (Worth abstract).
  2. A life annuity is reserved to her (the Worth abstract gives 30 shillings/year; the direct manuscript read shows a potentially different figure — this is an open question).
  3. Joan does not sign or mark the deed (working read of manuscript Image 10; professional paleography pending).
  4. No other record of Joan has been located.

The research directions below are questions, not conclusions. Claims must be assessed against the sources relevant to them, with uncertainty and conflicting evidence preserved. Law 6 (No Centering) supports broad inquiry without privileging a presumed identity.

Research Directions and Evidentiary Limits

These directions organize the questions and sources that may help investigate Joan's undocumented life. They overlap; they are not competing identity labels. No direction is a conclusion about Joan, and none is preferred because of the ancestry or social status it might suggest.

Community, Kinship, and Relationships

Evidence sought: Records that directly connect Joan to named relatives, households, communities, or other identifiable people.

What a finding could establish: A specific relationship or community connection, if the source and identity match are reliable. A name resemblance or shared location alone would not establish that Joan and another person are the same individual.

Outstanding questions: Is there a documentary bridge connecting Joan to a particular family or community? Can that bridge be independently corroborated?

Church, Migration, and Family Records

Evidence sought: Parish entries, church records, migration references, marriage records, and family documents that can be matched using dates, places, relationships, and other identifying details.

What a finding could establish: A documented event or connection for Joan if the identification is secure. An English-language record establishes how a person was recorded in that source; it does not by itself establish a person's complete ancestry or exclude other affiliations.

Outstanding questions: Can a candidate record be tied to this Joan rather than someone with a similar name? Which relevant record groups have actually been searched?

Content note: The following research direction includes the histories of forced servitude, enslavement, captivity, and colonial violence. These subjects are included for historical investigation, not because Joan's circumstances are known or presumed. The archive distinguishes source language, transcription, interpretation, and its own narration.

Households, Labor, Courts, and Legal Status

Evidence sought: Court proceedings, indentures, household records, and records that document freedom, coercion, captivity, or enslavement where such records survive.

What a finding could establish: A specific legal status or circumstance when a record identifies Joan and its context supports that reading. Neither a lack of prior records nor a property or annuity provision alone settles her legal status.

Outstanding questions: Are there records directly connecting Joan to a labor arrangement, legal proceeding, or documented condition? What do the relevant laws and records mean in their historical context?

Marriage, Probate, Property, and Inheritance

Evidence sought: Marriage records, wills, probate files, deeds, and inheritance records that may document a surname, prior marriage, family relationship, or legal interest.

What a finding could establish: The relationship or right stated in a source, subject to its wording and provenance. The deed's life provision does not by itself establish Joan's maiden name, ancestry, or full legal history.

Outstanding questions: Can a record be linked to Joan by more than a shared name? Are relevant probate and marriage records extant and accessible?

Colonial Records and Indigenous Scholarship

Evidence sought: Carefully contextualized land, court, household, and community records, considered alongside relevant scholarship and guidance from appropriate Indigenous historians and community authorities.

What a finding could establish: A particular documented connection when the evidence supports it. Colonial record categories may omit or distort aspects of people's lives; that limitation calls for careful interpretation, not an assumed alternative identity.

Outstanding questions: What relationships are actually documented? What remains invisible in the surviving colonial record? What additional interpretation or community guidance is appropriate?

The archive does not identify Joan as Narragansett or equate her with Anashuecot. Those remain unestablished claims, not findings.

Names, Language, and Manuscript Interpretation

Evidence sought: Original manuscript images, careful transcription, spelling variants, and records that can distinguish people with similar names.

What a finding could establish: A defensible reading of particular wording or a documented identity match. A transcription, an interpretation, and the original source are distinct and should remain distinguishable.

Outstanding questions: Which readings require professional paleography? What does the original execution block show? The maintenance amount and other disputed readings must remain open until the evidence supports a resolution.

Genetic Evidence and Documented Lineage

Evidence sought: Traceable test provenance, identification of the tested person and ancestral line, and a documented genealogical bridge to the research question.

What a finding could establish: A result can inform the lineage the test actually examines. Y-DNA concerns a direct paternal line; without a documented connection relevant to Joan, it cannot by itself establish her origin or ancestry.

Outstanding questions: Whose DNA was tested? Which line does it represent? Is a reliable connection to Joan established, or is the result contextual information only?

Earlier Identifications and the Correction Record

Evidence examined: The historical identification of Joan with Joan Beggarly / Alice Daniels associated with La Mance (1904).

Documented disposition: This identification was rejected because the chronology and identity do not fit Joan of Quidnessett: Alice Daniels Beggerly died c. 1643/44, while Joan is named in the 1682 deed, and the proposed identification involves the wrong John Greene.

This is a recorded correction, not an open identity possibility. Its history remains available in CORRECTIONS.md.

How to assess any result: Record the source, provenance, exact claim supported, limitations, and competing explanations. Preserve negative results as search history rather than proof of absence. Update conclusions when warranted, without erasing the evidence or reasoning that led to earlier conclusions.

The Honest Assessment (September 2026)

The archive has not established Joan's maiden name, family, origin, or earlier life. The four facts above define the current documentary starting point; they do not establish her ancestry, community affiliation, or legal status beyond what the deed actually records.

Research coverage is uneven. The amount of material found reflects where searches have been conducted, not the relative likelihood of any explanation. No research direction is ranked by preference, and absence of a record is not proof of absence.

The manuscript has received an image review, but some readings remain unresolved and professional paleography is pending. Further work should document which images and records have been examined, what remains outstanding, and what each source can and cannot establish. Law 5 (No Jurisdictional Assumption) and Law 6 (No Centering) support broad, even-handed searching without premature conclusions.
