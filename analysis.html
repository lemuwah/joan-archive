Analysis

Seven hypotheses, equal standards. The reader synthesizes. The archive does not.

Every model below starts from the same three verified facts:

  1. Joan is named as wife of John Greene in a March 1682 land deed (Worth abstract).
  2. A life annuity of 30 shillings per year is reserved to her.
  3. No other record of Joan has been located.

Everything below these facts is hypothesis. No model is privileged. Under Law 6 (No Centering), each receives equal treatment.

Model A — Narragansett Sachem-Line Woman

⚫ OPEN HYPOTHESIS

Basis: Joan's 40-year documentary silence is unique among Quidnessett wives. The Quidnessett / Cocumscussoc region was a documented site of English–Indigenous interaction. Phonetic variants (Anashuecot / Awashouse) appear in Narragansett land transactions in the same territory and era.

What would confirm: A bridging document connecting Joan to an Indigenous individual or community. Joan's mark (if found in the manuscript) showing an Indigenous pictographic pattern. DNA results connecting the Greene line to Indigenous ancestry.

What would eliminate: An English parish record for Joan. A marriage record in any English colony. Evidence that other frontier wives have similarly thin records.

Not yet searched: The manuscript (images 9–12). Tomaquag Museum review.

Model B — English Woman, Undocumented

⚫ OPEN HYPOTHESIS

Basis: "Joan" is an English name. She appears in English legal instruments, married to an English-named man. The absence of records for colonial women is common, though Joan's silence is unusually deep compared to neighboring wives.

What would confirm: An English parish record for a Joan matching colony, dates, and marriage to a John Greene. Evidence that frontier wives commonly have this level of documentary silence.

What would eliminate: A positive identification of Joan as Indigenous, Irish, or otherwise non-English.

Not yet searched: First Baptist Church Providence rolls. Newport Historical Society church records. Massachusetts court records 1637–1645.

Model C — Irish Origin

⚫ OPEN HYPOTHESIS

Basis: Y-DNA results point to an Irish Sea genetic cluster. Irish immigration to New England in this period is documented but under-studied. Irish women in colonial New England often left minimal records.

What would confirm: An Irish parish record for Joan. Ship manifest showing Irish immigration to the Narragansett region. Further DNA analysis narrowing the connection.

What would eliminate: DNA ruling out Irish connection. Positive identification of another origin.

Not yet searched: Irish parish records.

Model D — Indentured Servant

⚫ OPEN HYPOTHESIS

Basis: Would explain absence of maiden name, family, and parish record. Post-indenture marriage to a landholder is documented in the colonial period.

What would confirm: An indenture record for a Joan in RI, MA, or Plymouth. Court record showing a former servant named Joan marrying in the Narragansett region.

What would eliminate: Evidence of Joan having property rights inconsistent with former servant status.

Not yet searched: RI, MA, Plymouth indenture records.

Model E — Enslaved Person

⚫ OPEN HYPOTHESIS

Basis: Post-1676 legal framework created conditions where Indigenous and African enslaved persons entered English households. Would explain total absence of prior identity in English records.

What would confirm: A record of an enslaved woman named Joan in the Narragansett region. Evidence of enslavement in the Greene household.

What would eliminate: Evidence of Joan's free status prior to the marriage. The annuity provision may be legally unusual for an enslaved person, but not impossible.

Not yet searched: Post-1676 RI enslavement records.

Model F — Widow of Prior Marriage

⚫ OPEN HYPOTHESIS

Basis: Remarriage of widows was common in colonial New England. Could explain legal standing and absence of maiden name (she may be known by a prior husband's name in other records).

What would confirm: A probate or marriage record for a Joan [surname] who remarried John Greene.

What would eliminate: A marriage record showing Joan's maiden name. Evidence that John Greene was her first and only husband.

Not yet searched: RI probate records pre-1682.

Model G — Joan Beggarly / Alice Daniels (La Mance 1904)

🔴 ELIMINATED

La Mance (1904) conflated multiple women into "Joan Beggarly." Alice Daniels Beggerly died c. 1643/44. Joan of Quidnessett is alive in 1682. A dead woman cannot receive a life-estate. Timeline impossible. Wrong John Greene (Surgeon John of Warwick, not Quidnessett John). Wrong town.

See CORRECTIONS.md #2.

The Honest Assessment (September 2026)

Six models remain open. None has positive direct evidence. All are built on the same three verified facts plus varying amounts of structural, contextual, and circumstantial indicators.

Model A (Narragansett) has the most developed circumstantial case. Model B (English) has the strongest prior. Models C–F have not been adequately searched to evaluate.

The archive does not rank these models by preference. It notes that the amount of evidence gathered is itself biased by where the archive has looked so far. Law 5 (No Jurisdictional Assumption) and Law 6 (No Centering) require that all models be searched with equal rigor.

The manuscript (FamilySearch DGS 008204949, images 9–12) remains the single most decisive unexamined source.
