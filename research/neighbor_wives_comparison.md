# The Neighbor-Wives Calibration Study — is Joan's silence normal?

**Status:** ⚠️ PRELIMINARY — one verified row, nine unresolved rows. Do not quote the conclusion as settled until the unresolved households are checked against originals.
**Date:** 2026-09-09
**Purpose:** Calibrate what Joan's documentary silence means. If every peer wife is invisible, her silence is structural (favors Model B). If she is a lone outlier, the silence is distinctive (favors models predicting distance from English record systems).
**Method:** Take the documented men in Joan's immediate network (the 1672 Fones Purchase buyers, the 1682 deed witnesses, the neighboring proprietors) and ask of each: **is his wife ever named in any located record?** Then compare against Joan (one appearance, ever).

## What the one verified row tells us

Sarah Stanton Tibbitts is visible in **three different record systems** — religious (Quaker marriage), probate (the will), administrative (the tax list). Two things made her visible:

1. **She married into a Quaker network.** Friends' meeting records named women — with their own roles — when town clerks and deed-writers routinely didn't. This is the single strongest structural finding of the study: *record visibility for a wife in this place and period tracks religious affiliation more than anything else.*
2. **Her father-in-law's will survived** (fire-damaged but multiply abstracted) and named her.

Joan has no Quaker record, no church record, no surviving husband's will naming her (John's will is reported lost), and no probate of her own. She appears only in the one record system where a wife couldn't be left out without legal risk to the transaction: the deed reserving her annuity.

## Preliminary reading (updated after the Austin sweep, 2026-09-09)

- **Five of ten peer wives are now visible** (Stanton via Quaker records; Margaret Fones, Mary Andrew, Elizabeth Gould, Phillip(a) Carr via Austin's record abstracts). Five remain unresolved.
- **The early leaning is shifting toward "Joan's silence is distinctive."** Half her peer wives DO surface — in wills, deeds, marriage registers, and tax lists. Joan surfaces in none of those systems despite her husband being at least as documented as Fones, Andrew, or Carr. If the five unresolved rows also resolve to visible wives, Joan becomes the clear outlier of the set.
- **Counterweight, honestly stated:** all five newly-visible wives surface through *published secondary abstracts* (Austin). None has a pulled original yet. And three of the visible five are named only in their husband's estate documents — the exact class of document that is *lost* for John Greene (his will is reported burned). If John's will had survived like Carr's and Tibbitts's did, Joan might be as "visible" as any of them. The comparison must therefore be read as: **Joan is invisible in the records THAT SURVIVE for her household** — which mixes her actual record-visibility with the 1869 fire's selection effect.
- The **religious-affiliation pattern holds**: the one wife visible outside estate documents is the Quaker-married one.
- Bonus structural find: **Elizabeth Gould's settlement was an annuity** — £10/year for life in lieu of Narragansett land claims — the same instrument family as Joan's 30-shilling life annuity. A direct comparison case now exists for Stage 2 of the road.
