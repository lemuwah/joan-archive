## The 1654 Deed — Full Witness Line

Dated **13 July 1654**. Tacommonan deeds meadows and mowable land on Petawhomett Neck to Randall Howldon and Ezechill Hollyman (both of Warwick).

**Property:** Petawhomett Neck, on Narragansett Bay, opposite south end of Shawomett Neck. Bounded east by bay, south by Potowomett River, north by Coeassett Bay, extending four miles westward up the Potowomett River.

**Consideration:** £15 in wampum peag + annual gratuity of one coat "according to the custom of the English."

**Full witness line:**
> "Tocommonan X his marke. Jeremiah Westcott. **Awassuocitt X his marke** William Baker eldest son to him Samuell Edsall. above Wawnunockshaw X his marke an other son."

### Persons in the Deed

- **Tacommonan** — grantor, "Right owner" of Petawhomett Neck. ⚠️ Note spelling: Tacommonan, NOT Tacommanan. Another variant.
- **Tocommonan** — witness, signed by mark. ⚠️ Nearly identical to the grantor's name. Are these two different people, or the same person (grantor also witnesses)? Held open.
- **Jeremiah Westcott** — English witness
- **Awassuocitt** — witness, signed by mark. NOT described as sachem, co-owner, or political representative. Status beyond "witness" not indicated.
- **William Baker** — described as "eldest son to him [Awassuocitt]"
- **Samuell Edsall** — English witness
- **Wawnunockshaw** — described as "an other son" [of Awassuocitt], signed by mark

## The William Baker Problem 🚨

### What the Deed Says
The text explicitly calls William Baker "eldest son to him" — grammatically referring back to Awassuocitt.

### What the Colonial Record Says
A William Baker appears as an **English freeman/inhabitant of Aquidneck (Portsmouth)** as early as **1638**. He has a wife named **Mary**, consistent with an English colonial identity.

### The Interpretive Problem

Two readings:

**Reading A — Baker is Awassuocitt's biological son:**
- An English name for a Native sachem's son in 1654 would indicate deep cross-cultural engagement
- Not unprecedented: Narragansett sachems sometimes gave children English names or had children with English partners
- The deed text literally says "eldest son to him"

**Reading B — Baker is a separate English witness:**
- The witness line punctuation is ambiguous in the abstract
- "Eldest son to him" could be a clerking convention describing Baker's role/relationship differently
- Baker's documented Portsmouth residence and English wife fit an English identity
- No other source identifies Baker as a sachem's son

### What Would Resolve

**Would support Reading A:**
- Baker identified as Native or mixed-race elsewhere
- Baker appearing at Quidnessett/Potowomut with Narragansett connections
- Baker absent from typical English colonial records (probate, freeman lists) after 1654
- Original manuscript showing different punctuation/layout

**Would support Reading B:**
- Baker appearing as a separate English party in other deeds without Native connection
- Clear English genealogy for this Baker (parents, English marriage, English children)
- Original manuscript showing Baker on a separate line from Awassuocitt

**Status: HELD OPEN per Law 1. The deed text says one thing; the colonial record context suggests another.**

## Wawnunockshaw

- Identified as Awassuocitt's "an other son" in the 1654 deed
- Signs by mark (→ Narragansett, not English)
- **ZERO appearances** in any other searched source:
  - Not in Potter 1835
  - Not in *Narragansett Historical Register*
  - Not in any other RI Land Evidences entry
  - Not in King Philip's War records
- Could this be an early name for one of the 1672 Fones Purchase sons (Seecomp, Tyecuecsha, Nammeash)? **No evidence found either way.**
- Potter lists similar-sounding but distinct names: Wanumachon/Wanomachin (Point Judith sachem), Wawaloam (Miantonomi's wife). No equation with Wawnunockshaw.

**Status: DEAD END unless new source surfaces.**

## Awassuocitt — Broader Search Results

- The exact spelling "Awassuocitt" is **NOT confirmed** in any other RI Land Evidences entry beyond the 1654 deed
- The 1672 Fones Purchase uses **Anashuecot/Awashueset** — closely related but differently spelled
- Whether Awassuocitt (1654) and Anashuecot (1672) are the same individual is **widely assumed but NOT confirmed** from available digitized records
- Physical consultation of the RI Land Evidences index volume would likely clarify

### Phonetic Comparison
- **Aw-ass-uoci-tt** (1654) ↔ **Aw-ash-ue-cot** (1672) ↔ **An-ash-ue-cot** (1672)
- Shared structure: initial Aw-/An- + medial -ash-/-ass- + terminal -cot/-citt
- Within established variant cluster
- **But this is phonetic analysis, not documentary proof**

## Tocommonan — A New Variant

The deed lists both **Tacommonan** (grantor) and **Tocommonan** (witness by mark). These may be:
1. The same person (grantor signing as witness to his own deed — unusual but not unheard of)
2. Two different people with nearly identical names (father/son?)
3. A clerking error

**Tocommonan** adds to the variant table:
- Tomanick (1644)
- Tocommonan (1654 witness)
- Tacommonan (1654 grantor)
- Tocomino (c. 1652, recorded 1660-61)
- Tacomanan (1659)
- Tacommanan (1660)
- Tocomminon (1677-78)
- Tacummanan (1679)

⚠️ The 1654 deed has BOTH Tacommonan and Tocommonan. If these are the same person, it confirms extreme spelling instability within a single document.

## Impact on the 34-Year Arc Hypothesis

The Awassuocitt = Anashuecot identification remains **phonetically strong but documentarily unbridged.** The William Baker complication doesn't disprove it — even if Baker is a separate English witness, Awassuocitt and Wawnunockshaw are clearly Native (signed by mark). But the ambiguity about Awassuocitt's sons weakens the hypothesis that he was already a sachem with English-named children in 1654.

The strongest version of the arc remains:

**1644:** Awashousse (counselor to Pessicus)
**1654:** Awassuocitt (witness on Tacommonan deed) — ⚠️ UNPROVEN same person
**1661:** Awashouse (Nisquitianxsett deed)
**1672:** Anashuecot ("chiefe Sachem and heire properly")
**1676:** Anaftiawin (court martial) — ⚠️ UNPROVEN same person
**1678:** "Conveyed out of ye country" (Absolom affidavit)
