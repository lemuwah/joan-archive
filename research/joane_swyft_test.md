## What the record shows (verbatim anchors, Plymouth Colony Records vols. 1–2 — IA scan `recordsofcolonyo0102newp`)

1. **1642 (court orders):** *"Lres of administracbn are graunted to **Joane Swyft, of Sandwich**, to administer vpon her husbands estate, and to pay the debts as farr as the estate will amount vnto, by equall pporcSns, and is bound to the Govr & Assistants to do it, & Daniell Wing wth her."* — a widow running an estate, bonded, named.
2. **11 March 1642:** *"Joane Swyft, administratrix of Wm Swyft, deceased, hath payd to John Barnes v[iii] li. & iiij [d]… yt amounting to pay eich of his creditors vj s. viij d. in the pound… and hath deliv[ere]d unto her her husbands bills & writings for that money…"* — she settles debts at 6s 8d on the pound, in writing, by name.
3. **1640s (court, undated in scan flow):** *"A warrant to be sent forth to bring in the bodies of Jonathan Fish and Mary, his wyfe, Nathaniell Fish, Jane, the wyfe of Mr Willim Wood, Rose, the wyfe of Joseph Holly, __, the wyfe of Richard Kerby, __, the wyfe of Michaell Turner, & **Joane Swyft, widdow**, to giue euedence in John Ellis & his wifes case."* — one of six wives/widows summoned to testify; note the record knows the other five only as "the wyfe of," but names her directly.

**What she shows even before any identity question:** a woman with no property of her own can still be named, bonded, and called to testify in colonial court records — when the court needs her. Which sharpens the Joan question: the system *could* name women. It never named ours.

## The Geographic Test (Law 5 — No Jurisdictional Assumption)

A candidate Joane fails only if geography, chronology, or network makes her impossible — not because she sits in an unexpected colony's records.

### Leg 1 — Place identity
- **Sandwich** = Sandwich, Plymouth Colony (Cape Cod), founded 1637. No period alias connecting it to Quidnessett/Kingstown exists in [geography_index.md](geography_index.md); no "Sandwich" appears anywhere in the Quidnessett record set. ⚠️ *However:* the index is built from Joan's known network. A migration alias would only show up in records we haven't read. **Open, leaning distinct.**
- Movement Sandwich → Narragansett country 1640s is *plausible in the abstract* (families moved constantly; the ferry-and-coast trade ran the whole bay). Plausible is not documented.

### Leg 2 — Chronology
- Widowed by 1642, administering and paying debts 1642–43, still "Joane Swyft, widdow" when summoned later in the 1640s.
- **Our Joan** must be married to John Greene early enough for the compiled children (~1643 onward). A widow settling her husband's estate through 1643 and testifying as a *Swyft* widow in the late 1640s has a narrowing window — but not a closed one. Remarriage was common and fast.
- ⚠️ If she is still styled "Swyft" in records after ~1650, the case collapses. That endpoint check is the first cheap test (Plymouth vols. 3–4 sweep, queued below).

### Leg 3 — Network collision (the decisive leg)
- Her recorded network: **Daniell Wing** (co-bonded), **John Barnes** (creditor), the **Ellis case** witnesses (Fish, Wood, Holly, Kerby, Turner households) — all Sandwich/upper-Cape people.
- John's recorded network: Smith/Cocumscussoc, Quidnessett proprietors. **Zero overlap located.**
- What would change this: any Swyft–Wing–Barnes name surfacing in Narragansett-country records, or any Smith/Cocumscussoc name surfacing in Sandwich records. None located today.

### Leg 4 — The name
"Joane" is one of the most common English women's names of the period — the name match carries no weight alone. It earns the test, not the identity.

## Verdict at open of test

**Cannot rule out; cannot rule in.** She passes no leg and fails no leg. She stays on the board as ⚠️ CANDIDATE — SANDWICH LINE, listed beside Models A–H's people, not among them. ## T1/T2 results (2026-09-09, both run in-house, texts already downloaded)

**T1 — INCONCLUSIVE, leaning negative for the candidate.** The Plymouth scan in hand covers only 1633–1651, and its last Swyft is the 1640s witness summons. The 1651–1668 continuation lives in the *next* Shurtleff volume (`...newp_0` or v.3–4 scan — not yet downloaded). What T1 did establish: **through 1651 she is still "Joane Swyft, widdow"** — nine years after 1642, no remarriage on record. Every year that holds, the window for her becoming "Joan Greene by ~1643–1650" narrows further. The decisive volume is queued.

**T2 — NO COLLISION in RI records, with one adjacency flagged.** Bartlett RI vols. 1–3: **Swyft/Swift = 0** (no Swyft ever surfaces in the RI colony record 1636–1706). Wing/Barnes hits are mostly common nouns ("wing of your Royall protection," "barnes having corne"). The real surnames that do appear:
- **John Wing** and **Jonathan Fish** — admitted freemen *together in the same Portsmouth list* (RI vol. 3, p. 420-era). The Fish surname is in Joane's own witness cluster (Jonathan Fish and Mary his wife). Portsmouth is Aquidneck Island — her network touches the bay, but **Portsmouth ≠ Newport ≠ Quidnessett**, and a John Wing of Portsmouth is not Daniell Wing of Sandwich. ⚠️ Adjacency logged; not a collision. What WOULD upgrade it: any record putting a Wing or Fish in *North Kingstown/Kingstown/Quidnessett*.
- Barneses in RI vol. 3 (Peter of Providence, John of Providence/Newport) — same-town-as-network, not same-network.
- **Collision verdict: none.** The Sandwich cluster and the Quidnessett cluster do not touch in any record read to date.
