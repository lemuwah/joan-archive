## What Potter Says (verbatim extracts)

### Page 58 — Coganaquant confirmed as real sachem

> "Coganaquant, came before me and owned his hand and scale to the deed of gift herein specified, and declares that he was not in drink, but sober at the affecting of it, and denies that it was a sale; and further saith that when he sold the land to Maj. Atherton and the rest of the Bay-men, in presence of Richard Smith, he, the said Coganaquant excepted the neck of land herein specified, being given to Capt. Randal Holden, and Mr. Samuel Gorton, Sen., and further saith that he never made any lease thereof to Richard Smith, for more time than three years. Taken before me, JOHN GREENE, Assistant. Warwick, May 18, 1668."

**🚨 CRITICAL:** This is **Quidnessett John** taking Coganaquant's testimony in 1668 as a colonial Assistant. John Greene is not just a peripheral witness — he is the MAGISTRATE recording Coganaquant's protest against the Atherton Company's overreach.

### Page 58 — Atherton Purchase of Quidnessett (1659)

> "June 11, 1659. Maj. Humphrey Atherton and his partners, purchased Aquitawoset or Quidnesett, of Coqinaquand, bounded on the North-east by Mascacowage brook, by Cocumscussuc brook on the South-west, the sea on the South, and the common path on the North-west; excepting what had been sold before to Smith."

**Note:** Potter spells it **Coqinaquand** here (likely OCR artifact for Coginaquand). The seller of Quidnessett itself is Coginaquand — the same sachem who later protests to John Greene.

### Page 58 — Confirmation by three sachems (1660)

> "June 14, 1660. These purchases were confirmed by Quissucquansh, Scultop, and Quequaquomet."

### Page 60 — Tacommonan submission (1659)

> "Aug. 26, 1659, Tacommanan, (the father,) Wasewkit, his son, and Namowish, his grandchild, make a formal submission of the Coheassuck lands to the R. I. government."

**🚨 MATCH:** This is the SAME Tacommanan/Tocomminon named as Anashuecot's father in the 1677/78 Absolom affidavit. And **Namowish** = possible match for open thread #9 (Namowish → Nammeash).

### Page 61 — Mortgage signers (1660)

> "Oct. 30, Quissucquansh, Ninigret, Scuttup and Wequaqueniut, mortgage all the unsold lands in Narragansett to Atherton & Co."

## Name-Cluster Analysis

### Coginaquand / Coganaquant Cluster

| Source | Spelling | Context | Date |
|--------|----------|---------|------|
| Potter p. 58 | Coganaquant | Protests to John Greene as Assistant | 1668 |
| Potter p. 58 | Coqinaquand | Sells Quidnessett to Atherton | 1659 |
| Potter p. 83 | Cachauaquant, alias Jassarono | Sells Nannaquokset to Holden/Gorton | 1659 |
| Open thread #13 | Coginiquant / Cogineaquon / Cononjonant | Unresolved cluster |

**Assessment:** Coginaquand is a REAL, documented Narragansett sachem who:
- Sold Quidnessett (John Greene's own land) to the Atherton Company in 1659
- Protested the sale to John Greene personally in 1668
- Is identified as "alias Jassarono" (= Jassarono, a known name in Warwick records)

**This is NOT the same person as Anashuecot.** Coginaquand sold the land; Anashuecot is named as "chiefe Sachem and heire properly" in 1672. They may be related (sachem-line succession?) but they are distinct individuals.

### Quequaquomet / Wequaqueniut / Scuttup Cluster

| Source | Spelling | Context | Date |
|--------|----------|---------|------|
| Potter p. 58 | Quequaquomet | Confirms Atherton purchases | 1660 |
| Potter p. 61 | Wequaqueniut | Mortgages Narragansett lands to Atherton | 1660 |
| Potter p. 61 | Scuttup | Mortgages Narragansett lands to Atherton | 1660 |
| Source spine | Quequaquenuit | "Died young" — last records 1660–1664 | pre-1665 |
| Source spine | Scuttup | Dies 1668 — succession gap | 1668 |

**Assessment:** Wequaqueniut (Potter) = Quequaquenuit (source spine) — phonetically identical. This confirms he was active in 1660 as a sachem-level figure who could mortgage land. His early death (pre-1665) opened a succession gap that Anashuecot may have filled.

### Tacommanan / Namowish

| Source | Spelling | Context | Date |
|--------|----------|---------|------|
| Potter p. 60 | Tacommanan | Submits Coheassuck lands to RI | 1659 |
| Potter p. 60 | Wasewkit | Son of Tacommanan | 1659 |
| Potter p. 60 | Namowish | Grandchild of Tacommanan | 1659 |
| 1677/78 affidavit | Tocomminon | Father of Anashuecot | 1677/78 |

**Assessment:** Tacommanan (1659) = Tocomminon (1677/78) is now HIGHLY PROBABLE. Same territory (Coheassuck/Potowomut is adjacent to Quidnessett), same generation, same political role (land-rights holder). The 1659 submission names his son Wasewkit and grandchild Namowish — Anashuecot is NOT named here, which could mean:
1. Anashuecot was born after 1659
2. Anashuecot was female and excluded from land submissions (consistent with Model A)
3. Anashuecot's claim came through a different line

**Namowish** = possible match for the archive's open thread #9 (Namowish → Nammeash). Weakened but not dead.

### Anashuecot / Awassuocitt

**NOT FOUND IN POTTER.** No variant of Anashuecot appears on pp. 58–61 or in any of the Atherton Purchase documents. This is consistent with a post-1660 emergence or a female sachem excluded from English-facing transactions.

## What This Means for Joan

1. **John Greene was the magistrate who recorded Coginaquand's protest in 1668.** He wasn't just living at Quidnessett — he was the colonial official overseeing Indigenous land disputes ON THAT LAND. His relationship to Narragansett sachems was administrative, not incidental.

2. **The succession question sharpens.** Scuttup (d. 1668) and Quequaquenuit (d. pre-1665) both die young. Coginaquand protests in 1668. By 1672, Anashuecot is named "chiefe Sachem and heire properly." The gap is 1665–1672 — who succeeded whom?

3. **Tacommanan is real and ACTIVE in 1659.** His family controls Coheassuck lands. By 1677/78, his daughter(?) Anashuecot has been "conveyed out of the country" and Absolom testifies about the lineage. The family arc is: land-holder (1659) → sachem-heir (1672) → wartime displacement (1677/78).

4. **Namowish (grandchild, 1659) may be a lead.** If Namowish is Tacommanan's grandchild through a DAUGHTER, that daughter could be Anashuecot's mother or Anashuecot herself under a different name.
