## The Full Kin Group

The deed explicitly names six individuals and their relationships:

> *"I Anashuecot, chiefe Sachem and heire properly of ye land called quckeset … Together with ye volentary & free consent of Wampkegge and Ompamiatt my two Brothers, and Seecomp, Tyecuecsha & Nammeash my three sons…"*

All six described as **"sole heires & proprietors"** of Quidnessett.

### Family Structure

**Generation 1 (siblings):**
- **Anashuecot** — Chief Sachem, "heire properly"
- **Wampkegge** — Anashuecot's brother
- **Ompamiatt** — Anashuecot's brother

**Generation 2 (Anashuecot's sons):**
- **Seecomp** — son
- **Tyecuecsha** — son
- **Nammeash** — son

**Ancestor (from Absolom affidavit):**
- **Tocomminon** — Anashuecot's father. "Tocomminon father to Anashuecot." ⚠️ Affidavit NOT confirmed in *Narragansett Historical Register* — exact forms "Tocomminon" and "Absalom" not found in that volume. Requires verification against original manuscript.

## Phonetic Mapping Against Known Sachem Dynasties

### Ninigret (Niantic) Line
- Saccious → Ninigret I (Janemo) + Wepitanock (brother)
- Ninigret I → Weunquesh (daughter/successor), Ninigret II (half-brother)
- Quaiapen = Ninigret I's sister, married Mixan/Mexanno (Canonicus's eldest son)

### Canonicus (Narragansett) Line
- Canonicus (Quissucquansh) → Mixan/Mexanno (eldest son)
- Mascus (Canonicus's brother) → Miantonomo → Canonchet
- Mixan + Quaiapen → Scuttup, Quequakanewett, Quinimiquet

### Overlap Results

1. **Nammeash** → No close match. The -ash ending is common in Algonquian but insufficient for identification.
2. **Ompamiatt** → Most phonetically suggestive of the group. m/p consonant cluster has some resonance with Wampanoag naming patterns. No documentary bridge to any specific known sachem.
3. **Tyecuecsha** → Weak overlap with Tuckoneosset (shares T-k-u sound). Structurally different. No bridge document.
4. **Wampkegge** → No match. "Wamp-" prefix is widespread in Algonquian (cf. Wampanoag, Wamsutta) but generic.
5. **Seecomp** → No match located.
6. **Tocomminon** → No match located. Not a variant of any known sachem name in sources consulted.

**Conclusion: None of the six kin group members map cleanly onto known Ninigret or Canonicus dynasty members.**

## The Coginaquand Connection

### 1659: Coginaquand Deeds the Same Territory

A sachem named **"Coquinaquant" / Coginaquand** deeded Quidnessett land to Humphrey Atherton on **June 11, 1659** (the Atherton Purchase). He is identified as "Sagamore or Schem of Naraganset."

**Critical context:** The account states he was **made drunk and kept intoxicated for days** before signing. A concealed payment of £75 in wampum was involved.

Coginaquand's relationship to known dynasties: described as **Quaiapen's cousin and the cousin of her deceased husband Mixanno**. No reliable primary-source genealogy directly proves descent from Canonicus or Miantonomo.

**Source:** Potter, *Early History of Narragansett*; WikiTree Atherton Purchase page; RI State Archives catalog.

### 1661: Cachaquant and Awashous Sign Together

The 1661 Nisquitianxsett deed lists grantors as **Awashouse and Nucom**, described as owners of Nisquitianxsett. **Cachaquant/Kacha-aquant** also appears in a separate 1657 deed around Pettaquamscutt Hill.

**Key finding from Track 1 (Aug 28):** Awashouse signed the Nisquitianxsett deed alongside multiple paramount sachems including Cachaquant/Coginaquand. **This proves they are DIFFERENT people.** The Coginaquand name-cluster is definitively separated from the Anashuecot/Awashouse cluster.

### 1672: Anashuecot Claims the Same Territory

Anashuecot asserts he is **"chiefe Sachem and heire properly"** of Quidnessett — the same territory Coginaquand deeded 13 years earlier.

## Reconstructed Succession: Quidnessett Authority

**Coginaquand (1659 grantor) → Tocomminon → Anashuecot (1672 heir)**

⚠️ This is a **political and documentary succession, NOT a fully proven genealogical pedigree.**

### What's established:
- Coginaquand held authority to convey Quidnessett in 1659 ✅

## The Atherton vs. Fones Purchase — Land Claim Overlap

The two purchases were **separate but partly overlapping colonial land claims**:

### Atherton Purchase (1659)
- Included Quidnessett as its "North Purchase"
- RI regarded it as **contrary to its law** requiring colonial consent for private Indian purchases
- RI General Assembly characterized it as **"fraud piled upon extortion"**
- RI refused to recognize the Atherton title

### Fones Purchase (January 1, 1671/72)
- Made by John Greene, Thomas Waterman, John Andrew, Henry Tibbits, John Briggs, and John Fones from Anashuecot and kin
- **Confirmed by RI Assembly in 1677** with provisos:
  - Cannot interfere with East Greenwich or earlier grants
  - One-third reserved for the colony

### Resolution
RI asserted jurisdiction, rejected the unauthorized Atherton title, and selectively regularized the Fones proprietors' possession through the 1677 confirmation. **RI's invalidation of the Atherton deed effectively reopened the territory for Anashuecot's grant.**

This gives political context to John Greene's 1672 purchase: he wasn't just buying from a sachem — he was buying into a territory where the previous colonial purchase had been repudiated. The Fones Purchase was, in effect, the legitimate replacement.

## Narragansett Authority Structure — Lisa Brooks Model

Lisa Brooks (*Our Beloved Kin*, Yale) provides the analytical framework: authority in Narragansett country was **relational and distributed**, not a centralized chain of command.

- A sachem could exercise substantial authority, but land and political jurisdiction were embedded in a **network of relatives, neighboring sachems, and community consent**
- Power transmitted through **maternal descent, marriage, sibling/nephew relationships, and control of particular places**
- The Atherton Company's strategy was to **isolate particular signatories** and convert relational authority into an English-style deed

Anashuecot's deed structure — naming **two brothers and three sons** as co-grantors, all described as "sole heires & proprietors" — **fits exactly this distributed model**. The authority isn't concentrated in one person; it's distributed across the kin group.
