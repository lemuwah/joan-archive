# The Road to Joan's Family — an honest plan

**Date:** 2026-09-09 — requested and read by the archive owner
**Purpose:** Not a theory. A road. What to run, in what order, and what each step can and cannot tell us about who Joan's people were.
**Ground rule:** Joan is not findable under her own name in the digitized English record — ~30 repositories searched by name, all NULL ([NEGATIVE_LOG.md](../primary_sources/NEGATIVE_LOG.md)). So the road below never asks "where is Joan Greene?" again. It asks where her **household, her sons' obligations, and the women beside her** were — because that is where a woman with no deeds actually lives in the paperwork.

---

## What the search has already proven (use it)

The name-search is done and it is a **finding, not a failure**: Joan exists in exactly one instrument (24 March 1681/82) and is invisible everywhere else her name should appear if she were an ordinary English wife of a landholding freeman — colony records (Bartlett), vital records (Arnold), the genealogies (Austin, Savage), MA Bay, Plymouth, CT, the Royal Commission, even the published ethnographies. That totality is the single most informative fact we have. **Any road that explains the family must explain that silence.**

## The road, in order

### Stage 1 — Finish the papers that talk about her directly (days, all in-hand or one human step)

| Step | What | What it can tell us |
|---|---|---|
| 1a | **James instrument page** (R2 — blocker found: index on Image 9 illegible; sweep from Image 13 or pull hi-res Image 9) | Whether Joan's clause differs between sons — different wording = different legal fact about her |
| 1b | **1685 Clark→Brinley deed** (R1 — custody known: Jamestown Town Clerk / State Archives microfilm) | C-2 death date AND possibly "his widow" = second appearance |
| 1c | **1686 Tibbitts purchase** (R4) and **1695/96 damaged deed** (R12) | Whether Joan's annuity or name survives John |
| 1d | **RIHS Mss 461 "will complaint"** (R35 — physical pull, HIGH) | A *post-1682* Joan reference — the only known candidate for her name after the deed |

**Why first:** these are the only four places a document might still say her name. Cheap, fast, decisive. If 1d says her name, the whole search changes.

### Stage 2 — Follow the annuity (the thread the family itself left)

The 1682 deed made Daniel and James pay **30 shillings yearly for Joan's life**. That obligation outlives John and creates paper: receipts, disputes, releases, estate settlements.

- **R20** NK/Kingstown probate 1690–1730, all sons — did any estate settle a debt "to my mother"?
- **R10/R11** Havens and Corey deeds — do the provenance chains recite the 1682 life-estate?
- **R6/R7** the two Vaughan deeds — they quote John's lost will; the will is where Joan's provision would be spelled out.
- **R17–R19** the Flounders court/estate cluster — if Sarah is Joan's daughter (firewall question), her 1668 case is Joan's *earliest* shadow, 14 years before the deed.

**Why second:** this is the family interacting after the signature — exactly what you asked for. Every one of these is a record the family made because they had obligations to each other.

### Stage 3 — The Smith household and the town book (the widest honest net)

John spent ~40 years at Cocumscussoc, Richard Smith's trading post. Households like that generated **account books, daybooks, and letters** — and account books name everyone who ate, worked, sewed, nursed, and traded, including women who never signed a deed.

- **R30** the original of Smith's 14 May 1664 letter (print-only now — the original may say more)
- **R36** identify the Kingstowne town clerks 1674–1700 (Ring 0 of the scribes list) — their personal papers are unmined
- **R32** Thomas Minor's Stonington diary, 1653–1684 — a border diarist who named travelers, servants, and wives by name
- **R27** Winthrop Jr.'s medical casebooks — patients are recorded by household ("wife of…"), the single best sideways net for an undocumented woman in this corridor
- **R37** William Harris's Pawtuxet letters — he documented his neighbors obsessively

**Why third:** this is where "the people who loved her" would show up without needing her to own land.

### Stage 4 — Origin-side (Model C is no longer optional)

The Y-DNA sits in the Irish Sea cluster (R-Z255). That doesn't make Joan Irish — it's John's patriline — but it means the family's road *into* New England may not run through an English parish.

- **R39** Irish/Ulster migration + Cromwellian transportation lists (already scoped NULL for a *named* Joan — rerun as household/associate search)
- **R38** Bristol/London servant registers — the 1654–1665 sweep was NULL for a named woman; the next pass looks for the *Greene* men traveling with servants
- **R41** port books — Boston, Newport, New Amsterdam, Bristol

### Stage 5 — The community that knew her first (by protocol, always)

If any model in the A/H family is true, Joan's family is recorded in **Narragansett knowledge, not English paper.**

- **R46** Native Northeast Portal full variant sweep — digitized, respectful, immediately runnable
- **R47** Tomaquag / community-governed sources — by request and protocol only; this is a relationship, and the archive approaches it that way

## The comparison that tells us what the silence means

One row in the matrix is quietly the most important: **are other Quidnessett/Cocumscussoc wives equally undocumented?** (Model B row, ⬜ NOT STARTED — now also R50.)

- If yes → Joan's invisibility is *structural*, and Model B (undocumented English wife) gets stronger.
- If no → her silence is *distinctive*, and the models that predict difference from English record-keeping (A, C, D, E, H) get stronger.

That single comparison study — a dozen neighbor wives, one afternoon of checking — calibrates every model at once. I'd run it alongside Stage 2.

## What we promise the road, and what we don't

- We will know **whether more paper exists** — and where — within Stages 1–2.
- We may learn her **family's outline** (the sons' households, the Smith house, the Flounders line) even if her own name never appears again.
- We do not promise her maiden name. If it survives anywhere, it survives in one of the five stages above. If it doesn't survive anywhere, the road will show that honestly too — and *that* answer, documented, is itself the finding.

*"Follow the rivers. See where they meet for real. We follow the breadcrumbs all the way to Joan."* — this is that, sequenced.
