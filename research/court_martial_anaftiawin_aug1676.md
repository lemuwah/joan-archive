## Court Composition

Convened under Rhode Island's charter authority to exercise martial law. Members included:
- Governor Walter Clarke
- Deputy Governor John Cranston
- Several Assistants
- The Attorney General
- Military officers including **"Lieut. John Green"**

⚠️ **"Lieut. John Green" on the panel is NOT identified as John Greene of Quidnessett.** No source gives the Quidnessett John any military title. His first documented civic role is the 1679 Conservator of the Peace. The surgeon's son (John Greene Jr. of Warwick) held ranks of Captain (1664) and later "Major for the Main" — but was not found specifically titled "Lieutenant" either. Thomas Gould held the Lieutenant title at the 1671 Acquidnessett court. **Which John is the panel's Lieutenant remains OPEN.**

## The Anaftiawin Entry (printed p. 181)

Exact text, long-s modernized:

> "Anaftiawin of Narragansett denyeth, that he layed Hands on John Green of Narragansett, occasioned about the Death of a dumb Boy, although affirmed by three Witnesses to his Face, to wit: Mr. Thomas Gould, John Andra and Daniel Green."
>
> "The Court adjourned till Thursday next at ten of the Clock in the Morning, being the 31st of this Instant."

### Persons Named

- **Anaftiawin of Narragansett** — Accused of laying hands on John Green in connection with the death of a "dumb Boy." Denied the accusation.
- **John Green of Narragansett** — Alleged victim. The Daniel Green witness anchor (1682 deed confirms Daniel as son of John of Quidnessett) supports identifying THIS John Green as the Quidnessett John. The other Johns don't have documented sons named Daniel.
- **Mr. Thomas Gould** — Lead witness. Settlement Lieutenant. See `people/thomas_gould.md`.
- **John Andra** — Second witness. Likely John Andrews, co-purchaser on the 1672 Fones Purchase.
- **Daniel Green** — Third witness. Son of John Greene of Quidnessett (confirmed by 1682 deed). Freeman at Acquidnessett 1671.

### The "Dumb Boy"

Referenced only here. No name, no other documentation. "Dumb" in 17th-century usage = unable to speak (mute), not intellectually disabled. Hough's index lists "Dumb Boy, 181" — only this one reference.

## The Verdict Gap

**No verdict is recorded for Anaftiawin.** The court adjourns to August 31. When it reconvenes, it moves directly to Quonaehewacout and then Mollasses/Manasses. Anaftiawin is never mentioned again.

Every other defendant received a clear outcome:
- **Quanapen** (Sowagonish) — "Voted. Guilty… shall be shott to death"
- **Quanapen's one-eyed brother** — "Voted, that at present Judgment is suspended" (disability defense)
- **Sunkeecunasuck** — "Voted guilty… to suffer Death"
- **Wenanaquabin** — "Voted guilty… to be putt to Death"
- **Wecopeak** — "Voted guilty… and to dye as the others"
- **Mollasses/Mamuxuat** — Delivered for transport (enslaved)
- **Anaftiawin** — ❓ **SILENCE**

### Secondary Scholarship on the Verdict Gap (2026-08-30)

⚠️ **No secondary scholarship was found that discusses the Anaftiawin verdict gap specifically.** Searches across Lepore (*The Name of War*), Drake (*King Philip's War*), Calloway (*After King Philip's War*), and Pulsipher (*Subjects unto the Same King*) did not identify passages addressing Anaftiawin by name or discussing why his outcome was omitted.

The broader account confirms that the court martial convicted and executed four Indians, but **does not establish that Anaftiawin was among those four.**

**The gap is DOCUMENTARY, not evidence of acquittal.** Any claim that Anaftiawin was convicted, executed, released, or sold requires evidence from another record.

### Possible Readings (not theory — structural observations)

1. The verdict was recorded elsewhere and lost
2. The adjournment WAS the resolution — charges dropped or deferred
3. The record is incomplete at this point in the manuscript
4. A deal was struck off-record

## Quonaehewacout (August 31 session)

Immediately after the Anaftiawin entry and adjournment:

> "Quonaehewacout saith, that he was informed that all the Sachims was at the takeing and burning of Ireh Bulls Garrison."

Jireh Bull's garrison burned December 15, 1675, at Pettaquamscutt. 17 men killed. No biographical identification of Quonaehewacout located. **Name remains unlinked to the Anashuecot/Anochetuckett cluster** despite terminal-element similarity (-wacout / -tuckett / -uecot).

## Phonetic Variants: Anaftiawin

This is the ONLY appearance of the form "Anaftiawin" in the primary record trail.

**Verified variants (with primary source):**
- Anashuecot — 1672 Fones Purchase deed ✅
- Anashusett — 1672 Fones Purchase deed ✅
- Awashueset — 1672 Fones Purchase deed ✅
- Awashous / Awashouse / Awashshous — 1661 deeds (Potter) ✅
- Awashowat — 1661 deeds (Potter) ✅
- Awashousse — 1644 Act of Submission (Gorton) ✅
- Anaftiawin — 1676 court martial (Hough 1858) ✅

**Downgraded / unreliable:**
- Awasawin — ⚠️ No reliable primary source found. Originally reported from genealogical index; likely secondary derivation. DOWNGRADED.

**Unverified / needs sourcing:**
- Anashewacot — probable transcriptional variant, not verified in Potter
- Awashuwett — may be later reading

⚠️ **"Anaftiawin" is NOT independently attested outside Hough's transcription.** It should be treated as Hough's rendering, potentially of a different individual or a scribal variant, not automatically equated with Anashuecot.

⚠️ **Potter does NOT establish that the Awashous/Awashouse forms from 1661 deeds refer to the same person as Anashuecot in 1672** — though they're in the same phonetic neighborhood.

## Connection to Broader Archive

- The "John Green of Narragansett" victim is likely our John (Daniel witness anchor)
- Thomas Gould = settlement Lieutenant, neighboring proprietor, fellow jurisdictional-conflict figure
- John Andra = likely Fones Purchase co-purchaser John Andrews
- The court martial occurs 4 years after the Fones Purchase and 2 years before Anashuecot is "conveyed out of ye country" (1678 Absolom affidavit)
