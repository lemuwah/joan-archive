# Checklist: Catching AI Hallucinations in Archival Research

*Built from real contamination events in the Joan Archive project, 2026.*

---

## Before You Trust Any AI-Sourced Claim

### 1. Does the citation actually exist?
- [ ] Look up the exact volume, page number, and title in a library catalog or Internet Archive
- [ ] Confirm the page range covers the right century — volume numbers get reused across series (RI Land Evidence "Vol. VII" = 1770s Gaspee records, not 1680s deeds)
- [ ] If the citation is a "vol. X pp. Y–Z" format with no repository or series name, treat it as unverified until you hold the page

### 2. Does the source actually say what the AI claims it says?
- [ ] Read the source verbatim — not a paraphrase, not "consistent with," not "appears to state"
- [ ] Compare exact wording against the AI's quotation marks. If the AI quotes "or to her mother if she survive" and the source says "as long as their father or mother should live," those are different claims
- [ ] Check whether the AI merged two separate sources into one composite quote

### 3. Is the source primary or is someone copying someone?
- [ ] Trace the citation chain backward: who cited it first?
- [ ] If three databases cite the same detail with the same phrasing, that's one source copied twice, not three independent confirmations
- [ ] If nobody before the 20th century cites it, ask where the 20th-century source got it

### 4. Is the AI conflating two real things into one phantom?
- [ ] Check whether shared elements (same location, same surname, same grantee) come from two different documents that got merged
- [ ] A real 1658 deed + a real 1682 deed ≠ a real 1682 deed with details from both
- [ ] If the claimed document shares 3+ elements with two known documents but isn't either of them, suspect conflation

---

## While the AI Is Generating Analysis

### 5. Is it filling silence with narrative?
- [ ] "Likely," "probably," "would have," "must have" = the AI is guessing
- [ ] "Survived," "escaped," "was hidden" = the AI is choosing an interpretation. The source may only say "conveyed out of ye country" — what that means is unknown
- [ ] If the AI says "nothing further is known" about a person, it's quoting a source. If it then tells you what happened to that person, it's fabricating

### 6. Is it treating a hypothesis as a stepping stone?
- [ ] Watch for chains: "If A then B, and if B then C, therefore C." Each link needs independent proof
- [ ] "Tacommanan = Tocomminon" is probable. "Therefore Anashuecot governed Coheassuck" is inference on inference
- [ ] Mark every unproven link. If the chain has two unproven links, the conclusion is speculation

### 7. Is it smoothing contradictions instead of holding them?
- [ ] Two sources that disagree should stay disagreeing until one is independently verified
- [ ] If the AI says "these can be reconciled by..." it's narrating, not proving
- [ ] A plus-mark and a bow-and-arrow are a contradiction, not a "variant tradition"

---

## After the AI Delivers Results

### 8. Run the negative-space test.
- [ ] What does the AI NOT mention? Omissions can be as misleading as fabrications
- [ ] If a comprehensive catalog (like Chapin 1931) doesn't list a person, that absence is evidence — not a gap to paper over
- [ ] "Not found" is a finding. Document it

### 9. Would this claim survive a hostile reader?
- [ ] Imagine someone who wants to disprove your thesis reading this claim. Can they trace it to a page they can see with their own eyes?
- [ ] If the answer is "no, but it's consistent with..." — it's not proven
- [ ] If the answer is "the AI said so" — it's not evidence

### 10. Tag everything before it touches your repository.
- [ ] 🟢 PROVEN — you or a verified source read the primary document
- [ ] 🟡 PROBABLE — secondary source, not yet independently confirmed
- [ ] ⚪ UNVERIFIED — claimed but no located primary source
- [ ] 🔴 CONTAMINATION — traced to AI fabrication, circular citation, or conflation
- [ ] Never let an ⚪ quietly become a 🟢 by repetition

---

## The Core Rule

**If you can't show someone the page, you don't have the source.**

AI is a research accelerator, not a research authority. It finds leads. You verify them. The moment you skip verification because a claim "sounds right" or "fits the pattern," you've built on sand — and walking it back costs more than checking it would have.
