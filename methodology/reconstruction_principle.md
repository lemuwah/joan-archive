> This archive documents lives lived between approximately 1600–1750 in Narragansett country (present-day Rhode Island). All records from this period were created within colonial legal systems and reflect their biases, categories, and blind spots. Every name found here — English, Narragansett, Niantic, mixed-heritage, unknown — represents a real person whose full story these records were not designed to capture. Content may include references to war, captivity, enslavement, displacement, legal coercion, and loss. These are documented realities of the period, not abstractions. All cultural and genealogical content is included for research purposes in the quest to identify Joan Unknown Greene and will be revised or removed if requested by descendant communities or tribal representatives.

# The Reconstruction Principle

**Status:** Active methodology component — completes the cycle the Seven Laws begin.

---

## The Problem the Laws Don't Solve

The Seven Laws are a contamination filter. They strip unverified claims, kill planted narratives, quarantine compromised sources, and force every assertion back to primary documents. When applied rigorously, they produce one of two results:

1. **A clean evidence base** — what the primary sources actually say, stripped of inference
2. **A null result** — the evidence doesn't support any conclusion

Both are correct outcomes. But neither is an identification.

The laws tell you what you CAN'T say. They don't tell you what you CAN say. The gap between "we killed all the bad theories" and "here's what the evidence actually supports" is where the Reconstruction Principle operates.

## The Principle

> After the Laws have stripped a corpus to verified primary-source facts, the remaining evidence is subjected to network analysis. Identity is reconstructed from convergent independent edges (relationships, locations, documents, chronology), not from names or narratives.
>
> A reconstruction is accepted only when:
> (a) multiple independent edges converge on the same identity,
> (b) no contradictory evidence exists that the Identity Firewall would flag, and
> (c) the method has been validated on a control case with a known outcome.

## The Five Steps

1. **Strip** (Laws 1–7) → remove all contaminated claims from the corpus
2. **Census** → count what the primary sources actually contain — names, dates, roles, relationships, land, witnesses
3. **Network reconstruction** → build identity signatures from convergent independent edges, not from names or narratives
4. **Falsifiable prediction** → state what SHOULD exist if the hypothesis is true, then look for it
5. **Control case** → test the method on a known identity before trusting it on an unknown

Steps 1 and 2 are mechanical. Steps 3–5 are where the method either produces a result or honestly reports that it can't.

## How It Differs from Traditional Genealogy

Traditional genealogy asks: "Who was this person?" and searches for an answer.

The Reconstruction Principle asks: "What identity signature do the verified primary sources produce?" and measures whether that signature is unique.

- A **name** is not an identity — it's a label shared by many people.
- A **network** (name + husband + land + witnesses + location + chronology) can be an identity — if the edges converge independently and no contradictions exist.
- The **Identity Firewall** prevents premature merges: two people until evidence says otherwise.
- The **spelling split protocol** prevents false normalization: Ann ≠ Anne until evidence says they're the same woman.

## Where It's Being Tested

- **12-Name Experiment** (`methodology/12-name-experiment/`) — testing the reconstruction engine on 12 common female given names across 445 colonial land documents. Can relational density recover identities that names alone cannot?
- **Esther Smith control** (`methodology/12-name-experiment/controls/esther-smith.md`) — known identity used as positive control.
- **Gorton daughters control** (`methodology/12-name-experiment/controls/gorton-daughters.md`) — six known identities showing maximum achievable relational density.
- **Ripper stress test** (`methodology/stress-tests/ripper-case.md`) — negative control proving the laws correctly return null when evidence doesn't support a conclusion.

## What Constitutes Success

The Reconstruction Principle succeeds when:

1. The control case (Esther Smith) is correctly reconstructed from corpus data alone
2. Known identities (Gorton daughters) produce the expected high-density signatures
3. Unknown identities (Joan Greene) either produce a unique convergent signature OR honestly report that they don't
4. The negative control (Ripper) correctly returns null

A correct null is as valuable as a correct identification. The methodology is not designed to always find someone — it's designed to tell the truth about what the evidence supports.
