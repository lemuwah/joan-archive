# Stress Test: Jack the Ripper Suspects

**Tool used:** DeepSeek
**Date:** 2026-09-11
**Purpose:** Test whether the Joan Archive's Multi-Agent Laws function as a universal contamination filter, not just a colonial genealogy tool.
**Result:** ✅ The laws produce a correct null — every suspect collapses. The methodology is validated as domain-independent.

## What This Test Proved

The Seven Laws were applied to every major Jack the Ripper suspect. Every one collapsed:

- **Aaron Kosminski** — Police Hunch (Macnaghten memo 1894, Swanson marginalia 1980). DNA claim quarantined: shawl has no chain of custody, analysis not peer-reviewed. **Status: Named, unverified.**
- **Montague John Druitt** — Police Hunch (Macnaghten memo). Family rumor recorded by police official years after the fact. No document places him at any scene. **Status: Named, unverified.**
- **Walter Sickert** — Secondary Narrative (Patricia Cornwell books 2002/2017). Built on Stephen Knight's 1976 conspiracy theory, which was sourced from Joseph Gorman — who publicly retracted and admitted fabrication. **Status: Quarantined.**
- **Carl Feigenbaum** — Hearsay. Lawyer's press statement after client's execution; no signed confession, no corroborating document. **Status: Unprocessed.**
- **H.H. Holmes** — Secondary Narrative (descendant's books). No travel records, ship manifests, or contemporary documents place him in England in 1888. **Status: Unprocessed.**
- **Royal Conspiracy (Prince Albert Victor, Sir William Gull, John Netley)** — Contaminated. Sole primary source (Joseph Gorman/Sickert) publicly retracted the story as fabrication. **Status: Quarantined.**
- **John Pizer, Seweryn Kłosowski, David Cohen, Francis Tumblety** — Police Hunches or Press Narratives. None carry a Verified tag. None have a primary document placing them at any murder scene. **Status: Named, unverified / Unprocessed.**

### The Law 6 Inversion

DeepSeek independently applied Law 6 (No Centering) and reached the same conclusion the Joan methodology demands: the proper object of study is the victims, not the phantom. The five canonical victims — Mary Ann Nichols, Annie Chapman, Elizabeth Stride, Catherine Eddowes, Mary Jane Kelly — were real women with documented lives. The suspect industry exists only because the narrative was centered on a killer. Law 6 forbids that centering.

### The Law 1 Kill Shot

DeepSeek applied Law 1 to the *case itself* before examining suspects:

> The primary documents do not say "Jack the Ripper." They say five women were murdered in Whitechapel in 1888. The inquests returned verdicts of "willful murder by person or persons unknown." The name comes from the "Dear Boss" letter, which the police suspected was a journalist's hoax.

Therefore under Law 1, three foundational claims are immediately unprocessed:
- That a single individual committed all five murders
- That the murders were the work of a "serial killer" in the modern sense
- That any named suspect had anything to do with the deaths

Every suspect theory is built on the smoothed narrative that "Jack the Ripper" existed as a coherent identity. Law 1 quarantines that narrative at the door.

## What This Test Exposed

The laws are a **contamination filter**. They strip. They dismantle narrative. They correctly return null when the evidence doesn't support a conclusion.

But they don't reconstruct.

The Ripper analysis proved the stripping works universally — and stopped. That's the correct result for the laws alone. But the Joan Archive methodology doesn't stop at null. It has a reconstruction engine on top:

1. **Strip** (Laws 1–7) → remove all contaminated claims
2. **Census** (12-name experiment) → count what primary sources actually contain
3. **Network reconstruction** (relational density) → build identity signatures from edges, not names
4. **Falsifiable prediction** → state what SHOULD exist if the hypothesis is true
5. **Control case** → test the method on a known identity before trusting it on an unknown

The Ripper analysis executed step 1 perfectly. It never got to steps 2–5.

The gap between "the laws stripped everything" and "the methodology found someone" is the **Reconstruction Principle** — documented in `methodology/reconstruction_principle.md`.

## What Steps 2–5 Would Look Like for the Ripper Case

(Not to solve it — to test whether the method could produce a result if applied.)

**Step 2 — Census:** What do the primary documents actually contain? Inquest transcripts, contemporary police reports (not memoirs), mortuary records, witness statements taken under oath. How many distinct data points exist?

**Step 3 — Network reconstruction:** The victims have documented relational networks — addresses, lodging houses, pubs, workhouses, common acquaintances. Do those networks overlap? Do they converge on a location, a time pattern, a shared contact?

**Step 4 — Falsifiable prediction:** If a single killer exists, the victim networks should share at least one convergence point not explained by general Whitechapel geography. If they don't converge beyond random proximity, the single-killer hypothesis weakens.

**Step 5 — Control:** Take a known solved Victorian murder from the same period and neighborhood. Run the same method. Does it reconstruct the known killer's connection? If yes, the method has resolving power. If no, it doesn't — and that's also data.
