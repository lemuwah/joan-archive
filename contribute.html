What we haven't searched yet — and how you can help.

The Joan Archive is an open-access research project. The most valuable contributions are not interpretations — they are searches. Every repository checked, every null result logged, narrows the field for everyone.

How Corrections Are Weighted

Not all corrections carry the same weight. When the archive receives feedback, it is prioritized in this order:

  1. 🔴 Tribal / Indigenous scholarly correction — highest priority, immediate action. Narragansett or other Indigenous authority correcting descriptions of their governance, cultural practices, kinship, or history. These corrections restructure the archive, not just annotate it.
  2. 🟠 Primary source contradiction — a documented fact from a primary source that contradicts a claim in the archive.
  3. 🟡 Methodology error — an inference presented as fact, a citation error, a missing status tag, a Law violation.
  4. 🟢 General correction — typos, broken links, formatting, factual clarifications from secondary sources.

This is not hierarchy for hierarchy's sake. It reflects the archive's epistemological stance: Indigenous authority on Indigenous history takes priority because colonial records are partial instruments (see About). A correction from Tomaquag changes the structure. A typo fix changes a letter.

Who Might Have Known Joan?

Joan may not appear under her own name again. Search the people who shared her household, land, legal transactions, and local record network before, during, and after 1682:

  • Household and family: John Greene of Quidnessett, Daniel Greene, James Greene, Edward Greene, Benjamin Greene, Henry Tibbitts, and Mary Tibbitts.
  • Land and recording network: John Fones, George Wightman, William Carpenter, George Havens, Arthur Aylworth, John Nutsn, Richard Smith, and neighboring Quidnessett landholders.
  • Earlier regional network: Anashuecot, Wampkegge, Ompamiatt, Awawsunks, Cocumscussoc residents, and witnesses in the Fones Purchase and related Narragansett deeds.
  • Later record trail: James-to-Wightman and Benjamin-related deeds, probate or "will complaint" material, widow or annuity references, church records, and records naming the Greene children or their spouses.

Search name variants: Joan, Joane, Jane, Jone, Greene, Green, Green(e), Quidnessett, Quidnisset, Aquidnesett, Kingstown, Narragansett. Search the record, not just the name: a deed may name only a husband, witness, boundary neighbor, heir, widow, or beneficiary.

How to Submit a Document

  1. Keep the original file or an unchanged screenshot. Do not crop away the viewer title, collection, image number, or source URL.
  2. Record repository, collection, film/DGS number, image/page number, date accessed, and whether the image was public or login-restricted.
  3. Give a literal reading with uncertain letters marked `[?]`; do not silently expand abbreviations or modernize names.
  4. Separate what your eyes can identify from what an AI, index, or genealogy tree suggests.
  5. Send both positive findings and scoped negative searches. We preserve corrections and failed leads.

A Note for Indigenous Contributors

If you are from a Narragansett or other Indigenous research background, your input carries distinct and higher weight — not folded into general feedback. The archive recognizes that colonial records are partial instruments (see Epistemology.md) and that descriptions of Narragansett governance on this site are derived from non-Indigenous academic sources.

Contact has been initiated with the Tomaquag Museum. As of September 2026, no formal review has occurred.

Private channel: with "Joan Archive — Indigenous Review" in the subject line. Your correspondence will not be shared publicly without your permission.
