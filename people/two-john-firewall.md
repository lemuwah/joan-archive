# The Two-John Firewall

## Surgeon John Greene vs. John Greene of Quidnessett

> **This document is a hostile review. Every claim below is tagged by source quality. Unproven tradition is labeled as such. No narrative smoothing has been applied. If you find an unsupported assertion, challenge it.**

---

## Executive Summary

There are two John Greenes in early Rhode Island history who have been conflated by family tradition, secondary genealogies, and algorithmic family trees. This document establishes that they are **two different men** by three independent, non-circumstantial lines of evidence:

1. **Chronological impossibility:** Surgeon John was dead by 7 January 1658/59. Quidnessett John was alive in 1685.
2. **Geographic separation:** Surgeon John settled in Providence (1638) and Warwick (1643). Quidnessett John settled in Quidnessett, North Kingstown, near Richard Smith's trading post.
3. **Y-DNA divergence:** Surgeon John's confirmed descendants carry haplogroup **I-M223**. Quidnessett John's confirmed descendants carry haplogroup **R-BY186325**. These are on entirely different branches of the human Y-chromosome tree.

Everything beyond these three facts—including the popular "cousin" narrative that places both men under the Bowridge Hill tree—is **unproven tradition** awaiting primary source documentation.

## What the Evidence Proves vs. What It Does Not

### Proven (Primary-Source Level)

- **They are two different men.** Surgeon John was dead by January 1658/59. Quidnessett John was alive in 1685 and possibly to 1695. One man cannot be dead and alive simultaneously. This is a hard firewall.
- **They lived in different places.** Warwick vs. Quidnessett are geographically distinct settlements with no record of either man residing in the other's town.
- **They had different wives.** Joanne Tattershall (Surgeon) and Joan Beggarly (Quidnessett) are not the same person. The La Mance conflation that mashed multiple Joans into one figure has already been disproven.
- **They carry different Y-DNA haplogroups.** I-M223 and R-BY186325 are on completely different branches of the human Y-tree. This is not drift-within-lineage; it indicates no recent common paternal ancestor.

### Not Proven (Tradition or Conjecture)

- **The "cousin" relationship.** The Bowridge Hill pedigree that makes Quidnessett John a descendant of John Greene (1529–1560) is *inherited family tradition*, not confirmed record. George Sears Greene (1903) placed the Quidnessett line in a *separate section* and wrote that "a careful research is still necessary to prove the exact connection."
- **Quidnessett John's 1695 death date.** No will survives. Austin's c. 1695–1696 is citation-less. The 1685 deed is the last confirmed sighting.
- **Quidnessett John's English origin.** Every statement that he "came from Gillingham" or "came on the *Matthew*" rests on tradition, not embarkation records.

## The Gibbs Audit: Hostile Source Review

Alfred Gibbs (alfredgibbs.com) is a modern secondary compiler whose work is widely cited by algorithmic trees. He is **more careful than La Mance** in that he includes sourcing notes, but he commits the same fundamental sin: he builds a detailed narrative scaffold that makes unproven tradition *look* like established fact, then hides the disclaimers where readers skip them.

### Claim 1: "John Greene of Quidnessett was born in 1606"

**Gibbs presentation:** Timeline fact.  
**Actual primary source:** None.  
**What Huling (1884) said:** "nothing is certainly known" of his previous history.  
**What G.S. Greene (1903) said:** No birth date given.  
**Verdict:** **Unsourced.** The 1606 date is family tradition presented as data.

### Claim 2: "John Greene immigrated in 1635 aboard the ship *Matthew*"

**Gibbs source:** Hotten's *Original Lists of Persons of Quality* (1874), p. 81: "Jo: Greene" age 29 to St. Christopher's in the *Matthew* of London, 21 May 1635.  
**What Gibbs omits:** The passenger was bound for **St. Christopher's (West Indies)**, not New England. There are at least four other John Greenes in Hotten's 1634–1635 lists, including one age 25 to Barbados and one age 22 to Barbados.  
**Gibbs' own admission:** "The connection is circumstantial."  
**Verdict:** **Circumstantial at best.** Gibbs builds his entire immigration narrative around a West Indies passenger. This is La Mance behavior—finding a convenient record and forcing a fit.

### Claim 3: "First cousin twice removed of John Greene the Surgeon"

**Gibbs source trail:** Built from the Bowridge Hill pedigree: Robert Greene → John Greene (1529–1560) → Henry Greene → Robert Greene → John (1606).  
**Primary source check:** There is **no primary source** linking Quidnessett John to Robert Greene of Bowridge Hill, to John Greene (1529–1560), or to Henry Greene.  
**What Huling said:** "The whole matter, however, is one of tradition and conjecture rather than of fact."  
**What G.S. Greene (1903) said:** The book *does not contain* the John Greene (1529–1560) figure. It documents the Surgeon's line separately and explicitly separates the Quidnessett line.  
**Verdict:** **Fabricated relationship.** Gibbs calculates a precise cousin degree from a pedigree that his own sources admit is unproven. He has the DNA evidence (different haplogroups) and still publishes the cousin claim.

### Claim 4: "Died in 1695 at age 89, buried in the Old Field Lot"

**Gibbs presentation:** Timeline fact.  
**Primary source check:** No will survives. Austin (1887) gives c. 1695–1696 without citation. The 1685 deed is the last confirmed record.  
**Verdict:** **Unverified tradition presented as fact.** Gibbs buries the uncertainty in a sourcing note while putting "1695" in his timeline headline.

### Claim 5: "Settled in Quidnessett by approximately 1639"

**Gibbs source:** 1679 testimony that he lived with Richard Smith "forty years and more" when Smith established his trading post.  
**Verdict:** **Legitimate.** This is one of the few claims that rests on actual primary testimony.

### Claim 6: "Joan Beggarly married John Greene by December 14, 1638"

**Gibbs source:** Winthrop's journal.  
**Primary source check:** Winthrop wrote: "one Greene (who hath married the wife of one Beggerly, whose husband is living, and no divorce, etc."  
**Verdict:** **Legitimate, but Gibbs adds precision Winthrop doesn't have.** Winthrop says "one Greene"—he does not specify "John Greene of Quidnessett." The identification is circumstantial. Gibbs presents it as certain.

### Claim 7: The Medieval Boughton Pedigree

**Gibbs source:** Admits this comes from an unnamed "compiled family history" and acknowledges gaps ("names are lost," "unknown son").  
**Primary source check:** Zero primary sources. This is legendary genealogy.  
**Verdict:** **Family mythology.** Gibbs labels it "Layer 1: family tradition" vs. "Layer 2: proof," but it is still on his site, still indexed by search engines, and still contaminates the narrative. The archive's 5th law (no algorithmic contamination) is violated the moment Google indexes it as fact.

## Where Gibbs Repeats La Mance Errors

| La Mance Tactic | Gibbs Equivalent |
|-----------------|------------------|
| Presents tradition as fact | Timeline gives 1606 birth and 1695 death as fixed dates |
| Forces a ship passenger to fit | "Greene Jo" on the *Matthew* = Quidnessett John |
| Calculates precise relationships from unproven pedigrees | "First cousins twice removed" |
| Ignores contradictory evidence | Publishes cousin claim despite DNA haplogroup mismatch |
| Hides disclaimers in footnotes | Sourcing notes exist but are separated from the narrative |

**Why Gibbs is more dangerous than La Mance:** Gibbs is actually more honest than La Mance in one respect—he includes a sourcing note that says the Bowridge Hill pedigree is unproven. But the **narrative structure overrides the disclaimer.** A reader scrolling through his site sees a detailed timeline, a family tree with names and relationships, and a "Relationship to John Greene the Surgeon" section with a precise cousin calculation. The sourcing note is at the bottom. Most readers never see it. The visual authority of the timeline and tree communicates "this is established" even when the text says "this is tradition." This is exactly how La Mance's errors propagated—by looking authoritative.

## Conclusions

1. **The two-John firewall is solid** on three independent axes: chronological impossibility, geographic separation, and Y-DNA divergence.
2. **Gibbs is La Mance with better PR.** He knows the pedigree is unproven, he says so in a footnote, and then he builds his entire site around it anyway. The "first cousins twice removed" calculation is the most egregious example—he is doing math on vapor.
3. **The DNA evidence makes the cousin claim not merely unproven but biologically impossible** in the direct male line.
4. **Treat Gibbs' site as a secondary compilation of family tradition, not a primary source.** Trust only the claims he makes that cite actual colony records, court minutes, or Winthrop's journal. Everything else—birth, death, ship, English parents, relationship to the Surgeon, medieval pedigree—is tradition wearing a footnote.
