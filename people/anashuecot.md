## What We Know

### The 1672 Fones Purchase (primary)

📔 PRIMARY — Fones Record p. 90, confirmed by RI State Archives; heading in Potter 1835 p. 76.

On 1 January 1671/72, Anashuecot — describing himself as *"chiefe Sachem and heire properly of ye land called quckeset"* — conveyed the Devil's Foot / Fones Purchase tract at Quidnessett to **John Greene, John Fones, Henry Tibbits, John Andrews, John Briggs, and Thomas Waterman**, *"Together with ye volentary & free consent of Wampkegge and Ompamiatt my two Brothers, and Seecomp, Tyecuecsha & Nammeash my three sons."* All six are described in the deed as "sole heires & proprietors" of Quidnessett. 📖 EXTERNAL INTERPRETATION — the deed is an English legal instrument; what the transaction meant within Narragansett land practice is not something this document can tell us.

### Earlier appearances under the Awashouse/Awashowat variant cluster

- **15 Jun 1661:** "Awashouse" signs a declaration affirming Socho's land rights (Potter appendix p. 245). 📚 SECONDARY (Potter printing the original)
- **5 Oct 1661:** **"Awashouse and Nucom"**, described as owners, deed **Nisquitianxsett** to William Vaughan and the Misquamicuck purchasers (Potter appendix p. 267). He appears as a principal grantor, not a witness. 📚 SECONDARY
- **1660–1661 witness marks:** Awashwash, Awashous, Awashshass, Awashshous appear as witnesses on Sosoa's deed, Cachaquant's testimony, Pessicus's confirmation, and Wawaloam's affirmation (Potter appendix pp. 243–249). 📚 SECONDARY
- **1654 (OPEN):** "Awassuocitt" witnesses Tacommonan's Petawhomett deed, with sons named **William Baker** ("his eldest son") and **Wawnunockshaw** — an English-named eldest son of a sachem-line man in 1654 would be extraordinary; the identification with Anashuecot is UNPROVEN and the son names do not match the 1672 sons. Held open, not merged. 📚 SECONDARY (RI Land Evidences abstracts)
