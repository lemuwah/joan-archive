## Disclaimer

All records from this period were created within colonial legal systems and reflect their biases, categories, and blind spots. Every name found here — English, Narragansett, Niantic, mixed-heritage, unknown — represents a real person whose full story these records were not designed to capture. ### Freeman Record

Took the Rhode Island engagement/oath of allegiance at the court held at Acquidnessett/Wickford, 19–20 May 1671, together with his son Daniel. 📚 SECONDARY — *Narragansett Historical Register* Vol. 2 (1883); *Records of the Colony of RI*

This is a distinct event from the admission of John Greene Jr./II as freeman of Narragansett on 6 May 1673.

### Offices and Status

### Documented Sequence of Jurisdictional Conflict, 1663–1679

Presented as a record sequence. No motive is assigned to any party.

1. **Pre-1663** — Greene acquired Quidnessett land via Indian purchase, contrary to a Rhode Island General Court order. Title valid under Connecticut's claim, threatened under Rhode Island's. 📚 SECONDARY
2. **1663** — Greene, Richard Smith, and others declared for Connecticut. 📚 SECONDARY
3. **5 May 1664** — Rhode Island General Court issued a warrant for "John Greene Sen'r living at Narragansett." 📚 SECONDARY
4. **Early May 1664** — Removed from his home. Richard Smith's letter (Wickford, 14 May 1664, to Capt. Hutchinson) states Rhode Island men came "with a warrant under the governor's hand" and "forceably fetched him away." 📚 SECONDARY — *Narragansett Historical Register* Vols. 1–2 Part 2. **Original manuscript location unidentified** (possibly MHS Winthrop Papers — OPEN).
5. **May 1664** — Before the court, gave answers that offended ("the manner in which he had answered the Court"). Petitioned pardon. Court "passed by his offence," promised "all lawful protexion," retained him as freeman. 📚 SECONDARY
6. **1664–1671** — Seven years of jurisdictional limbo.
7. **19–20 May 1671** — Special court at Acquidnessett. Inhabitants assured peaceful possession conditional on acknowledging Rhode Island jurisdiction. Thomas Gould chosen Lieutenant and Conservator at this court. 📚 SECONDARY
8. **20 May 1671** — John Greene and son Daniel take the oath of allegiance, after written assurance regarding their land. 📚 SECONDARY
9. **1 January 1671/72** — Fones Purchase transacted directly with Narragansett sachem Anashuecot. 📔 PRIMARY
10. **1679** — Sworn as Conservator of the Peace. Affidavit supports Richard Smith Jr.'s title. 📔 PRIMARY

**Sources:** Potter, *Early History of Narragansett*; *Narragansett Historical Register*; *Records of the Colony of RI*; Richard Smith 1664 letter (in print).

### Land Records

**The 1666 Quidnessett plat.** ⚠️ CORRECTION (2026-08-29): this is not a deed. It is an ownership label on a proprietors' division plat of the northern part of Quidnessett Neck.

> "In 1666 the proprietors of the northern part of Quidnessetneck made a division of their lands, previously, so far as appears, unsurveyed. On a platf which purports to indicate the boundaries of each piece of property in that region in that year, a tract of one hundred and fifty-one acres is assigned to 'John Greene and Son.'"

📚 SECONDARY — "Notes on Quidnessett, No. 2," *Narragansett Historical Register*, p. 61

- 151 acres, bounded north/east/south by highways
- The son is **not named** in the surviving record
- **Which John is unproven.** The plat contains no disambiguating identifier. The earlier claim "Son = John Jr., age ~15" was unsupported and has been removed.

**The 1672 Fones Purchase.** On 1 January 1671/72, John Greene joined John Fones, Henry Tibbits, John Andrews, John Briggs, and Thomas Waterman in purchasing from the Narragansett sachem **Anashuecot** (also Awashowat, Awashouse — see `notes/2026-08-28_anashuecot_phonetic_variants.md`) a tract at Quidnessett known as the Devil's Foot or Fones Purchase. 📔 PRIMARY — Fones Record p. 90, RI State Archives

**The 1682 home-place partition deed.** Dated 24 March 1681/82. 📔 PRIMARY — NK Land Evidence; 📚 SECONDARY — F.L. Greene 1894 p. 10

- Daniel Greene: ~120 acres bordering Allen's Harbor
- James Greene: ~60 acres adjoining Daniel's land
- Each son to pay 30 shillings annually during the lifetime of John or Joan
- Land bounded in part by land of their brother John
- James described as the "natural son of John and Joan Greene" (17th-century usage = biological son)
- Life-estate clause: "as long as their father or mother should live" (F.L. Greene 1894 paraphrase) / "after his decease to Joane Greene his wife" (Worth 1921 abstract). The wording "or to her mother if she survive" is ⚠️ UNVERIFIED and appears in neither source — see `contradictions/mother-clause-wording.md`

**East Greenwich proprietorship, 1679.** "John Greene, Sr." drew the ninth house lot in the first ten-acre division and a ninety-acre farm in the second division among the original East Greenwich proprietors. 📚 SECONDARY — Bates 1918–19

**1679 affidavit.** John Greene's own affidavit stating he had lived at Cocumscussoc "forty years and more." 📔 PRIMARY

**~~1682 Pawtuxet consent~~ — SUSPENDED (2026-09-01).** Secondary sources claim a 19 May 1682 instrument in which Joan gives "free & voluntary consent" to sale of Pawtuxet land rights to William Carpenter for £10 and signs "her marke," witnessed by John Wickes and Samuel Gorton. The cited source — *Records of RI & Providence Plantations* Vol. VII pp. 177–178 — is **invalid**: that volume covers 1770–1776 and those pages contain 1773 Gaspee commission records. Verbatim text not located in any digitized primary source. Bates (1918–19) does not mention it. **Not counted as a verified appearance of Joan.** See `theory/proof_pieces/09_pawtuxet_problem.md` and `CORRECTIONS.md`.

## What We Don't Know

These are research directions, not failures.

1. **Birth date, birthplace, and parentage.** Entirely unknown. The traditional 1635 arrival aboard the *Matthew* is tradition, not record; per `people/two-john-firewall.md`, the *Matthew* passenger in Hotten was bound for St. Christopher's, and at least four other John Greenes appear in the 1634–35 lists.
2. **Immigration route and date.** Unknown. The 1679 affidavit's "forty years and more" is the only documentary anchor and it points to c. 1639–1642 residence, not arrival in the colonies.
3. **Exact death date.** After 24 March 1681/82. A deed of 25 Sept 1685 refers to "John Greene Sr. now deceased" (Bates 1918–19); if confirmed against the primary source this narrows death to between March 1682 and Sept 1685. The traditional "c. 1695" (Austin) is citation-less. This is contradiction **C-2** — see `contradictions/c2_death_date_deep_dive.md`.
4. **Location of his will.** Reported lost in the North Kingstown fire (Bates / F.L. Greene). Whether a recorded abstract or contemporaneous reference survives elsewhere has not been exhausted.
5. **Marriage date and place.** "Probably c. 1642" is inference from children's estimated births. No marriage record located.
6. **Joan's identity.** The archive's central question. Eight models A–H; G eliminated. "Joan Beggarly" is La Mance (1904) only and is excluded per Law 2 — Alice Beggarly's death before 1682 (Benedict Arnold letter + Winthrop Papers) makes that identification timeline-impossible. See `research/legal_danger_framework.md`.
7. **Whether he is the same man as John Greene of Newport.** Bates says yes. Held open per Law 1. See `people/john_greene_newport.md`.
8. **Which John is "John Greene and Son"** on the 1666 Quidnessett plat. Two theories held open: (a) this John, supported by location and Potter's inhabitants list; (b) a different John, since the plat says neither "of Quidnessett" nor "Senior."
9. **Whether the compiled children list is accurate** against the original *Records of the Colony of RI*.
