## What We Know

### Identity

- **Designation:** Mr. Thomas Gould of Quidnessett / Narragansett Country
- **Born:** Unknown
- **Died:** Unknown
- **Wife:** Unknown
- **Children:** Unknown

### Land Holdings

- **1657:** Purchased **Gould's Island** (Aquopimokuk) from sachem Koskotap
- **1661:** Made the **first purchase in Quidnessett** — "Thomas Gould made the first purchase in 1661, as can be shown by a plat now extant." Source: *Narragansett Historical Register*
- **~325 acres** at Calf Pasture Point (North Kingstown)
- **Neighboring proprietor** to John Greene's 151-acre tract on the 1666 Quidnessett plat — NOT a co-purchaser on that tract. Later Fones Record boundary descriptions mention "upon ye lands of Mr. Thomas Gould" near Greene's land, confirming adjacent but separate holdings.

### Offices / Status

- **Lieutenant** — chosen at the 19–20 May 1671 Acquidnessett court (alongside Richard Smith and Samuel Dyre as Conservators)
- **Conservator** — also chosen at the same 1671 court
- Listed among "persons engaged at Acquedneset" by Potter: "Mr. Thomas Gould, ... John Greene ... Daniel Greene ..."

**Source:** Potter, *Early History of Narragansett*; *Narragansett Historical Register*

### Jurisdictional Conflict

- Like John Greene, Gould's land titles depended on Indian purchases
- Both Gould and Greene declared for Connecticut pre-1671 to protect those titles
- At the **May 1671 Acquidnessett court**, both submitted to Rhode Island — Gould received Lieutenant/Conservator title, Greene received land protection
- **Later carried as a prisoner to Hartford by Connecticut** for adhering to Rhode Island's jurisdiction — Connecticut retaliated against him for the flip

**Source:** Potter; *Narragansett Historical Register*

### Connection to the Anaftiawin Court Martial (August 1676)

Gould appears as **"Mr. Thomas Gould"** — first-named witness against Anaftiawin at the Newport court martial:

> "Anaftiawin of Narragansett denyeth, that he layed Hands on John Green of Narragansett, occasioned about the Death of a dumb Boy, although affirmed by three Witnesses to his Face, to wit: Mr. Thomas Gould, John Andra and Daniel Green."

The "Mr." honorific and his settlement Lieutenant title confirm his status. He also had a **garrison** — referenced elsewhere in Hough (p. 185): "he was at Thomas Gould's Garrison."

**Source:** Hough 1858 (printed p. 181 / PDF p. 219)

### Relationship to John Greene of Quidnessett

- Fellow Narragansett country landholder (both pre-1666)
- Neighboring proprietors on the Quidnessett plat
- Both caught in the RI-Connecticut jurisdictional tug of war
- Both submitted to RI at the same 1671 court
- Both deeply connected to the Anaftiawin case: Gould as lead witness, Daniel Greene (John's son) as third witness
