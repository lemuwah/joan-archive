# Project Methodology Statement
**Date:** 2026-08-27  
**Author:** Lem (with documentation assistance from Migoo)

## What This Project Is

The Joan Archive is an independent research project conducted by a single researcher without institutional backing, grant funding, or paid research services.

This is deliberate.

## Why It's Done This Way

The project documents not just Joan Greene's historical record, but **the experience of doing primary-source genealogical research as a low-income, high-interest independent researcher.** The hurdles encountered — institutional access barriers, fragile-document policies, restricted digitization, paywalled databases, staffing limitations at archives — are themselves part of the record.

These barriers are real and they disproportionately affect researchers who:
- Don't have university library access
- Can't afford NEHGS memberships or paid research services
- Can't travel to Providence, RI or Salt Lake City for in-person archive visits
- Don't have institutional letterhead to lend weight to scan requests
- Are working on projects too small or too personal to qualify for grants

**This project proves that rigorous, honest, source-grounded historical research can be done from a kitchen table** — with free tools, open-access archives, and a commitment to never claiming more than the evidence supports.

## How Git Keeps It Honest

Every change to this archive is committed to a public GitHub repository with:
- Full version history (nothing is ever truly lost)
- Timestamped commits (you can see exactly when a finding was added or a claim was corrected)
- Diff visibility (you can see exactly what changed in every update)
- Public accountability (anyone can verify the research trail)

Git doesn't care about credentials. It cares about what you actually did, in what order, and whether you showed your work. That's the standard this project holds itself to.

## How AI Tools Fit In

AI tools (currently Migoo) are used as **research assistants**, not as sources. Specifically:
- Searching digitized archives and databases
- Formatting and organizing findings
- Stress-testing hypotheses (the 4-agent cycle)
- Documenting negative results and dead ends
- Maintaining the archive's structure and consistency

AI-generated content is **never treated as primary evidence** (Law 4 — No Algorithmic Contamination). Every AI contribution is tagged, every source is independently verifiable, and every claim traces back to a human-readable document that existed before the AI touched it.

## What's Explicitly Out of Scope

- **Grant applications** — the project documents what can be done without them
- **Paid research services** (NEHGS, professional genealogists) — same reason
- **Institutional partnerships** — the project is independent by design

This doesn't mean these are bad options. They're good options for people who have access to them. This project documents what's possible for people who don't.

## The Standard

The 4 Laws + 4 Operating Principles govern everything:

### The 4 Laws
1. **No Narrative Smoothing** — contradictions stay visible
2. **La Mance Law** — no claim accepted from La Mance (1904) without independent corroboration
3. **No Premature Disqualification** — hypotheses stay open until evidence closes them
4. **No Algorithmic Contamination** — AI output never becomes source material

### The 4 Operating Principles
1. Extra caution and respect toward Indigenous culture
2. No overclaiming
3. Be kind and explain reasoning
4. Stick to the 4 Laws always
