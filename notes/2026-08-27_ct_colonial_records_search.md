## Volume 2 (1665–1677)

### Henry Tibbitts — New Spelling Variants and Roles

| Page(s) | Spelling | Context |
|---------|----------|---------|
| 138 | **Henry Tippett** | Appointed constable of Wickford with Samuel Eldred |
| 157 | **Henry Tippett** | Again appointed constable for Wickford |
| 540 | **Henry Tibbots** | Named in Rhode Island complaint — seized by Connecticut authority |
| 553 | **Henry Tippitt** | Listed among Wickford constables |

**Significance for the Seven-Name Network:** Tibbitts served as a **Wickford constable under Connecticut's jurisdiction** — placing him in the same contested territory as John Greene. He was later seized by Connecticut authority, generating a Rhode Island protest. This confirms that Tibbitts was politically active in the Narragansett disputes, not just a neighbor.

**New spelling variants to add to search parameters:** Tippett, Tippitt, Tibbots (in addition to Tibbitts, Tibets, Tibbits).

### The Atherton Company — 1659 Deed and Sachem Names

**pp. 541–542:** Reproduces a **1659 deed from sachem Coginaquant** to the Atherton proprietors:
- Governor John Winthrop
- Major Humphrey Atherton
- Richard Smith Sr. and Jr.
- William Hudson
- Amos Richardson
- John Tinker

**⚠️ CRITICAL for Open Thread #9:** The sachem name in this deed is recorded as **Coginaquant/Coginaquand**. Footnote variants include: **Cojonoquant, Cachanaquant, Tassarono, Tasconohut, Tesiquant**. He is identified as a son or nephew of Canonicus.

This is the same name-cluster as the source spine's Open Thread #9 (Coginiquant / Cogineaquon / Cononjonant). The CT records add **five new spelling variants** to the cluster and confirm the sachem as an Atherton Company grantor — directly connecting the CT Atherton records to the Fones Record orbit.

## Volume 3 (1678–1689)

### ⚠️ IMPORTANT CORRECTION: Greene and Smith Were on Opposing Sides

The source spine's Seven-Name Network treats Greene and Smith as part of the same orbit. This is true geographically but **misleading politically:**

- **John Greene** appears in Vol. 3 as a **Warwick/Rhode Island agent** — he and Randall Holden advocated Rhode Island's jurisdictional claim in England (pp. 37, 270, 272)
- **Richard Smith** appears as a **Connecticut-aligned Narragansett proprietor** and Wickford commissioner (pp. 69, 269, 321)

They are neighbors who signed the same 1663 petition but ended up on **opposing sides** of the jurisdictional dispute. This doesn't break the Seven-Name Network — they still co-sign documents and live in the same orbit — but it means the network is **not a unified faction.** It's a contested community.

**Per Law 1 (No Narrative Smoothing):** This contradiction must be held open. Greene signing a Connecticut petition in 1663 and then acting as Rhode Island's agent in the 1680s is not inconsistent — it reflects the shifting politics of a contested region. But it means we cannot assume Greene and Smith were allies in all periods.

### Other Vol. 3 Findings
- **Ninigret** — pp. 103, 474–476, 488: complaints involving Uncas, captives, threats
- **Richard Smith** — p. 69: appointed commissioner for Wickford with Jere Bull; p. 269: signs letter re RI claims; p. 321: listed among Atherton proprietary heirs; pp. 511–512: petitions for wartime compensation
- **King's Province:** No single establishment act. The term appears at pp. 270–272, 320–321, 507 as *"Narragansett Countrey or the Kings Prouince"* — always in the context of contested jurisdiction

## Joan-Specific Findings

**Joan Greene does not appear in any of the three volumes.** This is a genuine negative result, not a dismissal (per Law 5).

**Why this is expected:** The CT colonial records are primarily General Court proceedings — political, military, and administrative records. Women rarely appear in these except in probate, criminal, or land conveyance contexts. Joan would be more likely to appear in:
- Town-level land records (not in these volumes)
- Probate records (checked Manwaring digest — no Greene match, but spelling variants not exhausted)
- Church records (not in these volumes)
