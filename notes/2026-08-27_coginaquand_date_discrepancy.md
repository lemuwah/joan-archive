# Coginaquand Date Discrepancy — 1659 vs. 1687
**Date:** 2026-08-27  
**Source:** Bartlett, *Records of the Colony of Rhode Island*, Vol. 3; Fones Record  

---

## The 5 Laws apply throughout this document.

---

## The Question

Bartlett Vol. 3 records a Coginaquand deed granting Namcock Neck, with entry dated around July 4, 1687. But the foundational Coginaquand Namcock Neck deed is well-established as **July 4, 1659** — one of the two original Atherton Company purchases.

## The Two Possibilities

### 1. Re-recording of the 1659 deed (MORE LIKELY)
The 1687 appearance in Bartlett may be a later re-recording, confirmation, or certified copy of the original 1659 deed, entered into the record book during the Andros administration (1686–89). This was common practice — the Fones Record itself is largely a 1703 compilation of earlier deeds.

### 2. New transaction in 1687 (LESS LIKELY)
Coginaquand could have made a new grant or confirmation in 1687. This would require him to still be alive and recognized as Chief Sachem 28 years after the original deed and 11 years after King Philip's War. Not impossible (Ninigret survived the war), but the language of the 1687 recording closely mirrors the 1659 original.

## Why This Matters

- If it's a re-recording: the witnesses (including Nath. Greene) may be **recording-witnesses**, not original deed witnesses. This reduces the significance of Nath. Greene's appearance.
- If it's a new transaction: it means a sachem was still exercising authority in 1687 and Greene family members were still involved in Narragansett land transactions post-John Sr.'s death.

**Per Law 1:** Both readings are held open. Resolution requires examining the original volume in Bartlett and comparing the exact language and witness formulas.
