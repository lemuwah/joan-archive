## Why This List Exists

## 🟢 Confirmed Browsable (Free, No Paywall)

### 1. Rhode Island State Archives — Preservica Digital Archive
- **URL:** https://sosri.access.preservica.com/home/
- **Key collection:** Land and Public Notary Records, Vol. 1 (1648–1696)
  - **Direct link:** https://sosri.access.preservica.com/uncategorized/SO_34511b06-2694-4cbf-8833-645ddd7384c2/
  - **Why it matters:** This is the exact volume that should contain the 1682 Pawtuxet deed. If the pages are browsable as manuscript scans (not just a catalog entry), Joan's mark could be here.
  - **Status as of 2026-08-27:** NEEDS VERIFICATION — the Preservica link exists but we have not confirmed whether individual page images are browsable or just the catalog metadata.
- **Also relevant:** Fones Record (1659–1679) — deeds and land records from Narragansett country
  - **Catalog:** https://catalog.sos.ri.gov/repositories/2/resources/409

### 2. Plymouth County Registry of Deeds
- **URL:** https://www.plymouthdeeds.org/1272/Records-Inventory
- **Coverage:** Searchable scanned grantor/grantee books **back to 1685**
- **Why it matters:** Late-17th-century deed images with visible handwriting, marks, and signatures. Search for women as grantors, grantees, spouses, witnesses, and in dower releases.
- **Cost:** Free to browse online.
- **Limitation:** Starts 1685, so 3 years after Joan's 1682 deed. But still within the comparison window for the mark typology.

### 3. Internet Archive — Colonial Record Volumes (Printed Transcriptions)
- **Rhode Island Land Evidences, Vol. I:** https://archive.org/stream/rhodeislandlande00wort/rhodeislandlande00wort_djvu.txt
- **Records of the Colony of New Plymouth (multiple vols):** https://archive.org/details/recordsofcolonyo1112newp
- **Records of Massachusetts Bay Colony:** Available via Internet Archive search
- **Limitation:** These are **printed transcriptions**, not manuscript facsimiles. Useful as finding aids (names, dates, page references) but cannot show visual marks.
- **Cost:** Free.

### 4. Connecticut State Library Digital Collections
- **URL:** https://cslib.contentdm.oclc.org/
- **Coverage:** Colonial Connecticut laws, court materials, manuscripts
- **Finding aid for land records:** https://cslarchives.ctstatelibrary.org/repositories/2/resources/32
- **Why it matters:** Connecticut deed books from 1640s onward. Some may have digitized manuscript images browsable through FamilySearch (linked from finding aids).
- **Cost:** Free.

### 5. Massachusetts Archives Digital Records
- **URL:** https://www.sec.state.ma.us/divisions/archives/collections/digital-records.htm
- **Coverage:** Colony records, court records, some land records
- **Why it matters:** Court of Assistants records (1630–1692) may contain depositions and petitions with women's marks.
- **Cost:** Free.

### 6. FamilySearch (Limited Free Access)
- **URL:** https://www.familysearch.org
- **Coverage:** Varies enormously by collection. Some colonial record microfilms are digitized and browsable with a free account. Others are restricted to FamilySearch Centers.
- **How to check:** Search Catalog by place name or film number. Look for the camera icon (no key = free online browsing).
- **Rhode Island wiki:** https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Rhode_Island_Land_and_Property
- **Connecticut wiki:** https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Connecticut_Land_and_Property
- **Cost:** Free account required. Some collections restricted.

## The Access Gap

This list illustrates a structural problem in colonial-era research: **the most important original manuscript pages are often the least accessible.** Printed transcriptions are widely available. Original handwriting — the marks, the marginalia, the clerk's annotations — is locked behind institutional access barriers that disproportionately affect independent researchers.

This is not a complaint. It's a documented observation. The institutions have real constraints (fragile documents, limited staff). But the effect is that the physical evidence — the thing a historian needs to see with their own eyes — remains out of reach for people who can't travel to Providence or Salt Lake City.

The Joan Archive documents this gap as part of the research process, not just the research results.
