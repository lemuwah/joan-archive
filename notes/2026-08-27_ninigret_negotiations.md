## The 29 October 1676 Exchange

Mediated by Major Fitz-John Winthrop. Three-part diplomatic transaction:

1. **Ninigret delivered** some unnamed captives associated with Philip's party to the English
2. **The English delivered** "the two children he desires" back to Ninigret
3. **Sebawcatt** was retained by the English as a hostage

### What We Know
- The two children were NOT part of the captives Ninigret delivered — they were people the English controlled and returned to him
- This was a negotiated diplomatic transaction under conditions of continuing English coercion
- Ninigret was managing which people stayed under his protection

### What We Don't Know
- The names of the captives Ninigret delivered
- The names or identities of "the two children he desires"
- Who Sebawcatt was — gender, age, lineage, fate (see separate note)
- Whether any of these people were connected to the Anashuecot/Fones Record orbit

## Ninigret's Kinship to the Narragansett Sachem Line

| Relationship | Person | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Ninigret's mother | Sister of Canonicus (paramount Narragansett sachem) | Fisher & Silverman |
| Ninigret's sister | Quaiapen ("Old Queen") — married Mixano (Canonicus's son) | Fisher & Silverman |
| Ninigret's probable wife | Sister of Miantonomi | Fisher & Silverman |
| Ninigret's daughter | **Weunquesh** — succeeded Ninigret as Niantic/Narragansett female sachem | Colonial records |
| Quinnapin | Described as son of Ninigret, grandson of Canonchet; married Weetamoo (1675) | Colonial Society of Massachusetts |
| Canonicus | Paramount Narragansett sachem; Coginaquant described as his son or nephew | CT colonial records Vol. 2 |

**Significance:** Ninigret was not a distant neutral power. He was direct family. His mother was Canonicus's sister. Moving someone to Ninigret's territory was moving them to a relative's house — and his daughter Weunquesh later became a female sachem herself, proving sachem-line women survived in this orbit.

## The Broader Context: Ninigret as Shelter

- Boissevain: *"many found refuge with the eastern Niantics"* — postwar merger of Narragansett and Niantic populations
- Fisher & Silverman: Ninigret's neutrality was strategic, preserving Niantic autonomy and power
- The United Colonies commissioners' policy required Narragansett people to deliver captives associated with Philip, assist the English, and provide hostages — Ninigret's compliance was calculated and selective

## Earlier Precedent: Long Island Captives

Ninigret had previously raided Long Island communities and carried away approximately 14 captives, described as prominent or "chief" women. These were later restored through English mediation. Named individuals from later antiquarian sources: **Wuchikittawbut** (also Catoneras) and the daughter of Montauk sachem **Wyandanch** (commonly identified as **Quashawam**), with the rescue associated with Lion Gardiner.

**⚠️ Per Law 2:** These identifications come from later antiquarian materials, not primary sources. Held ⚪ UNVERIFIED.
