## Background

John Greene of Quidnessett's household included at least one Indian servant named **Marcy** and her daughter **Sarah**. This is referenced in secondary literature but has never been traced to a primary archival source with court, date, volume, or page.

## Search Results

### What was found:
- The only currently located reference is a sentence on a **Rocky Hill Country Day School educational page** — no court, no date, no volume, no page number
- The reference treats the Marcy/Sarah presence as established fact but provides no citation chain

### What was NOT found:
- No primary court record naming Marcy or Sarah in Greene's household
- No servant indenture or disposal certificate for either name
- No probate entry listing them as household members
- No will or inventory mentioning them

### Why the Fones Record is unlikely to contain it:
- The Fones Record is primarily a collection of deeds, not servant records or court proceedings
- Servant assignments, indentures, and disposal certificates would be in **town records** or **court records**, not land evidence volumes

## Best Targets for the Original Record

In priority order:

1. **North Kingstown town records** — the town clerk holds the earliest local records; servant assignments would be municipal
2. **Washington County Court of Common Pleas** — servant disputes, assignments, and enforcement records
3. **RI General Assembly records** — if the assignment was made under the 1676 disposal legislation, it may appear in the Assembly minutes
4. **RIHS collections** — particularly:
   - Clarke Indian Manuscripts (MSS 461)
   - Edwards and Angell Legal Files
   - Narragansett land deeds collection
5. **Providence town records** — *Early Records of the Town of Providence*, Vol. 15, pp. 151–154 contains the disposal committee proceedings; Marcy/Sarah may appear in adjacent entries

## Why This Matters

If Marcy and Sarah are documented as living in Greene's household:
- It confirms Greene had Indigenous people in his home — establishing precedent for cross-cultural household composition
- The timing matters — if Marcy was assigned under the 1676 disposal, it's postwar. If she was there earlier, it suggests a pre-existing relationship
- Sarah's presence as Marcy's daughter means a **Native child grew up in Greene's household** — this is the domestic context Joan also lived in

**⚠️ Per Law 1:** We do not infer from Marcy's presence that Joan was also Indigenous. The facts are: (1) at least one Native woman lived in Greene's home; (2) Joan's identity is unknown. These are two separate data points.

## Status

**The Marcy/Sarah servant record is currently UNCITED.** It is referenced in secondary literature without a primary source. Until a court record, indenture, or town record is located, the claim that Marcy and Sarah lived in Greene's household rests on an uncited assertion.

This is not the same as saying it's false — it's saying the citation chain is broken and needs to be repaired before it can be treated as primary evidence.
