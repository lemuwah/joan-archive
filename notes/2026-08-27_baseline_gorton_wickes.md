## Purpose

The 19 May 1682 Pawtuxet deed (John Greene to William Carpenter) was witnessed by **John Wickes** and **Samuel Gorton** (likely Samuel Gorton Jr., since Samuel Gorton Sr. died 1677). The purpose of this baseline study is to determine whether this witness pair was **routine** or **notable** for the period and location.

**Why this matters:**
- If Wickes and Gorton witnessed dozens of deeds together in this period, their presence on Joan's deed is ordinary — they were simply available local notables.
- If they rarely appeared together, or if their presence on this deed is unusual given their social/political positions, it may tell us something about the significance of this transaction or the parties involved.
- The witness list is one of the few pieces of "social network" evidence in Joan's record.

## Methodology

### Sample Frame
- **Primary:** All recorded deeds in Warwick, Pawtuxet, and Quidnessett/North Kingstown, 1675–1690
- **Secondary:** All recorded deeds in Rhode Island colony where either Wickes or Gorton appears as witness

### What We're Measuring
- Total deeds witnessed by John Wickes in sample period
- Total deeds witnessed by Samuel Gorton (Jr.) in sample period
- Number of deeds where BOTH appear as co-witnesses
- Their witness rate on Greene family deeds specifically vs. deeds for other families
- Their general social/political standing in this period (town offices held, land ownership, church membership)

### Sources to Check
- *Early Records of the Town of Providence* (Internet Archive)
- *Rhode Island Land Evidences,* Vol. I
- Warwick town records (if digitized)
- *Records of the Colony of Rhode Island* (Bartlett ed.)

### Classification
- **ROUTINE PAIR:** Co-witnessed >5 deeds in the sample period
- **OCCASIONAL PAIR:** Co-witnessed 2–5 deeds
- **RARE PAIR:** This is the only known co-witnessed deed

## Preliminary Context (Not Findings)

- **Samuel Gorton Sr.** (d. 1677) was the founder of Gortonism and a major political figure. His son Samuel Jr. continued as a civic leader in Warwick.
- **John Wickes** was a Warwick/Pawtuxet landowner. The Wickes family intersected with the Greenes across multiple generations.
- Both families were part of the dissenting/separatist social networks that defined early Rhode Island — people who rejected Massachusetts orthodoxy.
- Their co-presence as witnesses *could* indicate social proximity, political alliance, or simply that they were the two nearest literate men when the deed was signed.

**Warning (Law 1 — No Smoothing):** Do not assume significance. Many witness pairs in colonial deeds were simply whoever was available. The baseline must be established before any inference about this specific pair's meaning.
