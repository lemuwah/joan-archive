**The original version of this file (committed earlier on 2026-08-27) classified Connecticut records as "LOW RELEVANCE FOR JOAN SPECIFICALLY" and recommended deprioritizing them.** That classification was based on the assumption that Connecticut land records would only cover Connecticut colony deeds, not Rhode Island ones.

**That assumption was wrong.** It was a textbook violation of the law that didn't exist yet when the file was written — but that's exactly why the law was needed.

Connecticut records are not "cross-border comparisons" — they are **direct records of the same contested territory where Joan lived.**

## What the Re-Search Found

### 1. John Greene Signed the 1663 Connecticut Jurisdiction Petition — 🟢 CONFIRMED

On **July 3, 1663**, inhabitants and proprietors of Narragansett petitioned to come under Connecticut government pursuant to the royal charter. **John Greene signed this petition**, alongside Richard Smith and other Narragansett residents.

This means:
- John Greene of Quidnessett appears **by name** in Connecticut General Court records
- He actively supported Connecticut's jurisdictional claim over the territory where he and Joan lived
- Rhode Island subsequently summoned or proceeded against John Greene for this
- **Connecticut's records of this dispute are primary sources for the Greene family's political and legal orbit**

**Source:** *Public Records of the Colony of Connecticut*, Vol. 1 (available on Internet Archive)

### 2. The Atherton Company Operated from Connecticut

The Atherton Company (1659) was a Connecticut-based land syndicate claiming vast tracts of Narragansett territory. Key members included John Winthrop Jr. (Governor of Connecticut), Major Humphrey Atherton, Richard Smith Sr., and other Massachusetts/Connecticut investors.

**John Greene is not documented as a principal Atherton purchaser**, but he was a Quidnessett landholder and local supporter of the Atherton-Connecticut position. The Atherton Company's records — deeds, petitions, court proceedings — document the same territory, the same sachem names (Coquinaquant/Coginiquant), and the same English settler network as Joan's deed orbit.

### 3. Connecticut General Court Records Cover the Narragansett Disputes

Three published volumes of Connecticut colonial records cover the relevant period:
- **Vol. 1:** General Court records, Feb 1650 – May 1665
- **Vol. 2:** Records 1665–1677
- **Vol. 3:** Records 1678–1689

All three are on Internet Archive. Productive search terms:
- Greene/Green, Richard Smith, Quidnessett/Aquidneset, Narragansett, Wickford, Atherton, Coquinaquant, John Winthrop

**These are browsable, free, printed transcriptions** — exactly the kind of source this project can use tonight.

### 4. Hartford Probate District — Manwaring Digest

**Manwaring's *Digest of the Early Connecticut Probate Records, Hartford District, 1635–1700*** (Vol. 1) is on Internet Archive and has a name index. No Greene family probate entries for the 1650–1680 period were found in the initial search, but the volume has not been manually checked under all spelling variants (Green, Greene, Greane, Grene).

### 5. Joan Greene in Connecticut Records — NOT YET FOUND

Joan does not appear as a named party in:
- Atherton Company deeds
- The 1663 jurisdiction petition
- Connecticut General Court proceedings

**This is a genuine negative result, not a dismissal.** Joan's absence from these specific records is documented. But:
- The Connecticut records have not been exhaustively searched
- Joan would be less likely to appear in political petitions (women rarely signed those) than in land conveyances or probate records
- The Atherton Company deeds involve sachem names from Joan's orbit (Coquinaquant = the Coginiquant/Cogineaquon name-cluster from Open Thread #9 on the source spine)

## Meta-Note: Why This Correction Matters

This file is a case study in why Law 5 exists. The original classification assumed that "Connecticut records = Connecticut deeds = not relevant to Rhode Island." That assumption ignored the fundamental reality of 17th-century Narragansett politics: **there was no agreed border.** Connecticut, Rhode Island, Massachusetts, and the Crown all claimed the same land. The records of each authority document the same people, the same land, the same disputes.

Dismissing a repository because of its modern state name is a category error. Law 5 prevents it.
