**Date:** 2026-08-27  
**Absolom Affidavit text (1677/78):** Anashuecot was *"conveyed out of the country"*  
**Father named as:** Tocomminon  
**Affidavit also names:** *"Washaucutt"* as a brother

## The Three Interpretations

### 1. EXPORTED AS CAPTIVE — 🟡 PROBABLE

**Evidence for:**
- "Conveyed" = active transport verb (carried, sent, delivered)
- "Out of the country" = standard postwar formula for removal beyond the colony
- Plymouth certificates described captives as *"sentenced and condemned to perpetual servitude"*
- Massachusetts authorized *"shipping them off or otherwise"*
- Documented destinations: Bermuda, Barbados, Jamaica, Spain, Portugal, Madeira, Azores, Cádiz
- **Metacom's wife and son were sold to Bermuda** — precedent for sachem-family export
- The 1676 Barbados statute prohibiting importation of New England Indians confirms the flow existed

**Evidence against:**
- No specific destination named for Anashuecot
- No shipping record or sale receipt identified
- The affidavit does not use the word "sold" or "enslaved"

### 2. FORCED EXILE / DEPORTATION — ⚪ UNVERIFIED

**Evidence for:**
- "Conveyed out" could mean simply removed/expelled
- Some Native people fled or were driven from the territory without being sold
- Exile of defeated leaders was a documented practice

**Evidence against:**
- The postwar policy context overwhelmingly involved sale/servitude, not free exile
- "Conveyed" implies someone else doing the moving, not self-exile

### 3. DEATH / EXECUTION EUPHEMISM — ⚫ UNLIKELY

**Evidence for:**
- Euphemistic language existed in colonial records
- Anashuecot disappears from the record entirely

**Evidence against:**
- Colonial records used direct death vocabulary for sachem executions:
  - Canonchet: *"captured and executed"* — shot, quartered, decapitated
  - Quaiapen: *"killed"* in ambush
  - Metacom: *"killed"* — body quartered, head displayed
- "Conveyed" is incompatible with death as a literal meaning
- The affidavit's purpose (preserving land rights) makes no sense if Anashuecot is dead — a death notice would serve that purpose directly

---

## Why the Affidavit Exists

If Anashuecot was exported, the Absolom affidavit is not a death notice — it's a **land-rights preservation document.** Absolom, as "Counsellor," is recording:
1. That Anashuecot was the legitimate heir ("chiefe Sachem and heire properly")
2. That Anashuecot has been physically removed
3. That Tocomminon was the father and Mr. Browne was left as guardian

This is a legal act by a political official documenting the displacement of a rightful heir — exactly the kind of document someone would create to preserve a land claim in the heir's absence.

## Implications for the Joan Hypothesis

### If Joan = Anashuecot:
- Anashuecot was exported c.1676–77
- Joan appears in Greene's household in 1682
- This requires a **return** from captivity within ~5–6 years
- Returns from Caribbean enslavement were documented but rare
- Returns from domestic servitude within New England were more plausible
- **The "or to her mother if she survive" clause in the 1682 deed** takes on new weight: it explicitly contemplates Joan's mother as someone whose survival is uncertain

### If Joan ≠ Anashuecot:
- The 31-year gap remains unexplained
- Anashuecot's fate is a separate tragedy
- Joan's identity is still unknown

**Per Law 1:** Both branches are held open. Neither is forced to resolution.
