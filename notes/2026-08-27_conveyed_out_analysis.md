## The Word "Country"

Before ranking interpretations, the word **"country"** itself is ambiguous:

- **Broad reading:** "country" = New England / the English colonies generally. "Out of the country" = overseas or beyond colonial jurisdiction.
- **Narrow reading:** "country" = the Narragansett country specifically. Colonial records use "Narragansett country" as a defined territory (cf. CT colonial records: *"Narragansett Countrey or the Kings Prouince"*). "Out of the country" could mean out of that specific territory — not necessarily overseas.

**The affidavit does not specify which meaning applies.** Per Law 1, both are held open.

## The Five Interpretations

### 1. EXPORTED AS CAPTIVE — ⚪ UNVERIFIED

**What it would mean:** Anashuecot was captured by colonial forces and transported overseas (to Bermuda, Barbados, Jamaica, or elsewhere) as a captive sold into slavery or perpetual servitude.

**Evidence for:**
- "Conveyed" = active transport verb (carried, sent, delivered)
- "Out of the country" = standard postwar formula in colonial captive records for removal beyond the colony
- Plymouth certificates described captives as *"sentenced and condemned to perpetual servitude"*
- Massachusetts authorized *"shipping them off or otherwise"*
- Documented destinations: Bermuda, Barbados, Jamaica, Spain, Portugal, Madeira, Azores, Cádiz
- **Metacom's wife and young son were sold to Bermuda** — documented precedent for sachem-family export
- The 1676 Barbados statute prohibiting importation of New England Indians confirms the flow existed

**Evidence against:**
- No specific destination named for Anashuecot
- No shipping record or sale receipt identified
- The affidavit does not use the word "sold" or "enslaved"
- "Country" might mean Narragansett territory specifically, not all of New England
- The affidavit was created to preserve land rights — if Anashuecot was permanently enslaved overseas, why preserve a claim?

### 2. FORCED EXILE / DEPORTATION — ⚪ UNVERIFIED

**What it would mean:** Anashuecot was expelled from the territory by colonial authorities without being sold — driven out rather than transported for sale.

**Evidence for:**
- "Conveyed out" could mean simply removed/expelled
- Some Native people fled or were driven from the territory without being sold
- Exile of defeated groups was a documented practice

**Evidence against:**
- The postwar policy context overwhelmingly involved sale/servitude, not free exile
- "Conveyed" implies someone else doing the moving, not self-exile
- Colonial authorities had economic incentives to sell rather than simply exile

### 3. DEATH / EXECUTION EUPHEMISM — ⚪ UNVERIFIED (UNLIKELY)

**What it would mean:** "Conveyed out of the country" is a euphemism for death — either execution or death in captivity.

**Evidence for:**
- Euphemistic language existed in colonial records
- Anashuecot disappears from the record entirely

**Evidence against:**
- Colonial records used direct death vocabulary for sachem executions:
  - Canonchet: *"captured and executed"* — shot, quartered, decapitated
  - Quaiapen: *"killed"* in ambush
  - Metacom: *"killed"* — body quartered, head displayed
- "Conveyed" is an active transport verb — it implies the person was taken somewhere
- If Anashuecot was dead, Absolom could have said so. The affidavit's purpose (preserving land rights) would be better served by a death notice than by ambiguous displacement language

### 4. SHELTERED WITH NEUTRAL / ALLIED NATIVE GROUP — ⚪ UNVERIFIED

**What it would mean:** Anashuecot was moved to the territory of a neutral or allied Native group — most likely Ninigret's Eastern Niantic — through Indigenous agency, not colonial capture. "Conveyed out of the country" = removed from the Narragansett country to Niantic territory.

**Evidence for:**
- **Ninigret's Niantic maintained formal neutrality** during King Philip's War
- **Ninigret was kin to the Narragansett sachem line** — his mother was the sister of Canonicus, the paramount sachem. This is not a distant alliance; it's direct family.
- **Narragansett refugees documented sheltering with the Niantic.** Boissevain: *"many found refuge with the eastern Niantics"* — the two populations were thereafter treated as one tribe
- **"Country" could mean Narragansett country specifically.** CT colonial records use "Narragansett Countrey" as a defined territory. "Out of the country" could mean out of that specific territory into Niantic lands — physically nearby but politically distinct
- **Kinship-based incorporation was the documented mechanism.** The postwar Niantic-Narragansett merger was a continuation of pre-existing kinship structures, not an ad hoc arrangement
- **Ninigret negotiated over specific captives.** A 29 October 1676 record shows Ninigret delivering some captives to the English while also requesting the return of two children — demonstrating that he was actively managing which people stayed under his protection
- **The affidavit preserves a land claim.** If Anashuecot was sheltered with kin, Absolom's act of recording the displacement makes perfect sense — it's documenting that the heir is alive but not present, maintaining the claim for a possible return

**Evidence against:**
- The affidavit uses "conveyed," which in colonial legal language typically implies an external agent moving someone — it's not the word you'd use for someone who fled voluntarily
- If Anashuecot was safely with Ninigret, why use language that sounds like coercive removal?
- No surviving record explicitly places Anashuecot among the Niantic

**Key kinship connections (documented):**
- Ninigret's mother = sister of Canonicus
- Ninigret's sister Quaiapen married Mixano (Canonicus's son)
- Ninigret probably married a sister of Miantonomi
- Quinnapin (described as son of Ninigret and grandson of Canonchet) married Weetamoo in 1675

### 5. CONCEALED WITHIN ENGLISH OR MIXED HOUSEHOLD — ⚪ UNVERIFIED

**What it would mean:** Anashuecot was hidden from colonial authorities by being absorbed into an English household — either as a "servant" (the legal category that made Native presence in English homes legible) or through marriage/cohabitation. "Conveyed out of the country" = removed from public view, no longer visible as a Narragansett sachem heir.

**Evidence for:**
- Native women and children were absorbed into English households after King Philip's War — this is thoroughly documented
- Household status could obscure Native identity and make someone less visible to colonial roundup systems
- John Greene's own household included at least one Indian servant (Marcy) and her daughter (Sarah) — establishing that Native women lived in Greene's house
- A Native woman in an English household, especially one married to the householder, would be functionally invisible to colonial record-keeping systems designed to track "enemy" Indians

**Evidence against:**
- "Conveyed out of the country" in colonial records denotes physical transportation, not concealment — the scholarship is clear on this (Newell, Plane)
- The documented postwar pattern is **coercive absorption** (forced servitude, indenture, enslavement within households), not protective concealment
- There is no documented network of English colonists secretly sheltering Native people from authorities
- If Anashuecot was hidden in Greene's household and later appeared as "Joan" in the 1682 deed, the affidavit language makes less sense — why say "conveyed out of the country" for someone living next door?

## What We Do NOT Know

Per Law 1, the following blanks are held open without filling:

- **Who did the conveying.** Colonial authorities? Narragansett allies? Ninigret's people? The affidavit doesn't say.
- **Where Anashuecot went.** Bermuda? Niantic territory? An English household? Somewhere else entirely? The affidavit doesn't say.
- **Whether Anashuecot survived.** The affidavit preserves a claim, which makes more sense if Anashuecot is alive — but that's inference, not evidence.
- **Whether Anashuecot ever returned.** No document records a return.
- **Whether Joan is Anashuecot.** Nothing in the "conveyed out" language connects or disconnects the two.

---

## Why This Matters

The interpretation of "conveyed out of the country" determines what kind of evidence we should be looking for:

- If **exported overseas** → search Bermuda/Barbados arrival records
- If **exiled** → search neighboring colony records for displaced Narragansett individuals
- If **killed** → the trail is cold
- If **sheltered with Ninigret/Niantic** → search Niantic territory records, post-1676 Narragansett-Niantic merger documents, tribal rolls
- If **concealed in a household** → search English household inventories, servant/indenture records, marriage records

**All five search paths remain open. No interpretation is ranked above the others.**
