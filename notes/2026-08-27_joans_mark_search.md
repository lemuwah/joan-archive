### 3. FamilySearch Microfilms — DETAILED FINDINGS

#### Film 22291 & 22292 — Peirce Manuscripts
- **Content:** "Peirce Manuscripts of Rhode Island Land Evidences, 1686–1826" by Louise Prosser Bates
- **Source:** Filmed from manuscripts at Rhode Island Historical Society, 1950
- **Nature:** **Manuscript (handwritten) abstracts** of original land evidences — not typeset
- **Wiki reference:** https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Rhode_Island_Land_and_Property
- **Result:** 🟢 STRONG LEAD — but date range starts at 1686, which is **4 years after** the 1682 deed. The 1682 Pawtuxet deed may or may not be included depending on how Bates organized her abstracts (chronologically vs. by source volume).
- **Access status:** UNKNOWN — requires checking the FamilySearch catalog directly (see instructions below).

#### 🆕 Film 564389 — Rhode Island Land Evidences, Vol. I (1648–1696)
- **Content:** *Rhode Island Land Evidences, Vol. I, 1648–1696* (likely the printed volume compiled by William Worthington)
- **Date range:** 1648–1696 — **directly covers the 1682 deed period**
- **Archive.org text version:** https://archive.org/stream/rhodeislandlande00wort/rhodeislandlande00wort_djvu.txt
- **Result:** 🟢 **NEW STRONGEST LEAD** — this volume's date range explicitly encompasses 1682 and covers all of Rhode Island colony land evidences for the period. If the 1682 Pawtuxet deed was recorded as a colony-level land evidence (which Pawtuxet deeds were, given the jurisdictional complexity), it should be in this volume.
- **Access status:** UNKNOWN — requires checking the FamilySearch catalog directly.
- **Important caveat:** The Archive.org link is to the **printed text** version. The FamilySearch film may be from the **manuscript original** or from the printed volume. If from the manuscript, Joan's mark may be visually reproduced. If from the printed volume, it will be typeset only (same limitation as Internet Archive Vol. 14).

### How to Check FamilySearch Film Access (Step-by-Step)

1. Sign in to [FamilySearch.org](https://www.familysearch.org)
2. Go to **Search → Catalog**
3. Click **Film/Fiche Number** search
4. Enter film number: **564389** (priority 1), **22291** (priority 2), or **22292** (priority 3)
5. Click the resulting title to open the catalog entry
6. Look at the **Film/Digital Notes** section for the access icon:
   - 📷 **Camera (no key)** = images browsable online, free, after sign-in
   - 🔐 **Camera with key** = digitized but restricted to viewing at a FamilySearch Center or Affiliate Library
   - ❌ **No camera** = not digitized; physical microfilm only
7. If the camera icon is present, click it to open the image viewer (starts at Image 1 of Item 1, browse frame by frame)

## Why the Mark Matters

Joan's mark on the 19 May 1682 Pawtuxet deed is the **only physical evidence she personally created**. Every other record mentioning her was written by someone else (clerks, scribes, John himself). The mark is:
- The closest thing to Joan's own "voice" in the archive
- Potentially diagnostic: the shape, confidence, and style of the mark could indicate literacy level, cultural context, or comparison with other known marks from the same period
- A concrete visual artifact in a project that has been working almost entirely from text transcriptions
- Comparable: if found, it can be placed side-by-side with marks from the Brown "Their Marks" project (Indigenous marks) and other colonial women's marks to see where it fits stylistically

Finding a visual image of this mark is the project's current #1 priority.
