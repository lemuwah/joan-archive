## Sources Searched

### 1. Rhode Island State Archives — Preservica Digital Archive
- **URL:** https://sosri.access.preservica.com/home/
- **Result:** ❌ No digitized image of the 1682 Pawtuxet deed found.
- **Notes:** The Preservica platform is live and searchable. A search for "pawtucket" returned results but nothing matching the specific 1682 Greene-to-Carpenter deed. The archive's digitization coverage of 1680s land records is unclear — many early deeds may not yet be in the system.
- **Classification:** CHECKED NEGATIVE — may warrant a direct inquiry to RI State Archives staff about their 1680s deed digitization status.

### 2. Internet Archive — "Early Records of the Town of Providence," Vol. 14
- **URL:** https://archive.org/details/earlyrecordsofto02prov (series)
- **Index volume:** https://archive.org/details/cu31924028840242
- **Result:** ⚠️ PARTIAL — Volume 14 is confirmed as "the first book for the recording of deeds and called Deed Book no. 1" (per Warwick Library reference: https://warwicklibrary.org/early-records-town-providence).
- **Critical limitation:** This is a **printed transcription**, not a facsimile. It would contain typeset text (e.g., "Joan Greene, her marke") — NOT a visual image of the actual handwritten mark.
- **What it CAN do:** Provide the exact page reference to locate the corresponding frame on microfilm.
- **Classification:** USEFUL FOR CROSS-REFERENCE ONLY — cannot provide the visual mark itself.

### 3. FamilySearch Microfilms 22291–22292 (Peirce Manuscripts)
- **Catalog reference:** https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Rhode_Island_Land_and_Property
- **Result:** 🟢 STRONGEST LEAD
- **Details:** These two reels contain the **"Peirce Manuscripts of Rhode Island Land Evidences, 1686–1826" by Louise Prosser Bates**, filmed from manuscripts at RIHS in 1950. FamilySearch states that digitized microfilm images without restricted access can be viewed online when signed in.
- **Why this matters:** These are **manuscript copies** of original land evidences — handwritten, not typeset. If Joan's mark was copied by the transcriber (as was common practice), this is where it would appear.
- **Next step:** Search FamilySearch Catalog by film number (https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/FamilySearch_Catalog_Film/Fiche_Number_Search). If online access is restricted, a FamilySearch Center visit would be needed.
- **Classification:** ACTIONABLE — highest priority follow-up.

### 4. Brown University "Their Marks" Project
- **Reference:** https://historicbostons.org/blog-1/bremer-6y9mb-8982t-7yxx2-ptzwc-s7ws8-f5xpe-zbgsy
- **Result:** ❌ Not applicable to Joan.
- **Details:** This project, led by Kimberly Toney at the John Carter Brown Library, focuses specifically on **pictographic signature marks of Indigenous people** on colonial-era deeds and petitions. It does not cover English colonial women's marks.
- **Note on respect:** This project is doing important work on Indigenous documentary heritage. We note its existence with respect and do not conflate its scope with our search for Joan's mark, even though the Anashuecot hypothesis exists as an open question in our archive. If Joan were connected to the Narragansett sachem lineage, her mark might theoretically be relevant to this project — but that connection is **unproven** and we will not presume it.
- **Classification:** OUT OF SCOPE — but worth monitoring if the Anashuecot hypothesis ever gains primary-source support.

## Why the Mark Matters

Joan's mark on the 19 May 1682 Pawtuxet deed is the **only physical evidence she personally created**. Every other record mentioning her was written by someone else (clerks, scribes, John himself). The mark is:
- The closest thing to Joan's own "voice" in the archive
- Potentially diagnostic: the shape, confidence, and style of the mark could indicate literacy level, cultural context, or comparison with other known marks from the same period
- A concrete visual artifact in a project that has been working almost entirely from text transcriptions

Finding a visual image of this mark is the project's current #1 priority.
