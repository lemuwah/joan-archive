## October 26, 1676 — Two Committees Appointed

### Committee About the Indians (Narragansett Affairs)
> *Voted, the Governor, Mr. Randall Holden, Mr. Caleb Carr, Mr. John Sayles, and the Recorder are chosen a Committee to ripen matters concerninge the Narraganset, and to draw up a letter to Connecticut Collony, and to make their returne to the Assembly to-morrow, at one o'clocke.*

**Note:** Randall Holden later partnered with John Greene as Rhode Island's agent in England. This places the Greene political orbit directly on the Narragansett committee.

### Indian-Disposal Committee
> *Voted, Mr. Joshua Coggeshall, Mr. William Cadman, and Mr. Peter Easton are appointed a Committee to draw up somethinge about the Indians which are come upon Rhode Island (or other Islands in this Bay), of their owne accorde, how they shall be disposed of, and to make their returne to the Assembly to-morrow, at one o'clock.*

---

## October 27, 1676 — Enacted Legislation (Full Text)

> *Voted, and it is enacted by this present Assembly, concerning the Indians that are come on any Island in the Narragansett Bay, of their owne accord, or that shall futurely soe come on, without any authority from this Collony, that in regard of the trouble that is like to be by them, and the danger of the liberty of some and the bondage of others, which is like to make so great a disturbance in this Island and Collony, to those English that have servants, and the neighbour Collonys. Likewise, beinge ready to take notice of those Indians that are soe as aforesaid, under our jurisdiction. Therefore, it is ordered, that all such Indians as aforesaid, that are entertained by any in this Collony, and hath not authority from the Committee appointed to dispose of Indians, or by an order or certificate under two Assistants' hands; or who cannot procure such order or certificate; all such Indians are lyable to be disposed of for the benefit of the Collony, by the Committees appointed to dispose of Indians; or any three Magistrates, may soe dispose of such Indians that hath not sufficient certificate or order as aforesaid. But if any pretend right to entertaine, or keepe any such Indians as aforesaid, contrary to this act, if they will give in sufficient baile to stand to a tryall at a Court of Tryalls, he may have a legall process.*

## What This Means for the Joan Research

### The Authorization Structure
Any English colonist keeping a Native person needed either:
1. Authority from the disposal committee, OR
2. A certificate signed by two Assistants

Without this, the Native person was "lyable to be disposed of for the benefit of the Collony."

### Implications for John Greene
John Greene held an Indian servant named Marcy. Under this legislation, he would have needed a certificate or committee authorization to keep her. **That certificate or authorization record may still exist** in:
- Washington County court records
- North Kingstown town records
- General Assembly records not reproduced by Bartlett

### Implications for Joan
If Joan was a Narragansett woman living in John Greene's household, this legislation would have applied to her unless:
- She was considered his wife (wives were not "entertained" servants)
- She had a certificate from two Assistants
- She arrived before the October 1676 legislation
- She was not classified as an "Indian" by the authorities

**Per Law 1:** All four of these scenarios are held open. The legislation creates a documentary expectation — either Joan was exempt from it (and we need to understand why) or she was subject to it (and there may be a certificate).

## ⚠️ CRITICAL FINDING: "Anochetuckett" in 1687 RI Record

Bartlett's records contain a name rendered as **Anochetuckett** in a 1687 document. This was not located in the 1676–1680 search window but falls within the broader project timeframe.

**Comparison:**
