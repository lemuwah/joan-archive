## Summary

The CT colonial records' Atherton Company deed (1659) adds **five new spelling variants** to the name-cluster from Open Thread #9. The sachem is identified as a son or nephew of Canonicus who conveyed Narragansett lands to the Atherton proprietors.

## Complete Variant List (as of 2026-08-27)

### From the Fones Record / Source Spine:
- Coginiquant
- Cogineaquon
- Cononjonant

### From CT Colonial Records (NEW):
- **Coginaquant** — main text of 1659 Atherton deed
- **Coginaquand** — alternate reading of same deed
- **Cojonoquant** — footnote variant
- **Cachanaquant** — footnote variant
- **Tassarono** — footnote variant (⚠️ radically different phonetic form)
- **Tasconohut** — footnote variant (⚠️ radically different phonetic form)
- **Tesiquant** — footnote variant

### Previously logged as fabrication (Law 4):
- **"Cojonoquond"** — fabricated AI composite merging Coginiquant + Cononjonant. This is NOT the same as the documented **Cojonoquant** variant above. The difference matters: one is a real colonial spelling, the other is an AI hallucination. They differ by one letter (t/d) but by everything in provenance.

## Genealogical Identity

The CT records identify this sachem as a **son or nephew of Canonicus** — the paramount Narragansett sachem. This places him in the sachem lineage, which is the same lineage context as the Fones Record's Anashuecot ("chiefe Sachem and heire properly").

**⚠️ Per Law 1:** This does NOT mean Coginaquant = Anashuecot or that they are related. It means they operate in the same kinship/political structure. The connection is structural, not proven.

## Significance for Open Thread #9

Open Thread #9 asks: read Potter's Atherton Purchase (~pp. 58–60) or Arnold 1894 to resolve the name-cluster. The CT colonial records partially answer this by providing the Atherton Purchase context directly — Coginaquant is the grantor of the 1659 Atherton deed. Potter is still worth reading for additional context.
