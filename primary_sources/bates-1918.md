# Louise Prosser Bates, "John Greene of Newport and Narragansett" (1918)
**Status:** 🟢 VERIFIED (Tier 2 — credentialed secondary, read from the page image) / 🟡 the underlying Jamestown deed itself has not been seen

*Rhode Island Historical Society Collections*, Vol. XI (1918), pp. 69–78, continued Vol. XII (1919). Page images pulled 2026-09-01 from archive.org item `rhodeislandhisto11prov` (leaves n93, n96).

## What Bates actually says — verbatim

**p. 74 — the death evidence:**

> September 25, 1685, Joseph Clark, above, of Newport, sold the land which was bounded south by Caleb Carr's land which was formerly in possession of "John Greene, sr., of Narragansett, now deceased," to Francis Brinley. [Jamestown Records.]
>
> It will be remembered that when John Greene bought the Conanicut land he was called John¹ Greene of Newport, and when the adjacent owner, Joseph Clarke, sold his land, he refers to the same Greene as "John Greene, sr., of Narragansett, deceased."

**p. 77 — the 1682 deeds, the will, the death:**

> In 1682 he seems to have begun to settle up his affairs. On February 4, 1682, Edward Greenman acknowledged, for himself and brother David, the deed he had given Greene to land in Newport in 1647. **Greene and his wife Joan deeded to their sons, Daniel² and James,² land in Quidnisset in return for thirty shillings a year paid by each of them so long as either parent lived. This was March 24, 1681/2.** He left a will, as is shown by the deeds of his son Edward, but it was doubtless destroyed in the North Kingstown fire. **He died before September 25, 1685.**
>
> The children of John¹ Greene and his wife Joan were Lieut. John² Greene of Newport; Henry² Greene of Quidnisset and "New Gearsey"; Daniel² Greene of Quidnisset; James² Greene of Quidnisset; Benjamin² Greene of Quidnisset; Sarah² Greene, who married Thomas Flounders about 1668.

## What this settles and what it does not

- **Settles (at Tier 2):** Bates's source for the pre-1685 death is a Jamestown (Conanicut) land record — Joseph Clark to Francis Brinley, 25 Sept 1685 — not a will. The "1695" date in Austin and later compilers has no support here.
- **Does not settle:** the Jamestown deed itself has not been seen by this archive. Bates is a careful researcher working from the originals, but under the Laws she is a secondary source until the record copy is pulled (Jamestown Town Records / RI State Archives).
- **New contradiction, held open:** Bates writes *"Greene and his wife Joan deeded to their sons."* The Worth 1921 abstract and the NK manuscript signature block show **John alone** signing and acknowledging. Either Bates read Joan's name in the grantor clause of a version we have not fully transcribed, or she paraphrased loosely. See `contradictions/mother-clause-wording.md` §1d.
- **Contradiction with F.L. Greene 1894 (p. 10), held open:** F.L. Greene has "him" signing an address to the King 16 July 1686 and witnessing a deed 13 May 1692, dying c. 1692–96. Bates has him dead by Sept 1685 and reassigns the later acts: Lieut. John² of Newport (alive 1690) and a separate John Greene *alias* Clarke of East Greenwich (b. 6 June 1651, m. Abigail Wardwell) whom F.L. Greene lists as John Sr.'s eldest son "John, Jr." Bates's un-merging is the more careful of the two, but this archive has not itself seen the 1686 address or the 1692 deed.
- Bates's wording for the annuity — *"so long as either parent lived"* — is a third paraphrase alongside F.L. Greene's *"as long as their father or mother should live"* and Worth's *"after his decease to Joane Greene his wife."* None of the three contains "or to her mother if she survive."
