## What We Found

### Deed 1: March 8, 1695 — Edward Greene to George Vaughan
- **Transaction:** 10 acres in East Greenwich
- **Key language reported:** "fell to me by will of my honored father, lately deceased"
- **Significance:** Secondhand evidence that John Sr. left a WILL, and that Edward inherited land through it
- **Source:** Cited via Austin's *Genealogical Dictionary of Rhode Island* p. 86 and Bates

### Deed 2: September 4, 1697 — Edward Greene to George Vaughan
- **Transaction:** 90 acres in East Greenwich
- **Key language reported:** land "given by father"
- **Significance:** Second deed referencing paternal inheritance

### What F.L. Greene and Bates Already Said
- F.L. Greene 1894 p. 10: "He left a will, as is shown by an allusion in a deed by his son Edward"
- Bates 1918: Same statement, similar wording
- Both describe the will as "doubtless destroyed in the North Kingstown fire"

**The Vaughan deed is the specific document they were referring to.**

---

## Why This Matters for Joan

Edward's 1695 deed says "by will of my honored father, lately deceased." The will itself burned. But:

1. **Colonial wills routinely named wives** — if Joan was alive when John made his will, she would likely be named as a beneficiary or executor
2. **The 1682 deed already established a life-estate for "him or his Wife"** — a will would presumably address Joan's ongoing support
3. **"Lately deceased" in 1695** — this could mean John died shortly before March 1695, or it could be loose language for "my late father" (common usage)

⚠️ **The will text itself is LOST.** Edward's deed quotes the inheritance, not Joan. We cannot know whether the will named Joan without finding the will or a probate record.

## Death Date Conflict — THIRD DATE

| Source | Death Date Claim | Identifier Used |
|---|---|---|
| Bates 1918 | Before September 25, 1685 | "John Greene, sr., of Narragansett, now deceased" (Jamestown Records) |
| F.L. Greene 1894 | After July 16, 1686; after May 13, 1692 | "he" (no identifier — Five-John Firewall applied, 1686 = probably Warwick John) |
| Edward²'s 1695 deed | "lately deceased" as of March 8, 1695 | "my honored father" |

### Analysis
- If Bates is right (dead before Sept 1685), then "lately deceased" in 1695 is loose language — 10 years is NOT "lately" by any normal standard
- If "lately" is taken literally, John died closer to 1695 — which conflicts with Bates's 1685 date
- If John died ~1694–1695, then F.L. Greene's 1692 witness deed COULD be our John after all (though the Five-John Firewall still applies)

⚠️ **"Lately deceased" is ambiguous.** In 17th-century legal usage, it can mean "recently" or simply "formerly" / "now dead." It is NOT a precise date claim.

⚠️ **This does NOT resolve C-2. It adds a third data point that could support either Bates (if "lately" is loose) or push back against Bates (if "lately" is literal).**
