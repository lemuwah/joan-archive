## The 1686 "Address to the King" — 🟡 PROBABLE: Warwick John, Not Ours

### What We Found (Web Search, 2026-09-04)
Multiple secondary sources describe a "John Greene" as Rhode Island's agent to England during the 1686 charter crisis (James II's *quo warranto* proceedings). This person is described as:
- Rhode Island's **official colony agent**, sent to England
- Later **Deputy Governor**, 1690–1700
- Associated with the Warwick political lineage, not Quidnessett

This profile strongly suggests **Major John Greene Jr. of Warwick**, the surgeon's son — a politically prominent colonial leader, not an elderly Narragansett-country farmer.

### What We Do NOT Have
- ❌ The 1686 address itself (text, signers, or image)
- ❌ The Andros Instructions text naming which John Greene
- ❌ Any primary source linking this specific address to Major John Greene Jr.
- ❌ Confirmation that the Warwick John and the colony agent are the same person
- ❌ Dates of birth/death for the Warwick John verified from primary sources by this archive

### Assessment
**🟡 PROBABLE but NOT PROVEN.** The weight of secondary evidence points to the Warwick John, but the archive has not read the source document. This is the same evidentiary standard the archive applies to Bates and F.L. Greene — no exceptions for the archive's own research.

**The Five-John Firewall logic is SOUND:** F.L. Greene does not identify which John Greene signed the 1686 address, does not use "Senior" or "of Quidnessett" or "of Narragansett," and the political profile of the colony agent does not match our John. But "probably not our John" ≠ "proven not our John."

## Bates's 1685 Claim — 🟢 LEADING but NOT VERIFIED

Bates cites a specific, checkable document: the September 25, 1685 Clark-to-Brinley deed in the Jamestown Town Records, which describes land formerly of "John Greene, sr., of Narragansett, now deceased."

**Why this is the leading hypothesis:**
1. Specific citation to a named repository ("Jamestown Records")
2. Matching identifier: "John Greene, sr., of Narragansett" aligns with Image 10's "John Green Senr"
3. The competing 1686 claim is probably (but not proven) a different John
4. The competing 1692 claim has no identifying information and follows the same F.L. Greene conflation pattern

**Why it's NOT verified:**
- ❌ The archive has NOT read the Jamestown Town Records directly
- ❌ The archive does not have an image or transcription of the 1685 Clark-to-Brinley deed
- ❌ The Five-John Firewall must be applied to this document too — "John Greene, sr., of Narragansett" could potentially refer to the Surgeon (also "Senior," also "of Narragansett" broadly)
