**Post-arrival Virginia traces:**

- **Daniell Greene (24)** — **NO post-arrival Virginia trace found.**
- **Edward Greene (6, on the *Paul*)** — **NO post-arrival Virginia trace found.**

### The vanishing question

Quidnessett John is in Narragansett Country by **1639** at the latest (his 1679 affidavit: "forty years and more" he lived with Richard Smith, who settled around 1637). 

**George Greene** and **Thomas Greene** are both documented in Virginia **in 1639**. ## 2. The 1682 Deed: Language About "All" Sons

### What survives

The **original will/deed was destroyed in the North Kingstown fire of 1869.**  Surviving references are secondary summaries.

The deed was executed **March 1682** (or 1681/2). The sons who received land are consistently listed as:
- **Edward** — 100 acres in East Greenwich
- **John** — already owned land in East Greenwich
- **Daniel** — 120 acres at Allen's Harbor, Quidnessett
- **James** — 60 acres at Allen's Harbor, Quidnessett
- **Benjamin** — already owned land in East Greenwich  

### The critical language

Contemporary descriptions say John Greene divided land among **"some of his sons who remained in Rhode Island, some of them having gone to New Jersey."** 

That word — **"some"** — is doing heavy lifting. It explicitly does **not** say "all my living sons." It acknowledges that other sons exist who are not included.

**Henry Greene** (born c. 1650, died 1694) was alive in 1682 but was **"late of Aquedneset and now of Gearsey" [New Jersey]** by 1680.  He is the known excluded son.

**Robert Greene** (born 1653) is generally listed as "deceased" in genealogies; no adult record of him exists. He may have been dead by 1682.

**"Heirs of…" clause:** I found **no surviving reference** to an "heirs of [missing son]" clause in the 1682 deed. A 1695–96 deed (James Greene → George Wightman) references transfers "from said Benjamin, passed over and conveyed unto James Greene his brother… bounded in part by Edward Greene his brother,"  but that is a later inter-sibling transaction, not the 1682 instrument.

**Verdict:** The 1682 deed uses language that **explicitly allows for excluded sons.** It does not claim to cover all living children. No "heirs of" catch clause has been located in surviving summaries. The original instrument is lost.

## 4. Quidnessett John's Own Age: Any Primary Source?

**No.** There is **no primary source** in which John Greene of Quidnessett states his age, birth year, or birth place.

The only chronological anchor is his **21 July 1679 affidavit**: "forty years and more, Mr. Richard Smith, that I then lived with, did first begin and make a settlement in the Narragansett…"  Richard Smith sailed for New England in **1637** and settled around that time. "Forty years and more" from 1679 puts John's arrival at **~1639 or earlier.** It does not give a birth year.

The birth year **c. 1606** is **entirely inferred** from the **Matthew passenger list** — and only if "Jo: Greene, age 29" on that ship is the same person. That is the unproven link you are challenging.

**Verdict:** Quidnessett John's age is **unknown from primary sources.** The c. 1606 birth year is derivative inference, not testimony.

## 5. The "Matthew to West Indies" Claim: Primary Source or Inference?

### The actual primary source

Hotten, *Original Lists of Persons of Quality*, p. 81, **21 May 1635**:
> "Jo: Greene" (age 29) "to St Christophers, imbarqued in the Matthew of London…"  

### What the source does NOT say

- It does **not** say this Jo: Greene went to the "West Indies" generically; it says **St. Christophers** (St. Kitts).
- It does **not** say he ever left St. Christophers.
- It does **not** say he went to New England.
- It does **not** say he became John Greene of Quidnessett.

The paintedhills.org and alfredgibbs.com claims that Quidnessett John "immigrated to the New World in 1635 aboard the ship named *Matthew*" and "After a brief period in the West Indies, Greene continued to New England"  are **pure secondary inference** based on name + age matching. There is **no primary source** bridging St. Christophers to Narragansett.

### Your point stands

If the Matthew connection rests on inference (name + age + destination), then Quidnessett John's arrival route is **unproven.** The fact that multiple other John Greenes arrived in 1635 (age 25 on the *Ann & Elizabeth* to Barbados & St. Christophers; age 22 on the *Alexander* to Barbados; age 27 on the *Frances* from Ipswich in 1634)  means the Matthew identification is **one unproven possibility among several.**

The **Merch Bonaventure** (London → Virginia, January 1635) had **Greenes** in the same migration wave. While it had no "John Greene," it documents a **parallel Greene family migration to the colonies in the same year.** If the Matthew claim is admitted to be inference-based, then the Merch Bonaventure route cannot be dismissed as "ruled out" simply because it lacks a John Greene on the passenger list — because the absence of John on that list is no stronger evidence against it than the presence of "Jo: Greene" on the Matthew is evidence for the traditional narrative. Both require inferential leaps to connect to Quidnessett John.

**Verdict:** You are correct. The Matthew claim is **secondary inference masquerading as fact.** The primary source only shows a "Jo: Greene, 29" went to St. Christophers. Everything beyond that is unproven.
