Here is a prioritized, no-fluff action plan. I have sorted it by probability of yielding a decisive answer and feasibility.
Tier 1: Do These First (High Yield, Actually Possible)
1. Find the pre-fire transcription of the 1682 deed
The original burned in 1869, but 19th-century Rhode Island genealogists routinely copied deed texts verbatim. You need:
Sidney S. Rider's manuscript collections at the Rhode Island Historical Society (Rider had access to pre-fire North Kingstown records and published extensively on Greene). Search his unpublished notes.
James N. Arnold's transcripts — Arnold copied vast numbers of pre-fire Rhode Island deeds for his Vital Record series. Check RIHS manuscript holdings.
The Greene Family Association papers — if any survive in private hands or at the New England Historic Genealogical Society (NEHGS), they may contain 19th-century transcripts of the 1682 instrument.
What to look for: The exact phrase "some of his sons" vs. "all my sons" vs. "heirs of" — and whether any daughter or grandson is named as an heir, which would signal excluded children.
2. Y-DNA: Target the Virginia Greene line directly
You need a documented male-line descendant of George Greene (20) or Daniell Greene (24) from the Merch Bonaventure. The path:
Step A: Identify the Virginia patentee who imported George Greene as a headright: Thomas Mathews, Chirurgion, Henrico County, 11 May 1639 (1,100 acres).  Trace Mathews's land chain forward. Did George Greene remain as a tenant, indentured servant, or freeholder? Henrico County deeds and court orders 1640–1650 may name him.
Step B: Search Henrico County Deeds & Wills, 1637–1650 (Library of Virginia, microfilm). If George Greene acquired or sold land, or appeared as a witness, you have a locational anchor.
Step C: If you can trace George Greene to a county and approximate death date, search that county's probate and deed records for a generation forward. Even one confirmed son → grandson → modern descendant gives you a Y-DNA target.
Step D: Post on FTDNA Greene Project forums and WikiTree explicitly requesting a documented descendant of George or Daniell Greene (Merch Bonaventure, 1635) for Y-700 testing. Offer to sponsor the test if necessary.
Reality check: If George and Daniell were indentured servants (likely, given their ages and the headright system), they may have died without trace, or their descendants may be unidentifiable. But you cannot know until you look.
3. St. Christophers records for "Jo: Greene, 29" (Matthew, 1635)
If the traditional narrative claims he went to St. Christophers and then New England, there should be a record of him leaving St. Christophers or appearing in New England afterward. Search:
The Calendar of State Papers, Colonial Series, America and West Indies, 1574–1660 (UK National Archives, CO 1/series). Look for St. Christophers musters, militia lists, or shipping returns 1635–1640.
The St. Christophers census/musters of 1671 (published in Caribbeana) — too late, but may show Greene families that could be traced back.
Barbados records — the Alexander (2 May 1635) carried another "John Greene, 22, to ye Barbadoes."  If the traditional narrative conflates multiple John Greenes, Barbados records may reveal a different candidate.
What you are testing: Whether any "Jo: Greene" appears in Caribbean records after 1635. If none do, the Matthew-to-Quidnessett route becomes even weaker.
Tier 2: Harder But Worth It
4. Quidnessett John's age: Undiscovered depositions
You have the 1679 affidavit. Look for earlier depositions:
Rhode Island Court Records, 1647–1670 (published in Rhode Island Colonial Records, vols. 1–2). Search every Greene mention for depositions, witness statements, or jury service where age might be stated.
Massachusetts Bay or Plymouth court records, 1635–1645 — if John Greene passed through Massachusetts before Narragansett, he may appear in a suit, land transaction, or servant contract. The Great Migration series (Anderson) is exhaustive for 1630–1635 but may miss someone who arrived 1635–1637.
Narragansett proprietary records, 1637–1645 — the "Narragansett Country" was disputed between Massachusetts, Rhode Island, and private patentees. Early land petitions or boundary disputes may name John Greene with an age descriptor.
5. The "other" John Greenes of 1635
You have at least four distinct John Greenes in the 1634–1635 shipping lists:
Jo: Greene, 29, Matthew → St. Christophers (21 May 1635)
John Greene, 25, Ann & Elizabeth → Barbadoes & St. Christophers (27 April 1635)
John Greene, 22, Alexander → Barbadoes (2 May 1635)
John Greene, 27, Frances from Ipswich (1634) 
Action: Treat these as separate hypotheses. Search Barbados and St. Christophers records for each. If any of them can be traced to a death or marriage in the Caribbean, they are eliminated. If none can be traced forward, then the "Matthew John Greene" identification with Quidnessett John rests on nothing but name and age — which is exactly what you suspect.
6. The Bowridge Hill pedigree: Stress-test it
The traditional English origin claims John Greene of Quidnessett was baptized 9 August 1606 at St. Thomas, Salisbury, son of John and Joan (Tattershall) Greene of Bowridge Hill, Gillingham, Dorset. 
What to verify:
Obtain the actual Salisbury parish register image (Wiltshire & Swindon History Centre). Do not trust transcriptions. Is the entry genuinely "John Greene" or "John Green"? Is the date 9 August 1606 or a misreading?
Check whether any other John Greene was baptized in Wiltshire/Dorset/Somerset between 1600–1610 who could fit the emigrant profile.
Search Dorset Quarter Sessions and Exchequer Depositions for the Greene family of Gillingham — did they have a son who "went to sea" or "went to the plantations"?
Tier 3: Probably Lost Causes (But Mentioned for Completeness)
The original 1682 deed — gone in the 1869 fire. Unless Rider or Arnold copied it, it is irrecoverable.
The Merch Bonaventure passenger list — Hotten is the only source. No ship's log or manifest survives in the Admiralty records (most were destroyed or never kept for merchant voyages). You cannot get ages or relationships beyond what Hotten published.
Virginia musters before 1624/5 — the 1624/5 muster is the earliest comprehensive census. For 1635–1650, you are dependent on fragmentary county records, headright patents, and occasional court references. A servant who died or moved before acquiring land may leave no trace.
The Single Most Important Question
Can you find a documented male-line descendant of George Greene (20) or Daniell Greene (24) and get them Y-700 tested?
If their haplogroup is R-BY186325, the Merch Bonaventure hypothesis becomes a blood-connection possibility. If it is anything else, the hypothesis is falsified for those two individuals. If no descendant can be found, the hypothesis remains untestable — which is itself a finding.
Everything else — deed language, Caribbean records, English parish registers — is circumstantial. Y-DNA is the only potential dispositive evidence here.
