# Known Limitations

**Status:** Active  
**Last updated:** 2026-09-06  
**Purpose:** Honest accounting of what this archive cannot do, has not done, and should not be trusted to do.

---

## 1. No Primary Sources Read by Human Eyes

As of September 2026, **no primary source document in this archive has been read by a trained paleographer.** All manuscript readings are AI transcriptions (Claude, Migoo, Kimi) working from low-to-medium resolution FamilySearch scans. AI transcription of 17th-century secretary hand is unreliable — every proper noun, every number, every legal term is a lead to verify, not a confirmed reading.

The archive tags AI-read content as 🟢 PROOF — AI TRANSCRIPTION, which means: "the original image exists and an AI read it." It does NOT mean a human confirmed the reading.

## 2. Fire-Damaged Records Gap

The 1850 North Kingstown records fire destroyed original wills, probate records, and early town records. Key documents affected:
- John Greene's will (referenced in Edward²'s 1695 deed but original burned)
- Henry Tibbitts's will (1708, proved 1713 — only secondary abstracts survive)
- Early NK vital records

This means some questions about Joan may be permanently unanswerable from the NK record set.

## 3. Secondary Source Dependency

Many claims in the archive's people pages come from web search results citing secondary compilations (Austin, Beaman, Tanner, RI Genealogical Register). These are research leads, not evidence. The Pawtuxet citation taught us that secondary sources can cite pages that contain completely different content. The archive's hostile review process catches these — but only after they've been committed.

## 4. Five-John Problem

At least five men named "John Greene" operated in 17th-century Rhode Island simultaneously. The archive's Five-John Firewall protocol attempts to disambiguate, but any unqualified "John Greene" reference in a source is inherently ambiguous. When sources disagree about which John Greene is meant (as with Sarah Flounders's father), the archive holds the contradiction open rather than choosing.

## 5. Colonial Lens

All primary sources in this archive were created within the English colonial legal system. They reflect English naming conventions, English property law, English record-keeping priorities, and English perspectives on Indigenous peoples. Narragansett naming practices, kinship structures, land tenure concepts, and governance systems are poorly represented or entirely absent from these records. The archive attempts to apply a multi-lens view (see `methodology/agent_orientation.md`) but the source material itself is structurally biased.

## 6. Single Researcher

This archive is maintained by one independent researcher with no institutional affiliation, no funding, and no formal training in paleography, early American history, or Narragansett studies. The AI-assisted methodology compensates for some limitations but introduces others (see §1 above and `contamination/` folder).

## 7. Scope of Indigenous Content

All Indigenous cultural, genealogical, and historical content represented in this archive is included for research purposes only, within the context of understanding Joan's identity and the documented interactions between English settlers and Narragansett people in 17th-century Rhode Island. This content will be revised or removed if requested by Narragansett tribal representatives or scholars.
