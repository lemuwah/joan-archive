## 1. Documentary Facts (verified from public sources)

### Smith-Greene Relationship — Stronger Than "Neighbors"
- John Greene testified he was **living with Richard Smith** when Smith established his Narragansett trading house
- Greene witnessed the Native princes' confirmation of Smith's land
- The 1664 Smith letter calls Greene his **"fast friend"** and complains authorities forcibly removed Greene from his Aquidnessett home
- **Status:** PLAUSIBLE — cited from Narragansett Historical Register and RIHS Collections. Primary source not personally verified.
- **Significance:** The relationship is Smith household → Greene household → Native land network, not merely geographic proximity

### Smith Jr. Slaveholding — Documented
- Richard Smith Jr.'s 1692 probate inventory records enslaved adults and children
- Smith Jr. Barbados commerce documented (fitted out ship, 1671)
- Smith Jr. personally trafficking enslaved people: **strong hypothesis, shipping-level proof still needed**
- **Status:** PLAUSIBLE — cited from Smith's Castle research. Probate inventory not personally verified.

### Smith Sr. "Servants" — Source Word Matters
- Roger Williams' 1679 description uses the word **"servants"**
- Smith Castle's scholarly argument that some may have been enslaved is **interpretation, not primary source fact**
- Smith Sr.'s will contains no concrete evidence of slaveholding
- **Status:** The word is "servants." Cannot silently rewrite as "enslaved" without surrounding document establishing that.

### John Greene's Documentary Fingerprint
- Settler / landholder / political actor / Native-English intermediary
- NOT merchant / trader / Atlantic commerce
- This asymmetry with Smith is real and sharp
- **Significance:** John's relationship with Smith tells us more about his access to the Native-English corridor than about slavery

### Joan Smith Firewall — Confirmed Separate Person
- Richard Smith Sr.'s will identifies daughter Joan, deceased wife of Thomas Newton
- Joan/Johan Smith lived at the same location (Cocumscussoc) as Joan Greene
- Any future record saying "Joan of Cocumscussoc" cannot be automatically attached to Greene's wife
- **Status:** PLAUSIBLE — cited from Narragansett Historical Register. Will not personally verified.

### Multiple Incompatible Joan Biographies
- Caskey Family site: Joan Beggarly, English birthplace, marriage date, parents
- Ullrich-Birchler: completely different supposed English origin
- Alfred Gibbs: Joan Beggarly died 1643/44
- All three are **mutually incompatible**
- **Significance:** Confirms the blank space has been filled repeatedly with certainty the primary record doesn't supply. Archive's refusal to choose without evidence is justified.

## 2. Methodology Validation

### Five-Route Reframe
Stop testing five ancestries. Start testing **five routes into the English legal record**:
1. Birth/kinship
2. Marriage
3. Indenture
4. Captivity/enslavement
5. Intermediary household/network

Then ask: **Which route best explains why this particular woman appears suddenly in 1682, already married to John Greene, with no surviving origin record?**

This is a better question than "what ethnicity was Joan."

### Three-Test Results
| Test | What shook out |
|------|---------------|
| Women's network | Real Smith/Greene women, but **no Joan Greene bridge** |
| Servant/captive pathway | Real mechanism at Cocumscussoc; **no Joan identification** |
| Marriage/prior marriage | **Still open**, with possible early household chronology |

All three returned "mechanism real, Joan link absent" — which is exactly what honest application of the Laws should produce.

### Structural Insight
**Joan disappears precisely at the point where an ordinary genealogical search expects a woman to acquire a surname.**

The relational experiment matters because we should ask:
- Who disappears?
- Who changes households?
- Who becomes a wife?
- Who stops being described by status and starts being described by relationship?
- Who is geographically present but textually absent?

## 3. Reverse Reconstruction Findings

### John's Biography Has the Same Structural Problem as Joan's
- The Narragansett Historical Register literally says "nothing is certainly known" about John's pre-1639 origin
- The later pedigree connecting John to Gillingham, Dorset is **tradition, not established fact**
- **Using John's supposed English ancestry to default Joan to English is circular reasoning**
- The two blanks touch each other — you can't use one unknown to resolve the other

### English-Colonial Household Trail ≠ English-Family Trail
- John entered the documentary record through Smith's trading house, not through an English parish
- The network that produced his household is Newport/Narragansett/Native commercial
- The later pedigree is tradition, not established fact
- **The surviving documentary trail is an English-colonial household trail, not an English-family trail**
- The assumption that everyone in that network is ethnically English is not supported by the records themselves

### Beggarly Not Required
- Remove the Beggarly surname and nothing in John's documentary network demands a particular origin for Joan
- The Beggarly connection is itself unsupported by contemporary evidence

### Network People Worth Mapping
- 1647 Greenman land purchase — documented John-to-family edge
- 1651 Caleb Carr transaction — witnesses (John Hicks, Providence Williams, Thomas May)
- John's early network is a mobile Newport/Narragansett group, not "two men alone in the wilderness"
- "John Greene and Son" receive 151 acres in 1666 — son unnamed. That unnamed son is itself a five-lens test case
