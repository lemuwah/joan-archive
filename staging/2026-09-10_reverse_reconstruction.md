## The Key Distinction

**The surviving documentary trail for John and Joan Greene is an English-colonial *household* trail, not an English-family trail.**

These aren't the same thing.

### What we have:
- John Greene entering the record through Richard Smith's trading house
- A network of Newport/Narragansett/Native commercial relationships
- Land transactions, jurisdictional disputes, political testimony
- A household that appears in English colonial paperwork

### What we don't have:
- An English parish record connecting John to a specific family
- Any birth, baptism, or marriage record for Joan in England or anywhere else
- Any record establishing Joan's ethnic origin, social status, or prior identity

### Why this matters:
The assumption that everyone in an English-colonial household network must be ethnically English is not supported by the records themselves. The records describe **roles, transactions, and relationships** — not origins.

John's earliest documented environment is a mixed commercial/diplomatic/land network. The later pedigree connecting him to Gillingham, Dorset is genealogical tradition, not documented fact.

**And John's own biography has the same structural problem as Joan's.** The Narragansett Historical Register says "nothing is certainly known" about John before his appearance in Smith's household. Using one unknown (John's supposed English origin) to resolve another unknown (Joan's origin) is circular reasoning.
