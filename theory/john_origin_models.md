## Why This Matters for Joan

John's origin defines the pool Joan came from. If John is Irish-origin, Joan may be Irish. If John is a servant, Joan's entry into colonial records shifts. Every assumption about Joan being English (Model B) rests on the assumption that John is English — **and that assumption is unproven.**

The Y-DNA says Irish Sea cluster (R-Z255 / R-FTB79899 / R-BY186325). His pre-Rhode Island life is a complete blank. No English parish record. No ship. No baptism. No documented arrival.

---

## The Models

### Model I — English Settler

**Status:** PLAUSIBLE — the received assumption. No primary source support.

**Basis:** Standard genealogical default. Everyone assumed he was English because the colony was English. No baptism, no parish, no ship manifest confirms this.

**What would confirm:** An English parish register with "John Greene" baptized ~1606, Bowridge Hill, Gillingham, Dorset (the traditional claim from La Mance-derived compilations). Or any English document placing him before his arrival in Narragansett Country.

**What would eliminate:** Confirmed non-English origin via records or DNA analysis of close matches.

---

### Model II — Irish Origin

**Status:** PLAUSIBLE — supported by Y-DNA.

**Basis:** Y-DNA haplogroup R-Z255 places John's patrilineal line in the Irish Sea cluster (Ui Dunlainge / O'Byrne / O'Toole modal). This is patrilineal only — it traces John's male line, not Joan's. But if John is Irish-origin, his social network likely included other Irish arrivals, and Irish communities in colonial New England were small, tight, and intermarried.

**What would confirm:** Finding John Greene / Grene / Greine in Irish records 1600-1640. Or finding documented Irish arrivals in his Narragansett network.

**What would eliminate:** DNA analysis showing R-Z255 assignment was in error, or finding the English parish baptism.

**Search targets:**
- Pre-Cromwellian Irish in New England 1630-1645
- "Joan" as anglicization of Siobhan — naming pattern research
- Bristol embarkation lists — female servants to New England 1640-1660

---

### Model III — Dutch-Connected

**Status:** PLAUSIBLE — circumstantial.

**Basis:** Narragansett Bay had active Dutch trading ties before English dominance. "Greene" / "Groen" is a common Dutch surname. If John arrived via Dutch trade routes, his paper trail origin shifts to New Amsterdam records. Richard Smith's trading post at Cocumscussoc had Dutch trading contacts (Gysbert Opdyck, Arnoldus van Hardenberg documented in the archive).

**What would confirm:** Finding "Groen" / "Greene" in New Netherland arrival records 1630s-1640s. Or documented Dutch trading post records mentioning John.

**What would eliminate:** No Dutch records exist for this name in the relevant period after thorough search.

**Search targets:**
- New Netherland Project (published translations)
- Dutch colonial records online — any Groen/Greene
- Dutch-English crossover settlers in RI

---

### Model IV — Servant / Indentured to Richard Smith

**Status:** PLAUSIBLE — linguistic evidence.

**Basis:** "Lived with Richard Smith" (1679 affidavit) is servant language as much as partner language. If John arrived as Smith's servant or indentured laborer:
- He had no independent paper trail before his term ended
- He wouldn't appear in freeman lists until his indenture was up
- His marriage to Joan might have happened AFTER his service
- Two people of low social status marrying explains why NEITHER has a pre-Quidnessett paper trail

**What would confirm:** Finding John Greene listed as servant, apprentice, or bound laborer in Providence/Warwick court records. Or finding a gap between his arrival (~1639) and his first freeman appearance that corresponds to an indenture term.

**What would eliminate:** Finding independent land ownership or freeman status before ~1647 (suggesting he was never a servant).

**Search targets:**
- Indenture records for Richard Smith's household
- Freeman lists — when does John first appear independently?
- Atherton Company membership lists — is John named as investor or just Smith's man?

---

### Model V — Hybrid (Irish + Servant)

**Status:** PLAUSIBLE — combines Models II and IV.

**Basis:** Irish laborers moved through English and Dutch ports to New England as workforce. If John was an Irish-origin laborer who arrived at Smith's trading post as labor, he's not a settler choosing RI for religious freedom — he's workforce brought to a trading post. That puts Joan in a completely different pool than "English wife of an English settler."

**What would confirm:** Any combination of Model II + Model IV evidence.

**What would eliminate:** Confirming English free-settler status (Model I proven).

## The Backward Search Logic

Instead of starting from Joan and searching forward, start from John's documented world and search backward:

1. Who was around him at Cocumscussoc/Smith's trading post?
2. Where did THEY come from?
3. Can we find Joan in that incoming stream?

John's Quidnessett neighbors in the 1670s-1680s were likely his neighbors in the 1650s-1660s too. Colonial settlements were sticky. If we identify who was around John and trace THEM backward, we find the community Joan entered.
