## Purpose

No primary source confirms Joan's ancestry. The archive maintains two active shadow models to account for the incomplete and biased nature of the colonial record. These models are not conclusions; they are structured hypotheses held open until evidence supports or falsifies them.

The archive does not collapse uncertainty into narrative. It pursues evidence that can differentiate between models.

---

## Shadow Model A: English Origin

**Basis in the record:**
- "Joan" is an English name. She appears in English legal instruments, in an English household, married to an English-named man.
- The surname "Beggarly" is a documented English surname appearing in English parish records (see `contamination/beggarly_surname_note.md`).
- No colonial record identifies Joan as Indigenous or places her within a Native kinship network.
- No English parish record has yet been found for a Joan Beggarly, but the absence of a match is common for 17th-century women and does not indicate Indigenous ancestry.
- English is the default identity assumption for a woman named "Joan" in a colonial English household (not a finding; a prior).

**What it explains:**
- Joan's name and legal context fit English colonial norms.
- The 1682 deed language ("dureing her naturall life") is standard English dower provision.

**Anomalies it must explain (honest count, post-corrections: 1–2):**
1. Joan's documentary silence — unique among Quidnessett wives, unusual even controlling for fire/poverty/isolation (see Cycle #5, H15).
2. Her outlier status in the neighbor-wife comparison set (4 of 6 comparable wives have documented maiden names; Joan does not).

These may be ONE observation or TWO depending on whether the comparison-set outlier status and the absolute silence are counted together or separately.

**What would resolve it:**
- Finding any English Joan who matches (colony, dates, marriage to a John Greene)
- Finding that other frontier wives have similarly thin records (expanded comparison set — GAP-O)
- Documenting that the 1869 NK fire destroyed a generation of wives' records

**Evidence status:** No positive evidence for English origin beyond the surname's existence and the default prior. No contradictory evidence.  
**Model status:** ⚪ UNVERIFIED.

## Shadow Model B: Indigenous or Mixed Ancestry

**Basis in the record:**

### Strong (survives Corrections #1 and #2)
- **Joan's 40-year documentary silence (H15).** She appears in exactly ONE verified legal document (March 1682 home-place deed) across 40+ years of marriage. This is unique among Quidnessett wives and is the strongest surviving structural indicator. See `agents/Explorer/2026-09-02_migoo_cycle5.md`.
- **The neighbor-wife comparison set.** Ann Smith, Mary Gardiner, Elizabeth Helme, and Penelope Stanton all have more extensive documentation including maiden names. Joan is the outlier. Methodology is clean and testable.
- **The Independent John hypothesis.** No colonial document links John Greene of Quidnessett to Surgeon John Greene of Warwick as kin. The Pawtuxet connection (the one thread tying Quidnessett John to Warwick) is now SUSPENDED per Correction #1. This strengthens the possibility that John himself was operating outside normal English kinship networks.
- **Trading post context.** Cocumscussoc (Richard Smith's trading post) was a documented site of English–Indigenous interaction, including intermarriage. John Greene settled there c. 1637 and lived in Narragansett country for 40+ years.

### Moderate (contextual, not direct)
- **Weunquesh 1679.** Proves female Indigenous land authority mechanism existed in the region. Contextual parallel, not a direct connection to Joan.
- **Greene's documented pro-Indigenous actions (1676/1679).** Including the court martial and his refusal to submit to Rhode Island authority over his Quidnessett land until guaranteed he could keep it.
- **Pre-war succession gap.** Scuttup's 1668 death opened a sachem succession gap in Quidnessett 4 years before the 1672 Fones Purchase.
- **Narragansett matrilineal culture.** Property and identity passed through the mother's line (Tomaquag Museum, Prof. Brooks). This is a cultural context, not evidence about Joan.
- **Colonial Indigenous erasure patterns.** The absence of Indigenous women from colonial records is structural and documented (Tomaquag, Brooks).

### ⚠️ SUSPENDED (post-corrections)
- ~~**"Or to her mother if she survive" matrilineal clause.**~~ This wording is NOT in any source the archive holds. F.L. Greene says "their father or mother" (= the sons' parents). Worth says "after his decease to Joane Greene, his wife." NK manuscript says "dureing her naturall life." See `theory/open-contradictions/mother-clause-wording.md`. **The matrilineal clause was the archive's strongest individual piece of evidence for Model B. Its suspension is a significant loss for this model.**
- ~~**Joan's second legal appearance (Pawtuxet deed).**~~ Citation killed. See `contamination/vol_vii_citation_kill.md`.
- ~~**Joan's mark as possible Indigenous pictographic signature.**~~ Mark not yet visually located. See GAP-A.

### Speculative (held open, not promoted)
- Awassuocitt phonetic hypothesis (low confidence)
- 1659 Awashouse-Greene co-occurrence (data point only, not evidence of relationship)
- Anaftiawin = Anashuecot phonetic chain (data preserved, chain deleted from promotion)

**What it explains:**
- Why Joan has no English records (she wasn't in the English record system)
- Why she is an outlier among Quidnessett wives (different cultural background)
- Why "nothing further is known about her" (she existed in Narragansett kinship systems, not English legal systems)

**What it requires but lacks:**
- Any positive documentary link between Joan and any Indigenous individual or community
- Joan's mark showing an Indigenous pictographic pattern
- A bridging document connecting the 1672 Anashuecot to the 1682 Joan
- DNA results connecting the Greene line to Indigenous ancestry

**Evidence status:** Contains legitimate structural indicators but no direct primary source connecting Joan to any Narragansett individual or community. The single-appearance analysis (H15) is the strongest lead and requires expansion (GAP-O).  
**Model status:** ⚪ UNVERIFIED.

## Model C: Joan = "Joan Beggarly" (Alice Daniels) — ELIMINATED

La Mance (1904) conflated multiple women into "Joan Beggarly." Alice Daniels Beggerly died c. 1643/44. Joan of Quidnessett is alive in 1682. **A dead woman cannot receive a life-estate.** Timeline impossible. La Mance contamination chain fully documented.

See `agents/Archivist/2026-09-02_lamance_disentanglement_proof_steps.md`.

**Model status:** ❌ ELIMINATED. Rock solid.

## The Honest Assessment (Post-Corrections, Cycle #5)

Model B remains more **parsimonious** than Model A: it explains Joan's entire documentary pattern with one premise (she was Indigenous). Model A requires Joan to be uniquely undocumented among her peers, which may have a mundane explanation but has not been demonstrated.

**However:** Model B is now a **negative case**, not a positive case. It works by ruling out alternatives, not by proving its own claim. Before the corrections, the "mother clause" and the second legal appearance gave Model B positive evidence. Both are now suspended. What remains is structural and circumstantial.

This is legitimate scholarship — plenty of historical cases are built on absence evidence. But it is inherently provisional. A single document could overturn it.
