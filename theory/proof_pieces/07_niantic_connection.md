## The Question

The Niantics were neutral through King Philip's War. Narragansett survivors merged with them afterward. Does the Niantic/Ninigret kin network connect to the 1672 Fones Purchase signatories? And if so, does that explain how Joan — and her mother — could be alive in 1682?

### The Critical Gaps

- Ninigret's **mother's name** — unknown. She was Canonicus's sister but English clerks never recorded her name.
- Ninigret's **second wife's name** — unknown. If she was Miantonomi's sister, that's another matrilineal bridge, but it's conjectural.
- **At least four unnamed daughters** — colonial records preserve Weunquesh and Ninigret II. The other children are invisible.
- Ninigret's **known aliases**: Janemo, Ninicroft, Wanaconchat, Anquawas — none of these phonetically match the 1672 signatories.

## Part 3: The Structural Connection — The Quaiapen Bridge

The phonetic comparison is negative, but the **structural** connection is strong and documented:

```
CANONICUS
├── Mixanno (eldest son)
│     └── married QUAIAPEN (Ninigret's sister)
│           ├── SCUTTUP — "chiefe Sagamore" at Quidnessett (earlier Fones Record)
│           └── QUEQUAQUENUIT — same title, same area
│
CANONICUS's sister (unnamed)
     └── NINIGRET (Niantic sachem)
           ├── QUAIAPEN (sister) → connects back to Mixanno line above
           ├── Weunquesh (daughter) — female sachem 1677–1686
           ├── Ninigret II (son)
           └── 4+ unnamed daughters
```

**Scuttup and Quequaquenuit are Ninigret's nephews** (through his sister Quaiapen). They held sachem authority at Quidnessett. They appear in earlier Fones Record instruments for the same area where Anashuecot signs in 1672.

The connection runs: **Ninigret → Quaiapen → Scuttup/Quequaquenuit → [GAP] → Anashuecot**

## Part 4: Why Ninigret Stayed Neutral — And Why It Matters

Ninigret's neutrality was **strategic, not accidental:**

1. **Opposed Philip's project.** Philip's Wampanoags had killed Niantic men during recruitment. Ninigret judged the English had superior resources.
2. **No Mohawk support.** Without access to Albany's gun trade and Mohawk retreat routes, an Indian coalition couldn't win. Ninigret understood this.
3. **Preservation.** As an older sachem, he prioritized survival of Niantic community, his heirs, and his authority.
4. **Strategic exposure.** Niantics occupied territory near Pequot country — unnecessary war was especially dangerous.
5. **English relationship as shield.** Emphasized loyalty to King Charles II and colonial commissioners. Cooperated just enough to avoid attack.

### The Refuge Function

After the Narragansett defeat, **surviving Narragansetts took refuge with Ninigret's people.** (Boissevain; Fisher & Silverman.) The record doesn't identify specific individuals, but the populations merged into what became the combined Narragansett-Niantic community.

**This is the mechanism by which Narragansett sachem-line women could survive the war without appearing in English records.**

Ninigret's neutrality created a **sanctuary zone.** If Joan's mother was a Narragansett woman — connected to the Quidnessett sachem network through the Quaiapen bridge — she could have survived the war by sheltering with the Niantics. She would appear in no English record. She would be alive in 1682.

## Part 5: The Missing Names Problem

Here is what the colonial record **did not preserve** about Ninigret's family:

- His mother's name (Canonicus's sister)
- His second wife's name (possibly Miantonomi's sister)
- His third wife's name
- The names of at least four of his daughters
- The names of Scuttup's wife/wives
- The names of Quequaquenuit's wife/wives
- The names of any female relatives who survived the war

**This is the same pattern as Joan's erasure.** The colonial record systematically failed to preserve Indigenous women's names — especially women connected to the sachem network through matrilineal lines. Joan's "invisibility" is not unique; it's the NORM for Indigenous women in English colonial records.

The question is not "why is Joan invisible?" The question is: **"Why would an English woman be AS invisible as an Indigenous woman?"**

## Part 6: What This Piece Proves and Doesn't Prove

### PROVES:
- Ninigret's neutrality created a sanctuary zone for Narragansett survivors
- Scuttup and Quequaquenuit (earlier Quidnessett sachems) are Ninigret's nephews through Quaiapen
- The Niantic-Narragansett merger is documented — survivors absorbed into one community
- Multiple Ninigret-family women's names are unrecorded — systematic erasure of Indigenous women from English records
- Ninigret's territory did not directly border Quidnessett, but his kinship network reached it through Quaiapen's line

### DOES NOT PROVE:
- Any phonetic connection between 1672 signatories and Niantic family names (tested: **negative**)
- Any direct genealogical link between Anashuecot and Ninigret
- That Joan's mother specifically sheltered with the Niantics
- That Joan = Anashuecot

### OPENS:
- **The Quaiapen bridge is the key structural connection.** If Anashuecot's kin group succeeded or branched from the Scuttup/Quequaquenuit generation, then the entire 1672 deed is connected to Ninigret through Quaiapen.
- **The unnamed women** in Ninigret's family represent exactly the kind of person "her mother" could be — a sachem-line woman, alive, undocumented by English clerks.
- **Ninigret's sanctuary zone** explains how such a woman could survive the war and be alive in 1682.

## The Structural Argument (Not Phonetic)

The connection is not through **sound** but through **structure:**

1. Quaiapen married into the Canonicus line
2. Her sons (Scuttup, Quequaquenuit) held Quidnessett sachem authority
3. Anashuecot held Quidnessett sachem authority in the next documented generation
4. Ninigret (Quaiapen's brother) stayed neutral and created a sanctuary zone
5. Narragansett survivors merged with Niantics
6. Joan appears in 1682 with zero English paper trail and a matrilineal inheritance clause
7. "Her mother" could be any of the unnamed women in this kin network who survived the war

The chain has a gap (Scuttup/Quequaquenuit → Anashuecot), but every other link is documented.
