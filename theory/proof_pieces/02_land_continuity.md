## The Question

Does the land Joan signed for in 1682 sit on the same land Anashuecot's kin group conveyed in 1672? If so, the land continuity supports Model A (same family, same land). If not, the connection weakens.

---

## ⚠️ CRITICAL FINDING: The Parcels Are Not Identical

Research reveals a **more complex** geographic picture than previously assumed. The three key documents reference **different but geographically related** parcels:

### The Three Parcels

**1. The 151-Acre "John Greene & Son" Lot (1666 origin)**
- Source: 1666 Northern Quidnessett division (Atherton-associated proprietary)
- Appears on the 1717/18 Quidnessett plat as "John Green & Son 151 Ac"
- Bounded on north, east, and south by highways
- The 1717/18 plat was actually **copied from the earlier 1666 plat** — it maps the original allotment, not new surveying

**2. The 1672 Fones Purchase Tract**
- Source: Fones Record p.90 — deed from Anashuecot's kin group to six English purchasers
- Much larger regional tract with natural boundaries: road from John Andrew's house toward New London, Devil's Foot Rock, Pasquatuck/Pasutat river and meadow, Mascachaug Cove, Potowomut River
- Placed generally **north and west of the post road from Devil's Foot Rock to Hunt's River**
- The 151-acre lot may fall within or adjacent to this broader area, but its metes and bounds **do not match** the Fones Purchase boundaries

**3. The 1682 Homeplace (180 acres, Allen's Harbor)**
- Source: 1682 Greene deed — 120 acres to Daniel, 60 to James
- Located **south of the southern highway** bounding the 151-acre lot, between that highway and Allen's Harbor
- This land had originally been laid out to **John Sanford in 1666** and was later acquired by John Greene

## What This Means for Each Model

### Model A — Joan = Anashuecot

**Previous assumption:** Joan signed for 1672 Fones Purchase land = same family, same land, direct continuity.

**Revised picture:** The homeplace land was originally **Sanford's 1666 allotment**, not the 1672 Fones tract. The documentary chain is: Sanford allotment (1666) → acquisition by John Greene (date unknown) → conveyances to Daniel and James (1681/82).

**This does NOT eliminate Model A, but it changes the argument.** The land continuity claim must now be reframed:
- The 1672 Fones Purchase shows John Greene's **relationship with Anashuecot's kin group** — that relationship is the evidence, not the specific parcel boundaries
- Greene acquired Sanford's adjacent land separately — possibly through his Narragansett connections, possibly through normal English purchase
- The 1682 homeplace deed shows Joan signing for **land Greene acquired through English channels** (the Sanford allotment), not directly for Fones Purchase land
- BUT: Greene held BOTH the 151-acre lot AND the homeplace AND was first-named in the Fones Purchase — he was accumulating land in one area from both Indigenous and English sources

**Remaining strength:** The geographic concentration of all three parcels in one area, plus Greene's unique position as both Fones purchaser and Sanford-land acquirer, is still unusual and consistent with someone embedded in both English and Indigenous land networks.

### Model B — Joan = Unknown English Woman

**Strengthened by this finding.** If the homeplace came through the Sanford allotment (an English channel), Joan's signature on the 1682 deed is a standard dower release on English-origin land — no Indigenous connection required.

**Remaining weakness:** Model B still must explain:
- Why Joan has zero English paper trail
- Why Greene's relationship with Narragansett sachems persisted across multiple decades
- Why Greene was first-named purchaser in a deed with an Indigenous sachem's kin group
- The survival anomaly (Proof Piece #3)

## The 1651 Prohibition — Legal Context for the Fones Purchase

Rhode Island General Assembly, 1651:

> *"[N]o purchase shall be made of any Land of the natives for a plantation without the consent of this State, except it bee for the clearinge of the Indians from some particular planatations already sett down upon; and if any shall so purchase, they shall forfeit the Land so purchased to the Collonie . . . ."*

- 1658: Fine of £20 added
- 1672: Colony **authorized** acquisition of Narragansett lands — same year as the Fones Purchase
- 1727: Penalty increased to £100
- 1759: Prohibition repealed

**Significance:** The 1672 Fones Purchase was made in the same year the colony authorized Narragansett land acquisition. Whether specific colonial consent was granted for this particular purchase requires further archival research into the 1672 General Assembly records.

## Rocky David Farm "Small Farms" Claim — CHALLENGED

The claim that "the six purchasers were only allowed to keep small farms within it as a compromise" is **unsupported and probably misleading.**

- A 1663 Fones Record provision (predating the 1672 purchase) planned **22 farms of 500 acres each** for twenty-two proprietors — these are substantial holdings, not small farms
- The 1672 purchase was confirmed in 1677 with provisos, by which time the proprietors numbered twenty-four
- The "small farms" claim may conflate later parcelization with original terms

⚠️ **Per Law 4:** Rocky David Farm is a secondary source. Their deed transcript may be accurate even if their editorial framing is not. Use the transcript; flag the interpretation.
