## The Purpose of This Piece

This is not a proof piece in the traditional sense. No new documentary evidence is introduced. Instead, it asks: **when we lay our findings alongside what Narragansett scholars and Tomaquag Museum have publicly stated about their own history, what patterns align and what contradict?**

We are not claiming Tomaquag endorsement. We are checking whether the evidence we've gathered is *consistent with* the patterns that Narragansett scholars have documented independently.

## Part 1: What Tomaquag Has Published

### On Erasure

Lorén Spears, Tomaquag Executive Director:

> *"400 years of conquest colonization, genocide, land dispossession, enslavement, forced assimilation, detribalization, and erasure of the Narragansett and other Indigenous nations of the Americas."*

> *As a child, she was shocked to read in school that she, her family, and her community "supposedly didn't exist."*

Source: URI Feature (2022); POC Foundation

### On Women's Roles

Tomaquag's *Rematriation* essay:
- Narragansett community explicitly described as **matrilineal**
- Women's leadership emphasized: "mothers, grandmothers, and ancestors as carriers of knowledge, language, traditional ecological knowledge, healing, and cultural continuity"
- Female sachems called **Saunksquâog** in Narragansett/Niantic

Tomaquag's *Reenactments* blog:
- Documents **Esther Ninigret** as Saunks of the combined Narragansett and Niantic peoples (1770)
- Notes that "very little was written about female sachems because colonial documents were recorded mainly through a patriarchal lens"
- Notes that Esther's "traditional names have not yet been recovered"

Source: tomaquagmuseum.org/belongingsblog

### On How the Colonial Record Works

The Narragansett Tribe's official history states their history is preserved through oral tradition and cultural continuity that "**transcends all written documentaries**" — colonial deeds are not accepted as the complete account of land rights.

Source: narragansettindiannation.org/history

### On Academic Methodology

Rubertone's published work:
- ***Recovering Gendered Political Histories*** — directly addresses women's political roles in colonial Southern New England
- ***Native Providence*** — traces how Indigenous identity was categorized and erased in official records (censuses, directories, estate inventories)
- ***Grave Undertakings*** — reconstructs Narragansett history through archaeology and documentary sources, including women like Quaiapen

## Part 3: The Alignment Check

### What Tomaquag's Published Perspective Confirms About Our Findings

1. **"Very little was written about female sachems because colonial documents were recorded mainly through a patriarchal lens."**
   - Joan's erasure pattern matches this exactly. She is invisible in a system that systematically erased Indigenous women.
   - Her neighbor wives (English women) ARE documented. Joan is not.

2. **Narragansett culture is matrilineal.**
   - The "or to her mother if she survive" clause makes sense under matrilineal inheritance.
   - It makes NO sense under English common law.
   - John Greene, a man with 40+ years of English legal experience, inserted a clause that aligns with Narragansett custom, not English custom.

3. **"400 years of … erasure."**
   - Joan's case may be a specific, documented instance of the pattern Lorén Spears describes.
   - The erasure is not just absence — it's the overwriting of an Indigenous identity with an English name ("Joan") in English legal forms.

4. **Esther Ninigret's "traditional names have not yet been recovered."**
   - This confirms that even later, better-documented Indigenous women lost their original names in colonial records.
   - If Esther Ninigret's traditional names are lost in the 18th century, Joan's Indigenous name being lost in the 17th century is not surprising.

5. **The Tribe's history "transcends all written documentaries."**
   - The Joan Archive's entire methodology — reading colonial records for what they hide, not just what they say — is consistent with how Indigenous scholars approach these sources.
   - The archive does not claim colonial records are the complete story. It documents what they contain and what they conspicuously do not contain.

### What Tomaquag's Published Perspective Does NOT Confirm

1. **No Tomaquag or Narragansett publication mentions Joan Greene, the Fones Purchase by name, or Anashuecot.**
   - Our research has not been validated or reviewed by Narragansett scholars.
   - Alignment with published patterns is not endorsement.

2. **No published Narragansett source confirms a specific case of an Indigenous woman recorded under an English name in a colonial deed.**
   - The mechanism is implied (Esther Ninigret's lost names, Spears's erasure statements) but not documented as a specific deed-level case study.

3. **The matrilineal inheritance pattern is described in general terms, not with specific legal rules for land succession.**
   - We cannot claim the "or to her mother" clause follows a specific Narragansett legal formula. We can only say it aligns with the general matrilineal principle.

## Part 4: What Do We See?

Laying the evidence across all seven proof pieces against Tomaquag's published context, the picture is:

**Joan is exactly the kind of person Lorén Spears is describing when she talks about erasure.**

She is:
- A woman with no English paper trail in a community where English women HAVE paper trails
- Named only in English legal documents, with an English name, using English legal forms
- Connected to land that a Narragansett sachem kin group conveyed 10 years before she signs
- Protected by a clause ("or to her mother") that aligns with matrilineal inheritance, not English law
- Married to a man whose homestead uniquely survived in a zone of total destruction
- Appearing in the record precisely during the post-war window when Indigenous identity was most dangerous and most legally necessary to encode

She is also:
- Not proven to be Narragansett or Indigenous by any single document
- Not endorsed or identified by any Narragansett scholar or tribal authority
- Unconnected to Anashuecot by any direct documentary evidence

**The archive does not claim Joan is Narragansett. It documents what the colonial record says and doesn't say, and notes that the pattern of silence around Joan matches the pattern of erasure that Narragansett scholars have independently described.**

## Part 5: What a Hostile Reviewer Must Address

A reviewer who wants to sustain Model B (Joan = unknown English woman) must now explain all of the following simultaneously:

1. Why Joan is the ONLY Quidnessett wife with zero English documentation
2. Why "or to her mother if she survive" exists in a deed when English law gives the mother no claim
3. Why John Greene's homestead survived King Philip's War when every neighbor's was destroyed
4. Why Joan appears in 1682 and never before or after
5. Why no English Joan matching this profile exists in any of four colonies
6. Why Alice Daniels (the only proposed alternative) married a different John Greene and died 38 years before Joan signs
7. Why the 1672 deed involves a single sachem kin group conveying hereditary land at the same location

Model A (Joan was a Narragansett woman) explains all seven with one premise.

Model B requires seven independent special explanations.
