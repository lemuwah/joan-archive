# Proof Piece #3 — The Survival Anomaly
## John Greene's Quidnessett Homestead During King Philip's War (1675–1676)

**Status:** ⚪ UNVERIFIED — circumstantially strong, no direct physical evidence
**Models tested:** A (Indigenous kin protection), B (standard English survival)
**Laws applied:** Law 1 (no narrative smoothing), Law 3 (no early exclusion)

---

## The Question

The Narragansett Bay mainland was devastated during King Philip's War. Nearly every English homestead in the Wickford/Quidnessett corridor was attacked or destroyed in late 1675 through spring 1676. John Greene's homestead is never documented as burned, attacked, or rebuilt. Why?

---

## Neighbor Comparison — Direct Evidence

### Destroyed / Attacked

- **Jireh Bull's garrison** (Tower Hill, south of Wickford): Burned December 15, 1675. ~15–17 defenders killed. Source: *Narragansett Historical Register*, Vol 1–2.
- **Richard Smith's Cocumscussoc / "Smith's Castle"**: Used as military staging base for the Great Swamp Fight. Burned by Narragansett forces in 1676 after colonial troops withdrew. ~40 soldiers buried on site. Rebuilt 1678. Source: Smith's Castle National Register documentation; *Narragansett Historical Register*.
- **Robert Westcott** (Warwick): Killed during the war. March 16, 1676 attack burned nearly every house in Warwick. Surviving family fled to Prudence Island. Source: *Narragansett Historical Register*.
- **Joseph Gardiner**: Killed in the Great Swamp Fight (December 19, 1675). Source: *Narragansett Historical Register*.
- **Wickford settlement**: Remaining structures destroyed ~March 19, 1676. Source: *Narragansett Historical Register*.
- **Pettaquamscutt settlement**: Attacked and destroyed December 1675, shortly before the Great Swamp expedition.

### Greene's Homestead — No Destruction Record Found

- No petition by John Greene for rebuilding or war damage relief has been identified in indexed colonial records.
- No named destruction of Greene's Quidnessett homestead appears in surviving records.
- No explicit statement of survival appears either.
- Greene signed/joined a 1676 petition to the English Crown, but it concerned political security and land rights — not rebuilding relief.
- John Greene, Daniel Greene, and James Greene all signed the July 29, 1679 petition of Narragansett inhabitants — a resettlement petition, not a damage claim.

## The Legal Inference Chain

⚠️ *The following reasoning is inferred from legal and documentary behavior, not from direct physical evidence. Flagged per Law 4.*

### Claim: Greene's house survived the war.

**Evidence supporting this inference:**

1. **No rebuilding petition.** Greene petitioned for his neighbors' relief but never for his own rebuilding. Every other documented family in the corridor that lost property either petitioned, relocated, or is documented as displaced.

2. **Continuous residence claim under oath.** Greene's July 21, 1679 affidavit states he had lived in Narragansett Country "forty years and more" (i.e., from ~1639). This was sworn testimony in a contested legal proceeding where four competing jurisdictions (RI, CT, MA, Crown/Atherton Company) were all looking for reasons to invalidate Narragansett land claims.

3. **Hostile courts would have challenged a gap.** The 1679 affidavit supported Richard Smith Jr.'s land title. If Greene had abandoned his homestead during the war — even temporarily — any of the four competing jurisdictions could have challenged the affidavit. No such challenge appears in surviving records.

4. **Land transfer to Daniel (March 1681/82).** Greene conveyed 120 acres bordering Allen's Harbor to his son Daniel, with annual payment of 30 shillings for the life of John or Joan Greene. He made a comparable transfer of 60 adjoining acres to son James. This is a family settlement / life-estate arrangement — you transfer recognizable parcels you continuously hold, not land you abandoned and reclaimed.

5. **Four jurisdictions would have seized abandoned land.** Rhode Island, Connecticut, Massachusetts Bay, and the Crown (via the Atherton Company) were all competing for control of Narragansett Country. Any vacancy in the Greene homestead would have been an opportunity to claim the parcel as abandoned. No such seizure attempt is documented.

### Evidence AGAINST this inference:

1. **No direct statement of survival.** No source explicitly says the house stood through the war.

2. **"Forty years" may mean long-term association, not uninterrupted occupancy.** The affidavit establishes Greene's presence in the region from ~1639. Independent records place him at Quidnessett in 1664, 1671, 1672, 1674, 1679, and 1681–82. But the war years (1675–1676) are a documentary gap.

3. **Greene may have temporarily withdrawn.** Other English families survived by evacuating to Aquidneck Island or sheltering in fortified garrisons, then returning. Greene could have done this and still claimed long-term residence.

4. **Greene was NOT garrisoned at Smith's Castle.** No source identifies Greene as a garrison soldier or occupant of Smith's Castle during the war. His association with Smith was historical (from ~1639), not military. This means the Smith's Castle burning does not directly implicate Greene's house either way — but it also means he wasn't sheltering there when it burned.

## Model Assessment

### Model A — Indigenous Kin Protection

**Argument:** Joan's Narragansett kin connections protected the Greene homestead during the war. Narragansett forces burned English homesteads selectively — families with Indigenous ties were spared.

**Strength:** Explains the survival anomaly with the fewest assumptions. If Joan was Narragansett (= Anashuecot), the kin network provides a specific mechanism for protection.

**Weakness:** No direct evidence of selective burning based on kin ties. This is an inference from the pattern, not a documented decision.

### Model B — Standard English Survival

**Argument:** Greene survived through the same mechanisms as other English settlers — evacuation, garrisoning, luck, or strategic positioning.

**Strength:** Other English families survived without documented Indigenous family ties (though all had Indigenous trade/diplomatic associations).

**Weakness:** Model B must explain why Greene's homestead was spared in a zone of near-total destruction, AND why he claimed continuous residence under oath in hostile courts, AND why he never petitioned for rebuilding, AND why no competing jurisdiction challenged his residency claim. Each explanation is individually plausible; together they accumulate.

**Cost of Model B:** Higher than Model A. Model B requires multiple independent explanations (lucky + evacuated but came back quickly + no one noticed the gap + house happened to survive + courts didn't challenge). Model A requires one: kin protection.
