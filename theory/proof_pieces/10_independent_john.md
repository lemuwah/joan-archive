# Proof Piece #10 — The Independent John Hypothesis
## Was John Greene of Quidnessett EVER Connected to Surgeon John Greene of Warwick?

**Status:** 🟡 STRESS-TESTED — no kinship document found; DNA divergent; will silent; Bates silent
**Models tested:** X (two branches of one family), Y (two unrelated men sharing a common surname)
**Laws applied:** Law 1 (No Narrative Smoothing), Law 2 (No Early Exclusion)
**Date:** 2026-08-31

---

## The Question

Every compiled genealogy treats Surgeon John Greene of Warwick and John Greene of Quidnessett as related — usually as brothers, or uncle/nephew, or father/son from the same English Greene family. **But does any colonial-era document actually say this?**

The Kimi analysis flagged the 1682 Pawtuxet deed (Joan consents to sale of "inherited" Pawtuxet land) as potentially connecting the two lines. The DNA project suggests they diverge. "Greene" was as common as "Smith" in colonial New England.

**This proof piece tests whether the connection was ever real, or whether it was constructed by later genealogists.**

---

## Part 1: Surgeon John's 1658 Will — What It Says

Dated: **December 28, 1658.** Proved: **January 7, 1659.**

Source: *History of Warwick* (Fuller); *Greenes of Rhode Island* (Archive.org)

### Named Heirs:

| Heir | Relationship | Bequest |
|---|---|---|
| **Philippa Greene** | Wife | Not detailed in available extracts |
| **John Greene** | Son | "That neck of land called Occupasnetuxet" — purchased from Miantonomi, Oct 1, 1642 |
| **Peter Greene** | Son | Named heir |
| **James Greene** | Son | Named heir |
| **Thomas Greene** | Son | Named heir |
| **Mary Sweet** | Daughter | Named heir |
| **Ann Hade** | Granddaughter | Daughter of Joan (deceased daughter) |

### What the Will Does NOT Say:

- ❌ Does NOT mention any John at **Quidnessett**
- ❌ Does NOT mention any John at **Cocumscussoc**
- ❌ Does NOT mention any person in **Narragansett Country**
- ❌ Does NOT convey **Pawtuxet** land (Occupasnetuxet is a different property)
- ❌ Does NOT name a brother named John
- ❌ Does NOT use kinship terms ("my brother," "my cousin," "my nephew") for any John outside his immediate children

### What This Means:

If Quidnessett John were Surgeon John's **brother**, the will's silence is unusual but not impossible — brothers don't always appear in wills. If he were Surgeon John's **son**, his absence from the will is **devastating** — Surgeon John named ALL his sons.

**The son hypothesis is effectively eliminated.** If Quidnessett John were Surgeon John's son, he would appear in the 1658 will alongside John, Peter, James, Thomas, and Mary.

## Part 2: The Two Johns' Origin Trails — Side by Side

| Feature | Surgeon John (Warwick) | John of Quidnessett |
|---|---|---|
| **Earliest RI record** | 1637 Providence; original proprietor | 1647 Newport deed (22 acres at Greenend) |
| **Arrival context** | With Roger Williams; later with Gorton to Warwick | 1679 affidavit: lived with Richard Smith c.1637–39 |
| **Political circle** | Williams → Gorton → Warwick founders | Smith's Narragansett trading post → Newport → Quidnessett |
| **Freeman status** | Warwick freeman, multiple offices | Newport freeman 1655; Acquidnesset oath 1671 |
| **Wife/wives** | Joane Tattershall (m. 1619, d. ~1635); Alice Daniels/Beggarly (m. 1638, d. ~1643/44); Philippa (m. ~1645) | Joan (maiden name unknown; appears only 1682) |
| **Death** | 1658/59, will proved Jan 7, 1659 | Between March 1682 and Sept 1685 |
| **Land base** | Warwick/Occupasnetuxet/Shawomet | Quidnessett/Narragansett Country |
| **Y-DNA** | English Greene modal (per DNA project) | R-Z255 Irish Sea cluster (Kit B2778) — DIVERGENT |
| **Children named** | John, Peter, James, Thomas, Mary, Joan (d.), Ann (granddaughter) | Lt. John², Henry², Daniel², James², Benjamin², Sarah² |

### Key Observations:

1. **Different arrival contexts.** Surgeon John came through Williams/Gorton. Quidnessett John came through Richard Smith's trading post. These are different social networks.
2. **Different political trajectories.** Surgeon John was a Warwick founder and officeholder. Quidnessett John held no colonial office — first documented civic act is the 1671 oath.
3. **Different wife documentation levels.** Surgeon John's three wives are all documented with names and approximate dates. Joan has NOTHING.
4. **Different death documentation.** Surgeon John has a dated, proved will naming all heirs. Quidnessett John has no surviving will (destroyed in fire) and death dated only by inference.
5. **Y-DNA DIVERGES.** Kit B2778 (Quidnessett descendant) is R-Z255 Irish Sea cluster. Surgeon John's documented descendants cluster in a different haplogroup (per Greene DNA Project). These are **not the same patriline** unless a non-paternity event occurred in the 340-year chain.

## Part 3: What Bates Says and Doesn't Say

Louise Prosser Bates (RIHS Collections XI–XII, 1918–19) is the most thorough published researcher on the Quidnessett John.

**Bates identifies:**
- John Greene of Newport, Narragansett, and Quidnessett as ONE person
- His wife as Joan
- His sons as Lt. John², Henry², Daniel², James², Benjamin²
- His daughter Sarah² (m. Thomas Flounders)

**Bates does NOT state:**
- Any blood relationship to Surgeon John Greene
- Any shared English ancestor
- Any kinship term connecting the two Johns

**The silence of the most thorough researcher is itself evidence.** Bates was working from the same colonial records we have. If there were a kinship document, she would have cited it.

## Part 4: The Pawtuxet Inheritance Problem (Retested)

### The 1682 Pawtuxet deed says John sold "inherited" Pawtuxet land.

### IF the two Johns are unrelated:

Where did Quidnessett John inherit Pawtuxet land?

**Possibilities:**
1. **From a non-Greene relative** — through his own family (unknown) or through a prior marriage
2. **Through Joan's family** — but the deed names John as grantor, not Joan
3. **Through the Pawtuxet proprietorship** — a share acquired independently
4. **The deed's use of "inherited" may be misread or paraphrased** — the actual deed text has never been located; the cited "Vol. VII pp. 177–178" is invalid (1773 Gaspee records) — see proof piece #9
5. **He DID inherit from Surgeon John's estate** — as a brother or cousin, even though the will doesn't name him (brothers inherit independently of wills; the will only conveys specific bequests)

### The critical distinction:

Even if Quidnessett John inherited Pawtuxet land from the Warwick Greene family, that could mean:
- (a) They're brothers from England who arrived separately (different ships, different sponsors, different networks)
- (b) They're distant cousins sharing a common English ancestor
- (c) The "inheritance" is through a non-Greene channel entirely

**The Pawtuxet inheritance does NOT require them to be father/son.** The will's silence eliminates son. It doesn't eliminate brother, cousin, or unrelated.

## Part 5: The "Independent John" Model

### What if John of Quidnessett was always independent?

**The narrative would be:**

~1637–39: A man named John Greene arrives in Narragansett Country. He's not from the Williams/Gorton group. He's associated with Richard Smith's trading post. "Greene" is a common English surname — like Smith.

1647: He appears in Newport with a small land purchase.

1651: He buys 100 acres at Quidnessett from Caleb Carr. Establishes his homestead.

1663–64: Richard Smith refers to "John Greene, sr." at his house in Quidnessett. The "sr." distinguishes him from his own son, not from another John.

1671: Takes the oath of loyalty at Acquidnesset with sons Henry and Daniel. No colonial office.

1672: First-named purchaser in the Fones Purchase — buying Narragansett sachem land from Anashuecot's kin group.

1675–76: King Philip's War. His homestead survives. His son Daniel is court-martialed.

1679: Sworn affidavit about 40+ years' continuous residence. Conservator of the Peace.

1682: One verified deed — the homeplace partition (Joan named). A claimed Pawtuxet sale (Joan consents) is SUSPENDED — citation invalid, text not located.

By 1685: Dead. Will destroyed in fire.

**This narrative requires NO connection to Surgeon John.** Every documented event is independently sourced.

## Part 6: Hostile Review of the Independent John Model

### Attacks:

**1. The surname coincidence.** Two men named "John Greene" in Rhode Island in the same decade is NOT unusual. Greene was common. The colonies had multiple John Smiths, John Browns, John Williamses. The surname match proves nothing about kinship.

**2. The Pawtuxet problem remains.** "Inherited" land in the Warwick corridor is the strongest evidence OF a connection. If they're truly unrelated, the inheritance needs an alternative explanation. This hasn't been provided yet.

**3. The DNA divergence is the strongest evidence AGAINST kinship** — but Y-DNA only traces the patriline. Non-paternity events, adoption, maternal-line connections could all produce divergent Y-DNA in biological relatives.

**4. Bates's silence is evidence but not proof.** Bates may not have investigated the question. Her scope was the Quidnessett John specifically, not a kinship comparison.

**5. The will's silence on kinship is strong but not dispositive.** Brothers and cousins routinely don't appear in each other's wills. The will eliminates "son" but not "brother."

### What survives hostile review:

- ✅ No colonial document links them as kin — VERIFIED
- ✅ Different arrival contexts (Williams vs. Smith) — VERIFIED
- ✅ Different political networks — VERIFIED
- ✅ Y-DNA diverges — VERIFIED (with non-paternity caveat)
- ✅ Bates silent on kinship — VERIFIED
- ✅ Will does not name Quidnessett John — VERIFIED
- ⚠️ Pawtuxet inheritance unexplained under independent model
- ⚠️ "Greene" as pseudonym is speculative without evidence

## Part 7: The "Greene as Pseudonym" Sub-Hypothesis

**Your question:** What if "Greene" isn't even his real English surname? What if it's adopted, like "Smith"?

**Evidence for:** 
- Common practice in colonial New England for servants, transported persons, and those reinventing themselves
- John of Quidnessett has ZERO pre-Rhode Island documentation — no English parish, no ship manifest, no baptism
- Y-DNA diverges from English Greene families
- His association is with Richard Smith's trading post — an interracial frontier economy where English identities were fluid

**Evidence against:**
- His sons use "Greene" consistently across decades
- His 1679 affidavit is sworn under that name
- No document questions his identity
- "Greene" is too common to need adopting — it wouldn't provide cover the way an unusual pseudonym might

**Hostile review verdict:** SPECULATIVE. Interesting but untestable without finding his actual English origin. Held open per Law 2.

## Part 8: Impact on Joan

### If Quidnessett John was always independent:

**Model A (Joan = Narragansett woman) gets STRONGER:**
- No English family connection means no English wife network to draw from
- His entire adult life was spent in Narragansett Country, associated with Smith's trading post
- Smith's trading post was an interracial frontier — intermarriage documented in this context
- Joan's invisibility is explained: she has no English family because HE has no documented English family
- The "or to her mother" clause: no English mother-in-law to protect, but a Narragansett mother with matrilineal land rights — ⚠️ WORDING UNVERIFIED — not in F.L. Greene 1894 or the Worth 1921 abstract; see contradictions/mother-clause-wording.md

**Model B (Joan = unknown English woman) gets WEAKER:**
- If John has no documented English family, where does an English wife come from?
- Who introduces them? Who witnesses the marriage? Who knows her maiden name?
- The total erasure of BOTH John's English origins AND Joan's English origins stretches coincidence

### The Pawtuxet exception:
- If the Pawtuxet "inheritance" is verified as coming from Surgeon John's estate, then SOME kinship exists — but it could be distant (cousin, step-relation) and wouldn't require Joan to be English

## Part 10: Current State of Knowledge

### PROVEN:
- They are separate men (different places, times, wives, children)
- Surgeon John's will does not mention Quidnessett John
- No colonial document states their kinship
- Y-DNA diverges (R-Z255 Irish Sea vs. English Greene modal)
- Bates is silent on kinship
- Greene was a common surname

### NOT PROVEN:
- That they are unrelated (absence of evidence ≠ evidence of absence)
- That "Greene" was adopted/pseudonymous
- The source of Quidnessett John's Pawtuxet inheritance
- Quidnessett John's English origin

### THE GAP:
The Pawtuxet inheritance is the ONE piece of evidence that requires some connection to the Warwick corridor. Until the verbatim deed text is read, we can't assess whether "inherited" is the actual word or a paraphrase.
