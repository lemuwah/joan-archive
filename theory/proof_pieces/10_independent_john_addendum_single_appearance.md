# Proof Piece #10 Addendum — The Single-Appearance Problem
## If Joan Has Only ONE Documented Appearance, What Does That Mean?

**Date:** 2026-08-31
**Context:** Pawtuxet deed (claimed May 1682) SUSPENDED pending verbatim verification. If it doesn't exist, Joan has ONE documented appearance: the March 24, 1681/82 home-place deed.

---

## The Question

F.L. Greene (1894) wrote: *"His wife, in 1682, was named Joan, but nothing further is known about her."*

If the Pawtuxet deed is a conflation, Greene was working from ONE deed. Bates (1918–19) may also have been working from ONE deed. Every subsequent researcher who says Joan appears "twice" in 1682 may be repeating a conflation error.

---

## What a Single Appearance Means for Each Model

### Model A (Joan = Narragansett woman)

**Gets STRONGER.** Here's why:

An English colonial wife — especially the wife of a man who was a 40-year resident, Conservator of the Peace, Fones Purchase signatory, and politically active — should appear in records **beyond** land transfers. Every comparable Quidnessett wife appears in at least ONE of: marriage records, baptismal records, court witness lists, family Bible entries, probate, church membership.

Joan appears in ZERO of these. Not one.

If she also has only ONE land transfer appearance (not two), her documentary footprint is:
- **ONE deed.** March 1682. Named as wife and as beneficiary of the 30s annuity after John's death. ~~Signs with mark.~~ (Worth abstract: John alone signs — Joan as signatory UNVERIFIED.) ~~Named in the "or to her mother" clause.~~ ⚠️ WORDING UNVERIFIED — not in F.L. Greene 1894 or the Worth 1921 abstract; see contradictions/mother-clause-wording.md
- **Nothing else. Ever. In any record system. Anywhere.**

That is the footprint of someone who **barely exists** in the English record system. Not someone who was temporarily underdocumented — someone who was fundamentally outside it.

### Model B (Joan = unknown English woman)

**Gets WEAKER.** The single-appearance problem compounds every existing anomaly:

1. **No English woman married to a prominent colonist for 20+ years leaves only ONE trace.** Even the least-documented Quidnessett wives (Esther Smith, Margaret Fones) appear in wills or family records beyond land transfers.

2. **The dower-consent mechanism was standard** — but the FACT that Joan needed to consent proves she was recognized as legally married. A recognized marriage between two English people should generate at least a marriage record, especially for a man who used English legal systems fluently.

3. **If there's no Pawtuxet deed**, there's no evidence Joan had any connection to the Warwick/Providence corridor at all. Her entire documented existence is one signature on one Quidnessett document.

4. **The "or to her mother" clause is now even more anomalous.** (⚠️ WORDING UNVERIFIED — not in F.L. Greene 1894 or the Worth 1921 abstract; see contradictions/mother-clause-wording.md — this paragraph stands only if the wording is confirmed.) In Joan's ONLY documented appearance, John Greene inserted a clause with no English legal parallel. This wasn't a pattern across multiple deeds — it was a one-time insertion in the single document where Joan exists. Why use this one opportunity to protect a matrilineal inheritance interest?

## The Core Argument

**A single deed appearance with zero supporting documentation is more consistent with a woman who entered the English record system through marriage but did not originate within it.**

The colonial record captured Joan once — when English law required her consent to a property transfer. It never captured her again because there was nothing else TO capture: no English family, no English parish, no English marriage, no English church.

She existed in the record system exactly as much as English law demanded and no more.

That is the pattern of an Indigenous woman with an English name, not an English woman with an unusual level of underdocumentation.

---

## What This Does NOT Prove

- Does not prove Joan was Narragansett
- Does not prove Joan = Anashuecot
- Does not prove the Pawtuxet deed doesn't exist (it may be in undigitized sources)
- Does not prove John of Quidnessett was unrelated to Surgeon John

**What it proves:** Joan's documentary footprint is more anomalous than any comparable colonist's wife in the Quidnessett area. The anomaly deepens if she had only one appearance rather than two.
