## Angle 1: "Joan Beggarly" — ELIMINATED (Model C Closed)

### The Settled Chain

The "Beggarly" identification is fully resolved. It refers to a **different woman married to a different John Greene:**

1. **Alice Daniels** married **Richard Beggarly** in England
2. Richard Beggarly refused to come to the New World
3. Alice came alone — Winthrop recorded this December 13/14, 1638, calling her scandalous: "the wife of one Beggarly" who "married one Greene"
4. The "Greene" she married was **Surgeon John Greene of Warwick** — NOT John Greene of Quidnessett
5. Alice Daniels Beggarly died **~January 1643/44**
6. Joan signs both 1682 deeds — **38 years after Alice's death**

**Timeline impossible. Model C is closed.**

### The Contamination Chain

- **Winthrop Journal (1638)** — records "the wife of one Beggarly" marrying "one Greene." This is Alice Daniels marrying Surgeon John of Warwick.
- **Bartlett (1856)** — footnote in *Records of the Colony of RI & PP* Vol I, p.16 references "John Greene of Providence married Beggerly." This describes the Warwick surgeon.
- **La Mance (1904)** — misattributes the Beggarly surname to the **Quidnessett** John Greene's wife. Cites no sources. Confuses the two Johns.
- **Secondary genealogies (1904–2026)** — Ancestry, FamilySearch, WikiTree, alfredgibbs.com all inherit the La Mance error. Over 10,000 family tree entries trace back to this single uncited 1904 claim.

### What the Benedict Arnold Records Confirm

Benedict Arnold's records and the Winthrop Papers together establish:
- Alice Daniels = wife of Surgeon John Greene of Warwick
- Surgeon John also had a first wife, Joan Tattershall (married Salisbury 1619, died ~1635, never in Rhode Island)
- Neither Alice nor Joan Tattershall is the Joan who signs in 1682

### The Two-John Firewall

The confusion between two John Greenes — each married to a woman named Joan — is a **recognized problem** in the literature (noted explicitly by alfredgibbs.com and multiple genealogical forums). The archive's Three-John Framework resolves this:

- **Surgeon John Greene of Warwick** — married (1) Joan Tattershall 1619, (2) Alice Daniels Beggarly 1638
- **John Greene of Quidnessett** — married Joan [unknown maiden name], who signs in 1682
- These are **different men in different towns with different families**

⚠️ **Per Law 2:** La Mance (1904) is the contamination origin. Everything downstream that says "Joan Beggarly" for the Quidnessett John traces through La Mance with no independent source.

## Angle 2: Any Other Woman Named Joan in Colonial Records (1640–1680)

### Search Scope
Rhode Island, Connecticut, Massachusetts Bay, Plymouth Colony records for any woman named Joan who:
- Disappeared from records before 1682
- Was unmarried or widowed
- Could have married John Greene of Quidnessett

### Results

- **Joan Sullivan** — Indentured to Capt. John Turner ~1668; legal action 1681, Salem MA. **No connection to Greene.**
- **Joan Hyde** — Married Ralph Hyde, Portsmouth RI, 15 Nov 1679. **Married to someone else.**

**No other qualifying Joan found.** No unmarried or widowed woman named Joan disappeared from any searched colony's records at the right time to marry John Greene.

## Angle 3: Servant / Indenture Records

### Search Scope
New England servant and indenture records for any woman named Joan transported to the colonies.

### Results

**Joan Sullivan** (Salem, MA) is the only named Joan in a servant/indenture context. No connection to Greene, Smith's trading post, or any Narragansett Country figure.

⚠️ **Hypothesis note:** An indentured woman would have had no dower rights to extinguish. Joan's joinder on both 1682 deeds would be legally irrelevant if she were a current or former servant.

## Angle 6: "Anashuecot" in Roger Williams's Works

### Search Scope
Does the name "Anashuecot" appear anywhere outside the Fones Record?

### Results

**"Anashuecot" does not appear** in:
- *A Key into the Language of America* (1643) — Project Gutenberg, EEBO, Archive.org editions
- Published Williams correspondence (NHPRC catalog)
- Any other digitized colonial source outside the Fones Record

Williams's *Key* contains Narragansett vocabulary with forms beginning with *Ana-/Anas-* (e.g., *Anaskhómmin* = "to hoe or break up"), but nothing matching "Anashuecot."

### What This Means

The name exists **only in the Fones Record context.** Four explanations remain open (per Law 3):
1. Personal name not captured in Williams's selective vocabulary
2. Title or status designation, not a personal name
3. Variant spelling that doesn't match digitized transcriptions
4. Localized form from the Quidnessett area (Williams worked primarily with Canonicus's lineage at Narragansett Bay proper)

The absence from Williams does NOT prove the name is spurious — his vocabulary documented common words, not personal names.
