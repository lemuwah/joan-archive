## Angle 1: Joan Beggarly / Beggerly — The Traditional Identification

### What the La Mance Sieve Says

Per Law 2, the "Joan Beggarly" surname traces through La Mance (1904) with no independent 17th-century source confirming that Joan's maiden name was Beggarly. This has been tagged **Tier 4 — INVALIDATED** in the archive.

### What the Winthrop Papers Actually Show

The Winthrop Papers consolidated index (Massachusetts Historical Society) lists:
- **"Beggarly (Beckley), Alice"**
- **"Beggarly (Beckley), Richard"**
- **"Beggarly (Beggerly), Mrs. ——"**

The editors treat "Beggarly" and "Beggerly" as variant spellings of the same surname, cross-referenced with **"Beckley."**

### ⚠️ UNRESOLVED: Which John Greene?

The Winthrop Papers Vol. I, p. 16 footnote **could not be retrieved verbatim** from digitized sources. The key question remains: does the footnote identify "Mrs. Beggarly" as the wife of **Surgeon John Greene of Warwick** or **John Greene of Quidnessett**?

**Known contamination chain:**
- La Mance (1904) attributes Joan Beggarly to Quidnessett John — no source cited
- Alice Beggarly is documented as the wife of **Surgeon John Greene** (married Dec 1638, died Jan 1643/44)
- The alfredgibbs.com article explicitly notes that earlier genealogies confused the two Johns
- The confusion between two John Greenes, each married to a Joan, is a **recognized problem** in the literature

**Status:** The Beggarly identification is **not disproven** but remains unverified. The Winthrop footnote text is the critical document and has not been directly examined.

## Angle 3: Servant / Indenture Records

### Search Scope
New England servant and indenture records for any woman named Joan transported to the colonies.

### Results

**Joan Sullivan** (Salem, MA) is the only named Joan in a servant/indenture context. No connection to John Greene, Richard Smith's trading post, or any Narragansett Country figure.

**No other qualifying Joan found in indenture records.**

⚠️ **Note on Hypothesis C (Servant/Indentured Woman):** This was previously eliminated in the archive because indentured women had no dower rights to extinguish. Joan's joinder on both 1682 deeds would be legally irrelevant if she were a current or former servant. The absence of any matching indenture record further weakens this hypothesis.

## Angle 4: Dutch New Amsterdam / New Netherland Records

### Rationale
John Greene was associated with Richard Smith's trading post at Cocumscussoc, which had Dutch commercial connections. Could Joan have come through Dutch channels?

### Results

**No.** The surviving New Netherland record groups (provincial-secretary registers 1638–1660, patents/deeds 1630–1664) produce no qualifying Joan connected to Smith's trading post or to Greene.

## Angle 5: Ship Passenger Lists (1630–1670)

### Search Scope
Any unaccompanied woman named Joan arriving in New England.

### Results

The only named Joans found were on the reconstructed 1630 *Mary and John* manifest:
- **Joan Denslow** — child in a family group
- **Joan Ford** — child in a family group
- **Joan Rockwell** — part of a family group
- **Joan Rossiter** — young female in a family/community group

**None were unaccompanied adult women.** No later connection to Greene found for any of them. No definitive original passenger list for that voyage survives; published lists are reconstructions.

**No unaccompanied woman named Joan found on any other passenger list 1630–1670.**

## Angle 6: "Anashuecot" in Roger Williams's Works

### Search Scope
Does the name "Anashuecot" appear anywhere outside the Fones Record? Specifically in Roger Williams's *A Key into the Language of America* (1643) or his published correspondence.

### Results

**"Anashuecot" does not appear** in:
- *A Key into the Language of America* (Project Gutenberg, EEBO, Archive.org editions)
- Published Williams correspondence (NHPRC catalog)
- Any other digitized colonial source outside the Fones Record

Williams's *Key* contains Narragansett vocabulary with forms beginning with *Ana-/Anas-* (e.g., *Anaskhómmin* = "to hoe or break up"), but nothing matching "Anashuecot."

### What This Means

The name "Anashuecot" exists **only in the Fones Record context.** This could mean:
1. It was a personal name not captured in Williams's vocabulary (which focused on common words, not proper names)
2. It was a title or status designation, not a personal name
3. It was a variant spelling that doesn't match digitized transcriptions
4. It was a localized form used in the Quidnessett area that Williams (working primarily with Canonicus's lineage at Narragansett Bay proper) didn't encounter

⚠️ **Per Law 3:** All four explanations remain open. The absence from Williams does NOT prove the name is spurious — Williams's vocabulary was selective and his personal-name documentation was limited.

## Consolidated Assessment

### Model A (Joan = Anashuecot)

**Neither confirmed nor eliminated by this audit.**
- The name Anashuecot appears only in the Fones Record — no independent corroboration, but no disproof either
- No alternative English Joan has been found in any searched record group
- The absence of alternatives **raises** the cost of Model B (where did she come from?)

### Model B (Joan = Unknown English Woman)

**Severely weakened.**
- Six search angles across four colonies, Dutch records, ship manifests, and indenture records produced **zero** qualifying alternative Joans
- The only candidate (Joan Beggarly) is itself unverified and may be a La Mance contamination
- Model B must now explain: if Joan was English, why does she exist in NO English record system at all?

### Model C (Joan = Joan Beggarly)

**Held open but unverified.**
- The Beggarly surname is not independently confirmed by any 17th-century source
- The Winthrop Papers footnote text has not been directly examined
- The confusion between two John Greenes (each married to a Joan) is a documented problem in the literature
- Alice Beggarly (wife of Surgeon John) is confirmed; whether a second Joan Beggarly exists is unknown
- **Per Law 3:** This hypothesis is not eliminated. The Winthrop footnote is the critical gap.
