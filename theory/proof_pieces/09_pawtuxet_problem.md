## What WAS Found in the Warwick Records

### 1. "John Greene Junior" in Warwick Governance (1648–1663+)

The Early Records of the Town of Warwick contain **extensive** documentation of a "John Greene Junior" serving in Warwick government across 15+ years:

- **1648:** Listed among original purchasers of Warwick; chosen for Assembly
- **1649:** Town Clerk, Assistant, wrote declarations, carried letters, chosen for mill undertaking at Occupasnetuxet
- **1650:** Summoned by Massachusetts; town ordered him not to go
- **1651:** Town Clerk and Assistant; ordered to take Treasury accounts
- **1652–53:** Commissioner to General Court (multiple terms); Clarke; authorized to grant warrants; paid for drum supplies
- **1656–58:** Continued as Commissioner, Clarke, Assistant
- **1659–63:** Assistant and Clarke; continued active role

**This is definitively Surgeon John's son** (the future Deputy Governor). He is "Junior" to his father ("Mr Greene Senior" / "Mr John Greene Senior" appears alongside him in the same records).

### 2. The 1659 Nanaquakesett Deed — Awashouse Confirmed (p. 38)

The Warwick records contain a deed dated **May 27, 1659** where **Cachanequenecke alias Tesseronohutt** (a Narragansett sachem) gives land at Nanaquakesett to Randall Houlden and Samuel Gorton.

Witness list includes:
- **Awashouse** — identified as **"Indian"**
- **Alequacomett** — identified as **"Eldest sonn to ye abovesaid sachem"**
- **Newcomb** — Indian witness

And on the confirmation by Pessicus/Mossup/Sucquaune:
- Witnesses: **John Greene** and **Phillip Green**

⚠️ **This is the same Awashouse who appears in Potter 1835 as a land owner at Nisquitianxsett (Oct 5, 1661) and who is phonetically equivalent to Anashuecot (Fones Record 1672).** The Warwick record confirms Awashouse was present at Gorton/Houlden land transactions, witnessed by John Greene.

## The Critical Question: Which John Witnessed the 1659 Deed?

The 1659 Nanaquakesett deed witnesses include **"John Greene"** (no qualifier) and **"Phillip Green."**

| Candidate | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **John Greene Junior (Warwick/Deputy Gov)** | PROBABLE — he was Assistant and Clarke in Warwick in 1659; this is a Warwick-recorded deed involving Gorton and Houlden (his political circle) |
| **John Greene of Quidnessett** | POSSIBLE — he was living in Narragansett Country by 1659; the deed concerns Narragansett land |
| **"Phillip Green"** | Unknown — which Phillip? Surgeon John had a son Peter, not Phillip. A separate Phillip Greene? |

**Both Johns could plausibly be the witness.** The lack of a qualifier ("Junior" / "Senior" / "of Quidnessett") makes it impossible to distinguish from this record alone.

## What This Means for the Independent John Hypothesis

The Warwick records **strongly confirm** that "John Greene Junior" in Warwick governance = Surgeon John's son. This man served as Clerk, Assistant, Commissioner across 15+ years alongside his father ("Mr Greene Senior").

**If Quidnessett John is a separate person**, he never appears in Warwick town records. The Pawtuxet connection — the one thread tying him to Warwick — is now UNVERIFIED.

**If the 1682 Pawtuxet deed doesn't exist** (conflation hypothesis), then:
- Joan has ONE documented appearance (March 1682), not two
- The locational bubble stays at Quidnessett/Narragansett Country
- No Warwick-corridor connection
- The Independent John hypothesis becomes even stronger

**If the deed DOES exist** (just not in digitized sources), then:
- The Pawtuxet inheritance question stands
- Some connection to Warwick-corridor land exists
- But Surgeon John's will still doesn't name Quidnessett John
