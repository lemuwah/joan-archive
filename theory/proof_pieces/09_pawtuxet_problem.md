## The Question

On **19 May 1682**, Joan gave her "free & voluntary consent" to the sale of **Pawtuxet** land to William Carpenter for £10. She signed with her mark. Witnesses: John Wickes and Samuel Gorton.

But Pawtuxet is in the **Warwick/Cranston corridor** — Surgeon John's territory, 20+ miles from Quidnessett. How does Joan's husband own Pawtuxet land?

The Kimi analysis suggested this could mean our John IS connected to the Warwick Greene line. This proof piece tests that claim.

---

## Part 1: What the Deed Says and Doesn't Say

### Says:
- Grantor: **"John Greene"** (NO residence qualifier)
- Grantee: William Carpenter
- Consideration: £10
- Land: Pawtuxet
- Nature of acquisition: **Inherited** (deed describes it as inherited)
- Joan's separate consent: "free & voluntary"
- Joan's signature: "her marke"
- Witnesses: John Wickes, Samuel Gorton

### Does NOT Say:
- **"John Greene of Quidnessett"** — not in the deed
- **"John Greene of Warwick"** — not in the deed
- **"John Greene Senior"** — not confirmed in the deed text
- Which ancestor the land was inherited from
- When the inheritance occurred

**Source:** RI Land Evidences Vol. VII, pp. 177–178

⚠️ **Per Law 1:** The identification of this John Greene as "John of Quidnessett" is a GENEALOGICAL INFERENCE, not the deed's own text.

## Part 2: Why "John of Quidnessett" Is the Standard Identification

The contextual case:

1. **Joan.** The same woman who signed the March 1682 homeplace deed (which IS explicitly Quidnessett land) also signs this one. Same name, same "mark" signature, same year.
2. **Timing.** March 24, 1682 → May 19, 1682 = less than 2 months apart. Looks like a coordinated disposition of assets.
3. **No other John Greene had a wife named Joan** in 1682:
   - Surgeon John (d. 1658/59) — dead 24 years
   - Deputy Governor John (Warwick) — wife was Ann Almy
   - Lt. John² — wife was Mary Jefferay (per Austin/Bates)
   - East Greenwich John — wife was Abigail Wardwell (m. 1684 — after this deed)

**Verdict:** The identification is strong. Joan is the anchor.

## Part 3: The Explosive Question — How Did He Get Pawtuxet Land?

### The deed says the land was "inherited."

**Inherited from whom?**

### Possibility 1: Surgeon John Greene of Warwick (the family connection)

If our John **inherited** Pawtuxet land, the most obvious source is Surgeon John Greene (d. 1658/59), who held extensive Warwick-area lands. But:

- Bates treats the two as separate lines
- No document establishes a father-son or brother relationship
- Surgeon John's will (1658) is extant and names his heirs — does it name a John at Quidnessett?
- Y-DNA diverges from the expected English Greene line (Irish Sea cluster)

⚠️ **If our John inherited from Surgeon John, he IS in the Warwick family line.** This would mean:
- The two Johns are NOT separate lines — they're the same family
- Quidnessett John's "invisibility" before 1671 might be explained by his being a younger son in Surgeon John's family
- The DNA divergence would need a non-paternity event somewhere in the 340-year chain
- Joan married into an ENGLISH family, not vice versa

### Possibility 2: A separate Pawtuxet proprietary share

Pawtuxet had its own proprietorship distinct from Warwick/Shawomet. Our John could have:
- Purchased a share independently (but deed says "inherited," not "purchased")
- Inherited through a non-Greene relative
- Inherited through Joan's own family (but the deed names John as grantor, not Joan)

### Possibility 3: The 1658 Carpenter connection

A **1658 deed from "John Greene Junr Warwick"** sold Pawtuxet-area land to William Carpenter — the **same William Carpenter** who bought from our John in 1682.

**"John Greene Junr Warwick" in 1658 = ???**

| Candidate | Birth | Age in 1658 | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Surgeon John's eldest son (Warwick John / Deputy Gov.) | 1620 | 38 | POSSIBLE — "Junr" to his father |
| Our John of Quidnessett | c. 1606 | ~52 | UNLIKELY as "Junr" — he's OLDER than Surgeon John's son |
| Lt. John² (if born c. 1645) | c. 1645 | ~13 | TOO YOUNG for land conveyance |

The 1658 "John Greene Junr Warwick" is almost certainly **Surgeon John's eldest surviving son** (the future Deputy Governor), who was 38 and would be "Junior" while his father was alive.

**But then:** If the 1658 sale already conveyed this land to Carpenter, what was OUR John selling in 1682? Was it a **different** Pawtuxet parcel? Or did Carpenter acquire from multiple Greenes over time?

## Part 4: The William Carpenter Evidence

William Carpenter was an established **Pawtuxet proprietor** who bought land from **multiple sources**:
- The Arnold family
- "John Greene Junr Warwick" (1658)
- "John Greene" (our 1682 deed)

The 1658 and 1682 transactions are almost certainly **different parcels** — Carpenter was assembling a landholding from multiple sources over decades.

---

## Part 5: What the Witnesses Tell Us

**John Wickes** and **Samuel Gorton** are both Warwick/Shawomet men.

This does NOT prove the grantor is a Warwick man. It proves:
- The transaction took place in the Pawtuxet/Warwick social network
- Wickes and Gorton knew both parties
- The witnesses are appropriate for Pawtuxet land, regardless of the grantor's residence

**But it IS worth noting:** These are NOT Quidnessett witnesses. The March 1682 homeplace deed (Quidnessett land) presumably had local witnesses. The May 1682 Pawtuxet deed has Warwick witnesses. This is consistent with two different geographic transactions.

## Part 6: Model Impact — The Explosive Part

### IF John of Quidnessett inherited Pawtuxet land from the Warwick Greene family:

**Model A impact:**
- Does NOT eliminate Model A. Even if John was born into the Warwick Greene family, he could still have married a Narragansett woman.
- Actually STRENGTHENS Model A in one way: a man with inherited English property AND a Narragansett wife explains both the English deed mechanics (he knew how to use them) AND the anomalous clauses (he was protecting Indigenous interests).
- The "or to her mother" clause becomes even MORE anomalous — why would an Englishman from a well-documented family insert a matrilineal clause for an English wife?

**Model B impact:**
- Would strengthen Model B IF "inherited" meant inherited through Joan's family. But the deed names John as grantor, not Joan.
- Does NOT explain Joan's invisibility — if John was from the Warwick Greenes, his wife SHOULD be well-documented (Ann Almy is; Phillippa is; Joane Tattershall is).
- Actually makes Joan's erasure MORE anomalous: every other woman married into the Warwick Greene family IS documented.

### The key test:

**Read Surgeon John's 1658 will.** It's extant. It names his heirs. Does it name anyone at Quidnessett/Cocumscussoc? Does it convey Pawtuxet land? If yes — explosive. If no — the inheritance came from somewhere else.
