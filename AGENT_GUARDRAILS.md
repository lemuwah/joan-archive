## 1. Transcribed Narratives Are Not Verbatim Quotes

Bates's summary, Worth's abstract, F.L. Greene's paraphrase, and any secondary transcription represent **the author's description of the deed**, not the deed's own words.

- Do not treat "Greene and his wife Joan deeded" (Bates) as proving Joan's legal role — it proves Bates described it that way.
- Do not infer consent, signature, mark, or co-grantor status from summary language.
- Only the manuscript is verbatim. Until the manuscript is read, all claims about Joan's specific legal actions in the deed are **⚫ SUSPENDED**.

**Currently suspended claims:**
- "Joan signed with a mark" — no source says this for the March 1682 deed
- "Joan gave consent" — Worth doesn't mention consent; Bates says "and his wife Joan deeded" which is his narration
- "Free and voluntary consent" — may come from the suspended Pawtuxet deed
- "Or to her mother if she survive" — not in any source we hold (see `contradictions/mother-clause-wording.md`)

## 2. No Centering One Model

The archive holds **multiple competing hypotheses** about Joan's identity. No agent output should frame the investigation as confirming or building toward a specific model.

- Present evidence.
- Map it to all applicable models.
- Let the reader decide.

The archive investigates **who Joan was**, not a preferred theory about who Joan was.

---

## 3. Equal Model Treatment

No hypothesis receives more visual space, more confident language, or more prominent placement than its evidence warrants.

**Open Models (all ⚫ HYPOTHESIS):**

- **Model A — Narragansett sachem-line woman.** The Anashuecot hypothesis. Phonetic, geographic, temporal overlap.
- **Model B — English woman, undocumented.** Many colonial women left no record. Not unusual.
- **Model C — Irish origin.** Y-DNA points to Irish Sea cluster. Could be patrilineal only.
- **Model D — Indentured servant.** Would explain absence of maiden name, family, parish record.
- **Model E — Enslaved person.** Post-1676 legal framework created conditions for this.
- **Model F — Widow of prior marriage.** Could explain legal standing without requiring Indigenous identity.
- **Model G — Beggerly / La Mance.** Alice Daniels. 🔴 ELIMINATED — wrong John Greene, wrong town, timeline impossible.

Every open model gets:
- Its own evidence section when analysis.html is built
- A "what would confirm this" column
- A "what would eliminate this" column
- Equal visual weight unless evidence warrants otherwise

## 4. Verified Facts — The Complete List

This is everything the archive can state as fact about Joan:

1. Joan is named as wife of John Greene in a March 1682 land deed (Worth abstract)
2. A life annuity of 30 shillings per year is reserved to her (Worth abstract)
3. No other record of Joan has been located

Three facts. Everything else is open. Every model starts from these three facts and nowhere else.

## 5. CARE-B Checkpoint

Before any commit that touches public-facing content or analysis:

- **C** — Collective benefit? Does this serve the community of descendants and scholars, not just one narrative?
- **A** — Authority to control respected? Is Narragansett governance described only from Narragansett sources, or flagged as external interpretation?
- **R** — Responsibility for harm? Could this claim, if wrong, cause real harm to living people or communities?
- **E** — Survives ethical review? Would this pass scrutiny from Tomaquag Museum staff?
- **B** — Bias check: Does this output privilege one model over others without evidence?

All five must pass. If any fails, revise before committing.

---

## 6. External Interpretation Tagging

All descriptions of Narragansett governance, cultural practice, or political structure derived from non-Indigenous academic sources must be tagged:

> 📖 EXTERNAL INTERPRETATION — derived from [source]. Not verified by Narragansett authority.

Or removed entirely until analysis.html is built with proper context.

---

## 7. No Cross-Lens Smoothing

When the Multi-Lens Rule is implemented, each lens asks its own questions independently. Do not:
- Use Lens 1 findings to answer Lens 4 questions
- Assume Lens 5 phonetics prove Lens 2 identity
- Smooth across lenses to create a unified narrative

Each lens produces its own findings. The reader synthesizes. The archive does not.
