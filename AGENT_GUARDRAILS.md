## 1. Transcribed Narratives Are Not Verbatim Quotes

Bates's summary, Worth's abstract, F.L. Greene's paraphrase, and any secondary transcription represent **the author's description of the deed**, not the deed's own words.

- Do not treat "Greene and his wife Joan deeded" (Bates) as proving Joan's legal role — it proves Bates described it that way.
- Do not infer consent, signature, mark, or co-grantor status from summary language.
- Only the manuscript is verbatim. Until the manuscript is read, all claims about Joan's specific legal actions in the deed are **inferences from secondary descriptions**.

---

## 2. The Suspension Rule

If a claim's source has been withdrawn, invalidated, or shown to be circular, the claim is **⚠️ SUSPENDED** — not deleted, not restated, not silently dropped.

- Every suspended item must appear with its ⚠️ tag in every file that references it.
- Suspended items may not appear on public pages (`index.html`, `context.html`) without the tag.
- Resolution: locate the primary manuscript and read the actual words. Until then, the claim is held open.
- See `evidence/suspended_items.md` for the master list.

---

## 3. The La Mance Law (Law 2) Is Retroactive

La Mance (1904) conflated two John Greenes and created a phantom maiden name ("Joan Beggarly"). Any claim traceable to La Mance requires independent primary corroboration.

- This includes all downstream databases (Ancestry, FamilySearch, WikiTree, FindAGrave) that copied La Mance.
- "10,000 trees say it" is not corroboration — it is amplification of one error.
- See `contamination/la_mance_chain.md`.

## 4. What We Actually Know About Joan

Four facts. Everything else is open. Every model starts from these four facts and nowhere else.

1. Joan is named as wife of John Greene in a March 1682 land deed (Worth abstract + Image 10 AI transcription)
2. A life annuity is reserved to her (amount under review — Worth says 30 shillings; manuscript read may differ)
3. Joan does not sign or mark the 1682 deed (Image 10, 🟢 PROOF — AI TRANSCRIPTION)
4. No other record of Joan has been located — no birth, baptism, marriage, death, burial, probate, church, or court record

Do not add to this list without a primary source image. Do not subtract from it without documenting the withdrawal in `CORRECTIONS.md`.

## 5. The Five-John Firewall

Five different men named John Greene appear in colonial Rhode Island records. They are NOT the same person. Confusing them is the single most common error in Greene genealogy.

| # | Identity | Key Identifier |
|---|----------|---------------|
| 1 | John Greene, Surgeon of Warwick | "surgeon", Gillingham Dorset, wives Joanne Tattersall + Philippa, will 1658 |
| 2 | John Greene of Quidnessett | "lived with" Smith, wife Joan, 1679 affidavit — **THIS IS OUR JOHN** |
| 3 | John Greene Jr of Warwick | "John Greene Junr" in deeds, son of Surgeon John |
| 4 | John Greene of Newport | May = #2 per Bates (PLAUSIBLE alias) |
| 5 | John Greene of Kingstown | May = #2 under post-incorporation town name (PLAUSIBLE alias) |

Before attributing ANY "John Greene" record to our John, verify which John is meant. If the source does not specify, the record is **unassigned**.

## 6. The Joan/Johan Smith Firewall

Joan/Johan Smith — daughter of Richard Smith Sr. — married Thomas Newton. She lived at Cocumscussoc, the SAME location as Joan Greene.

Any record that says "Joan" at Wickford/Cocumscussoc/Narragansett without a surname could be EITHER woman. The surname is the only disambiguator.
