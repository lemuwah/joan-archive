# Synthesizer — Cycle #5 (2026-09-05)
## The Honest State of the Joan Archive After 11 Searches

---

## What We Know

One woman. One deed. Five mentions in one article. Zero information about who she was.

Joan Greene appears in the historical record exactly once as a primary source: March 24, 1682, a deed transferring the family homeplace in Quidnessett to her sons Daniel and James. She is called the wife of John Greene. She receives an annuity — thirty shillings per year from each son, so long as either parent lives. She does not sign the deed.

Louise Prosser Bates, writing in 1918, mentions Joan five more times in a two-part genealogical article about John Greene. Every mention uses Joan as an identifier — "John and Joan Greene of Newport and Quidnisset" — to place children in their family. Bates never says where Joan came from. Never gives her maiden name. Never says when she died. Never speculates about her identity. Joan was not Bates's subject. John was.

The article that serves as the foundation for every genealogical database entry about Joan contains zero independent information about Joan.

---

## What We Don't Know

We don't know Joan's maiden name, parentage, place of origin, ethnicity, tribal identity (if any), marriage date, death date, or burial place. We don't know if she could read. We don't know her age. We don't know if she was English, Irish, Dutch, Narragansett, Wampanoag, Niantic, African, mixed, or something else entirely. We don't know if she chose to marry John or was compelled. We don't know if she was free or bound.

We don't know if she was alive in 1685 when John died. We don't know if she was alive in 1695 when James sold part of the land. We don't know anything about her life before March 1682 or after it.

Six models attempt to explain her identity. None have proof. None have been eliminated. The archive holds all six open.

## What Changed Today

1. **Anochetuckett 1687 is geography, not a person.** The river named for Anashuecot's lineage. Dead end for the "Anashuecot alive" theory.

2. **Smith's household is a ghost.** Nobody named at Cocumscussoc except Smith, his unnamed children, unnamed servants, and John Greene ("lived with" — 1679 affidavit). No women documented.

3. **John has brothers.** The 1675 Worthington deed names James and Thomas as John's BROTHERS, not sons. There are two James Greenes — uncle and nephew.

4. **Worthington can't help us anymore.** Joan appears once (p. 173). The critical deeds (Clark-to-Brinley 1685, Wightman 1695/96, Carr 1651) are in LOCAL town records that Worthington didn't abstract.

5. **Bates is exhausted as a secondary source.** Her article contains no Joan information beyond the name. Her value is in the LOCAL RECORDS she cites — NK, Jamestown, Newport — which we have not read.

6. **Three contaminations caught and killed.** Bow-and-arrow (AI), Joan Tibbitts (fabricated), John's death as PROOF (downgraded).

## The Path Forward

The archive has reached the end of what secondary sources can provide. Worthington is read. Bates is read. Bartlett is read. Potter is referenced. Austin is referenced.

Joan's truth — if it exists in any document — is in the LOCAL TOWN RECORDS:

- **NK Land Evidence Book 1** — the Wightman 1695/96 deed. Does it mention Joan? Does it reference the annuity? This is the single most important unread document.
- **Jamestown Land Evidence Records** — the Clark-to-Brinley 1685 deed. "Now deceased." Can we see the original?
- **Newport Town Records** — births, marriages. Were any of Joan's children recorded?

These records exist. They are held at the RI State Archives and/or the respective town clerks. Some may be on FamilySearch microfilm.

The archive is clean. The laws are holding. The system caught three errors today. The honest state is this: we know Joan's name, her husband, and her provision. Everything else requires documents we haven't read yet.

One woman. One deed. The rest is silence — for now.
