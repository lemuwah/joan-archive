## What Happened Since Cycle #4

Two critical self-corrections reshaped the evidentiary landscape:

1. **Correction #1 (Pawtuxet citation kill):** The "Vol. VII pp. 177–178" citation for Joan's second legal appearance was definitively invalidated. Those pages contain 1773 Gaspee commission records. Bates (1918) — the most authoritative source — does not mention the Pawtuxet deed. Joan now has **ONE verified legal appearance** (March 1682).

2. **Correction #2 (Mother-clause wording):** The phrase "or to her mother if she survive" — the archive's central documentary anomaly — is not found in any source the archive holds. F.L. Greene says "their father or mother" (= the sons' parents). Worth says "after his decease to Joane Greene, his wife." The NK manuscript says "dureing her naturall life." The anomalous wording was most likely a pronoun shift introduced during paraphrase in July 2026.

**Together, these corrections removed the two most cited pieces of evidence across all four prior agent cycles, the live site, and the proof pieces.** The archive documented both corrections publicly in CORRECTIONS.md with full provenance.

## The Honest Model Comparison (Revised)

### Model A — Joan Was a Narragansett Woman
**Type of argument:** Negative case (absence-of-evidence) + contextual pattern

**Core evidence:**
- Joan's unique documentary silence among Quidnessett wives (1 anomaly)
- The Independent John hypothesis: John himself has no documented English family connection, no pre-RI paper trail, and spent his entire adult life at the Narragansett cross-cultural frontier (supporting context)
- Documented mechanisms: female Indigenous land authority existed (Weunquesh); colonial Indigenous erasure patterns documented (Tomaquag/Brooks); Narragansett culture is matrilineal (Tomaquag)

**What it explains:**
- Why Joan has no English records (she wasn't English)
- Why she is an outlier among Quidnessett wives (different cultural background)
- Why "nothing further is known about her" (she existed in Narragansett kinship systems)

**What it requires but lacks:**
- Any positive documentary link between Joan and any Indigenous individual or community
- Joan's mark showing an Indigenous pictographic pattern
- A bridging document connecting the 1672 Anashuecot to the 1682 Joan

### Model B — Joan Was an Unknown English Woman
**Type of argument:** Default assumption + fire/loss explanation

**Core evidence:**
- "Joan" is an English name in an English household executing English legal instruments
- English is the default identity assumption for colonial New England (not a finding; a prior)

**Anomalies it must explain (honest count: 1–2):**
1. Joan's documentary silence — unique among Quidnessett wives, unusual even controlling for fire/poverty/isolation. This may be ONE observation or TWO depending on whether the comparison-set outlier status and the absolute silence are counted together or separately.

**What would resolve it:**
- Finding any English Joan who matches (colony, dates, marriage to a John Greene)
- Finding that other frontier wives have similarly thin records (expanded comparison set)
- Documenting that the 1869 fire destroyed a generation of wives' records, not just John's will

### Model C — Joan = "Joan Beggarly" → **ELIMINATED** (Cycle #4, rock solid)

### The Parsimonious Assessment
Model A remains more parsimonious: it explains Joan's pattern with one premise (she was Indigenous). Model B requires Joan to be uniquely undocumented among her peers, which may have a mundane explanation (fire, isolation) but has not been demonstrated.

**However:** Model A is now a negative case, not a positive case. It works by ruling out alternatives, not by proving its own claim. This is legitimate scholarship but it is inherently provisional — a single document could overturn it.
