## H15: Joan's Annuity as Death Marker

The 1682 deed gives Joan a life annuity — payable "dureing her naturall life." When that annuity stops being an encumbrance on the land, Joan is dead.

**Test:** The James Greene → George Wightman deed (Feb 15, 1695/96) sells 18.25 acres of the homeplace. If that deed mentions Joan's annuity as still active, she's alive. If it sells clean title with no annuity reference, she's dead or the annuity lapsed.

**Where:** Bates cites RIHS Collections vol. XI p. 25. NOT in Worthington. Local NK deed book. FamilySearch DGS for NK Land Evidence.

**Status:** PLAUSIBLE — testable with one document read.

## H16: John's Brothers as Joan's Entry Point

The 1675 deed (Worthington p. 102) reveals John had two brothers: James and Thomas Greene. If John came to Narragansett Country through a family network, his brothers might have been part of that network. Joan could have come through the same channel.

**What to search:** Who are James and Thomas Greene? Where did they come from? Did either marry? Any records of wives, households, servants?

**Weakness (self-identified):** The brothers received land FROM John in 1675. That makes John the senior landholder and them the dependents, not the other way around. Joan's entry is more likely through Smith's household or John's own connections, not through younger brothers he was providing for.

**Status:** PLAUSIBLE but weak — brothers are a genealogical lead, not a Joan entry mechanism.

## H17: Anochetuckett River = Sachem Territory Marker

The 1687 Bartlett record names "Anochetuekett River" as a boundary for land that IS the same territory as the 1672 Fones Purchase. The river is named after a sachem or sachem line. This confirms the Anashuecot name was geographically anchored to this specific land.

**What it means for Joan:** If Joan = Anashuecot, she literally lived on land named for her lineage. If Joan ≠ Anashuecot, the geographic overlap is still documented — but it's the land, not the person.

**Status:** PROOF — the place name is documented. The connection to Joan is PLAUSIBLE (Model A only).

## H18: Missing Baptisms as Frontier Evidence

No birth or baptism record exists for ANY child of John and Joan Greene. Quidnessett had no church in the 1640s–1660s. Smith's trading post was a frontier outpost, not a parish.

**What this means per model:**
- Model A (Narragansett): No Christian baptism expected. Consistent.
- Model B (English): Possible on frontier with no minister. Consistent.
- Model C (Irish Catholic): No Protestant minister would baptize. Consistent.
- Model D (Indentured): No church access during service. Consistent.
- Model E (Enslaved): No baptism expected. Consistent.
- Model F (Widow): Depends on first marriage context. Neutral.

**Status:** PROOF (of absence) — but consistent with ALL models. Not diagnostic.

## H19: Two-James Confusion as Contamination Vector

The 1675 deed reveals a "Mr James Greene" who is John's BROTHER. The 1682 deed names a "James Greene" who is John's SON. Uncle and nephew with the same name. Every compiled genealogy that doesn't separate them is potentially corrupted.

**Specific risk:** The Absolom affidavit reference to "James" — is this James the brother (adult landholder in 1675) or James the son ("natural son" in 1682)? The identity changes the entire reading.

**Status:** PLAUSIBLE contamination risk — audit needed.
