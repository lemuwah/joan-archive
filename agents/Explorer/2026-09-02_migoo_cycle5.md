## New Hypotheses

### H14 — The Bates Grantor Contradiction as Evidence
**Claim:** The contradiction between Bates 1918 ("Greene and his wife Joan deeded") and Worth 1921/NK manuscript (John alone signs) is not a transcription error — it may reflect two DIFFERENT instruments that Bates read but Worth abstracted only one of.

**Why it matters:** Worth abstracts only the James (60-acre) deed. The Daniel (120-acre) deed is a separate instrument. If Joan appeared as co-grantor on the Daniel deed but not on the James deed, Bates's statement could be accurate for one instrument and Worth's for the other. The NK manuscript at image 10 shows the Daniel deed — but it has not been fully transcribed.

**What to look for:** In the Daniel deed (120 acres), does Joan's name appear in the grantor clause rather than just the beneficiary clause? Does a second mark or signature appear in the signature block that has been missed?

**Testable by:** Full paleographic transcription of DGS #008204949, images 9–12 (both instruments). This is already the archive's #1 digitization ask.

**Status:** SPECULATIVE but grounded in a real documentary discrepancy.

### H15 — The Silence IS the Evidence: Single-Appearance Analysis
**Claim:** Joan's documentary footprint — exactly one verified legal appearance in 40+ years of marriage — is itself the most probative evidence, now that the "mother clause" and second appearance are gone.

**Why it matters:** With the corrections, the argument shifts. The question is no longer "why does the deed contain anomalous matrilineal language?" (it may not). The question is now: **why does a woman married to a landholding freeholder for decades appear in exactly one document?**

**The comparison set survives the corrections cleanly:**
- Ann Smith (Richard Smith's wife) — documented in probate, deeds, court
- Mary Gardiner — documented with maiden name
- Elizabeth Helme — documented with maiden name, church records
- Penelope Stanton — documented with maiden name

Joan is the outlier regardless of the "mother clause." Her absence is the anomaly.

**Possible explanations under Model B (English woman):**
1. Records destroyed in fires/wars — but other Quidnessett wives survived the same fires
2. She married John late in life and died shortly after — contradicts the life-estate provision (would be pointless for a dying woman)
3. She was illiterate and never signed anything — but illiterate English women still appear in church, tax, and probate records
4. She was from a colony that lost records — requires identifying which colony, which has never been done

**Possible explanations under Model A (Indigenous woman):**
1. She existed in Narragansett kinship systems, not English legal systems — explains total absence from English records
2. She entered the English record system through one act: her husband's land partition deed — consistent with cross-cultural marriages documented elsewhere (Pocahontas, Weetamoo)
3. "Nothing further is known about her" is what 1894 scholars expected when an Indigenous wife appeared only at the point of English legal necessity

**Status:** ANALYTICAL HYPOTHESIS — not testable directly, but the comparison set is verified evidence.

### H16 — The F.L. Greene Date Contradiction as a Second-John Problem
**Claim:** F.L. Greene 1894 has John Greene Sr. alive in 1686 (signing an address to the King) and 1692 (witnessing a deed). Bates 1918 has him dead before 25 Sept 1685 (based on the "John Greene, sr., now deceased" reference in the Joseph Clark deed). These CANNOT both be true for the same person.

**Why it matters:** If F.L. Greene's 1686 and 1692 dates are correct, they might refer to a DIFFERENT John Greene who was also called "Sr." — adding a SIXTH John to the Five-John Firewall. Or F.L. Greene conflated two Johns. Or Bates misread the 1685 deed.

**Testable by:**
1. Read F.L. Greene 1894 pp. 10–11 verbatim on archive.org — what exactly does he say about 1686 and 1692?
2. Check the 1685 Joseph Clark deed verbatim — does it really say "now deceased"?
3. Check whether the 1686 and 1692 documents specify "of Quidnessett" or just "John Greene"

**What would each result mean:**
- If F.L. Greene's dates are wrong → F.L. Greene is less reliable than Bates (affects how we weight his paraphrase of the deed)
- If F.L. Greene's dates refer to a different John → the Five-John Firewall needs expansion
- If Bates's "now deceased" is a misread → John lived longer than believed, and Joan's life-estate started later

**Status:** FACTUAL CONTRADICTION — held open per Law 1. Not a hypothesis; a documented discrepancy between two secondary sources.

### H17 — The "Dureing Her Naturall Life" Clause as a Functional Firewall
**Claim:** Even after stripping the "mother clause," the deed's life-estate provision is functionally distinctive. It is not that the LANGUAGE is anomalous (it isn't — "dureing her naturall life" is standard English dower). It is that the CIRCUMSTANCE is anomalous: a woman receiving a life-estate whose identity is completely undocumented for 40 years before and after.

**Why it matters:** A standard English dower provision in an otherwise standard English deed becomes anomalous when applied to a woman who doesn't exist in any other English record. The clause itself doesn't prove she's Narragansett. But the clause PLUS the 40-year silence PLUS the comparison set creates a pattern that Model B struggles to explain.

**This is weaker than the "mother clause" argument.** It's circumstantial rather than linguistic. But it is REAL — it doesn't depend on unverified wording.

**Status:** ANALYTICAL FRAMEWORK — the argument has been downgraded from "linguistic anomaly" to "circumstantial pattern." Honest.

### H18 — The 1659 Awashouse-Greene Witness Co-Occurrence
**Claim:** In the 1659 Nanaquakesett deed (Chapin 1926, p. 38), Awashouse witnesses a Narragansett land transaction, and "John Greene" (no qualifier) also witnesses. If Awashouse = Anashuecot (phonetic hypothesis, held at low confidence per Hostile Reviewer), then a person in the Anashuecot name-cluster and a person named John Greene co-witness a deed 23 years before Joan appears in the 1682 deed.

**Why it matters:** This is NOT proof of a marital connection. But it is a DOCUMENTED CO-OCCURRENCE: Awashouse and John Greene appear on the same legal instrument. The names are in the same witness list. This is the kind of "boring document" the Explorer has always argued might hide evidence in plain sight.

**The weakness:** Which John Greene? The Warwick John (likely, since this is a Warwick-recorded deed involving Gorton and Houlden) or the Quidnessett John (possible, since Nanaquakesett is Narragansett territory)? Without a qualifier, we can't distinguish.

**Testable by:**
1. Identify whether "Phillip Green" (also on the witness list) connects to either John's family — does either John have a son/brother named Phillip?
2. Check other 1650s–1660s deeds for Awashouse + any Greene co-occurrences
3. Check whether Surgeon John's son ("John Greene Junior") would have been "John Greene" (no qualifier) in a 1659 Warwick deed when his father was still alive — the father's records use "Mr Greene Senior"

**Status:** DOCUMENTED CO-OCCURRENCE (the names are real and on the same deed). The identity connections are SPECULATIVE.

### H19 — Joan's Death and the Missing Probate
**Claim:** When Joan died, her life-estate terminated and the sons' fee simple vested absolutely. There should be SOME record of this transition — a probate entry, a court notation, a deed reference saying "Joan being now deceased." No such record has been found.

**Why it matters:** Under English law, the termination of a life-estate is an administrative event. If Joan died while the North Kingstown records were intact (before the 1869 fire), there should have been a notation. If she died in the 1690s or 1700s, records from that period in NK survive. Her absence from death/probate records extends the silence pattern beyond 1682.

**Where to look:**
1. North Kingstown town records post-1685 — any notation of the life-estate terminating
2. Daniel² and James² Greene deeds after Joan's death — do they reference "our mother Joan being deceased" or similar?
3. Any Greene probate record 1690–1720 — does any refer to Joan's life-estate terminating?

**Status:** EVIDENCE GAP — no search for Joan's death record has been systematically conducted.
