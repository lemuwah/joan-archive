## Finding 2: "Conveyed" — Regional Legal Mechanics + Greene Servant Paperwork

**The idea:** Test whether "conveyed" (as in the Absolom affidavit's *"conveyed out of ye country"*) had a fixed legal meaning in 1670s Rhode Island, and look for surviving certificate/register paperwork from the 1676 disposal legislation that might independently corroborate a Greene-household placement.

**What was found:**
- No surviving certificate register from the 1676 disposal legislation has been identified as its own series — the certificates did not survive intact; only entries embedded in town papers and colony records survive.
- "Conveyed" had **no single fixed legal definition** in 17th-century Rhode Island usage — the same word covered moving people, transferring property, and removing documents. Meaning was context-dependent: **"conveyed out"** reads as jurisdictional removal (a person taken out of a jurisdiction/country); **"conveyed to"** reads as naming a destination or recipient.
- The Providence disposal committee record **survives**: *Early Records of the Town of Providence*, Vol. 15, pp. 151–154; originals held at RIHS (call number F89 .P9 P9). Contains the graduated service-term schedule (children under 5 served until age 30, etc.).
- Two "Jacob Green(e)" entries found in a different colony's disposal paperwork: 10 Aug 1676, Charlestown, Massachusetts — Jacob Green and Jacob Green Jr. received Indian boys. One boy's father is recorded as "late of Quantisett," dead; mother "late of Narragansett," dead. These are **Massachusetts placements**, and Quantisett/Narragansett parentage puts the children in the right regional orbit — but nothing ties these Greens to the Quidnessett Greenes, and the placement is in the wrong colony.
- No Greene servant record specifically from Narragansett country has been located. The Marcy/Sarah entry (Finding 1) remains the closest Rhode-Island-side match, but it has no precise date or location attached to it.
- Repositories flagged for further search: RIHS Clarke Indian Manuscripts; Massachusetts Archives Collection 30; Newberry Library Ayer MS 586; Yale New England Indian Papers Series.

**Why this matters to the project:** The Absolom affidavit's "conveyed out of ye country" is a 🟢 VERIFIED anchor sentence in the source spine, but its evidentiary weight depends entirely on which of the two readings ("removed from jurisdiction" vs. "transferred/delivered to someone") is correct. This finding establishes that RI usage does not settle the question by vocabulary alone — it has to be settled by the surrounding sentence structure in the affidavit itself, and by comparison to the Providence committee's own usage in Vol. 15.

**What contradiction would appear if false:** If a fixed technical/legal definition of "conveyed" turns up in a Rhode Island statute or court glossary, this finding's central claim (contextual, non-fixed meaning) collapses and the affidavit's phrase would need to be re-read against that fixed definition specifically.
