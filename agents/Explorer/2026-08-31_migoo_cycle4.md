## New Hypotheses

### H10 — The Pre-War Succession Hypothesis
**Claim:** The Quidnessett sachem succession gap is NOT caused by King Philip's War. It's caused by Scuttup's 1668 death and Quequaquenuit's earlier death. Anashuecot's 1672 authority comes from a **pre-war** succession event, possibly mediated through Coginaquand and/or Tocomminon.

**Why it matters:** This reframes the entire timeline. Joan's 1682 appearance is NOT explained by war disruption — it's explained by a succession process that was already underway before the war began. The war added urgency (legal encoding of land rights) but didn't create the conditions.

**Testable by:** Searching for any record of a succession ceremony, council, or authorization between 1668 and 1672.

**Status:** SPECULATIVE — supported by documentary chronology but no direct evidence of a succession event.

### H11 — The Awassuocitt = Anashuecot Identity Hypothesis
**Claim:** The 1654 deed witness Awassuocitt is the same person as the 1672 Fones Purchase signatory Anashuecot/Awashouse.

**Why it matters:** If true, Anashuecot was:
- Already active in land transactions by 1654 (18 years before the Fones Purchase)
- Operating in the Potowomut/Coheassuck area (adjacent to Quidnessett)
- Associated with Tacommonan's deed network
- Father of English-named sons (William Baker — interpretive problem)

**Contradictions if true:**
- The William Baker problem: deed says "eldest son to him" but Baker appears as English freeman/inhabitant of Portsmouth as early as 1638. Is Baker truly Awassuocitt's biological son, or is the witness line being misread?
- If Anashuecot was active in 1654, his "3 sons" in 1672 would need to be reconciled with Baker and Wawnunockshaw
- Wawnunockshaw: ZERO appearances anywhere else

**Testable by:** Searching RI Land Evidences for Awassuocitt under all spelling variants; comparing witness lists across 1650s deeds.

**Status:** PHONETIC HYPOTHESIS — plausible but not proven. Per Law 1, held open.

### H12 — The Tomanick-Awashousse Counselor Pair
**Claim:** The 1644 Act of Submission places Tomanick AND Awashousse together as Pessicus's chief counselors. If Tomanick = Tocomminon (Anashuecot's father per Fones Record) AND Awashousse = Awassuocitt/Anashuecot, then Anashuecot's father and Anashuecot himself appear together in 1644.

**Why it matters:** Would push the kin group documentation back to 1644 — a father-son pair serving as Pessicus's counselors.

**Contradictions if true:**
- Tomanick = Tocomminon is phonetically plausible but unproven
- Awashousse = Awassuocitt = Anashuecot is a THREE-step phonetic chain
- No document states any of these identities

**Status:** HIGHLY SPECULATIVE — three stacked phonetic hypotheses. Per Law 1, recorded but not promoted.

### H13 — Greene as Indigenous Protector/Ally
**Claim:** John Greene's 1676 actions (transporting Potuck for peace negotiations) and 1679 position (Conservator of the Peace, sworn deposition about sachem consent) suggest an active, ongoing relationship with the Narragansett community — not just proximity.

**Why it matters:** If Greene was actively protecting Indigenous interests, marrying a Narragansett woman is consistent with his documented behavior, not anomalous.

**Contradictions if true:**
- The Potuck episode ended in betrayal (Talcott attacked despite Greene's promise) — does this weaken Greene's standing with the community?
- No direct evidence of sheltering or employing Indigenous persons post-war

**Status:** SUPPORTED BY DOCUMENTARY EVIDENCE (1676 sloop, 1679 Conservator role) but the marriage connection remains inferential.
