## 🔴 CRITICAL CORRECTION: THE 1663 CT PETITION

### What We Previously Claimed
The archive stated that John Greene "signed the 1663 CT jurisdiction petition" — tagged as confirmed.

### What the Broadened Search Found
**The 1663 CT petition cannot be verified.** No petition bearing John Greene's signature in 1663 appears in the Connecticut Colonial Records or in Bartlett.

The document commonly described as the "1663 petition" appears to be **conflated with the 1668 Wickford inhabitants' petition** (Bartlett Vol. II, pp. 226–227). That petition asks Connecticut to assume jurisdiction and protection over the Narragansett-country settlement.

**John Greene is NOT among the printed signers of the 1668 petition.** The signers include:
- Daniel Denison, William Hudson, Richard Smith, John Viall, Joshua Hewes, Thomas M. Joy, John Crabtree, Macklin Knight, Lawik Vandick, Thomas Fflanders, Frances Batts, Walter H. Howes, Amos Richisson, John Cole, Samuell Eldrid Sen'r, Samuell Wait, Alexander Ffenixe, David Maddocke, Jno. Paine

**However, John Greene DOES appear in Bartlett Vol. II:**
- p. 45: Warrant for "John Greene, Sen'r, living at Narragansitt"
- p. 51: Pardon for adherence to Connecticut

The **pardon for CT adherence** confirms John was involved in the CT jurisdiction dispute — but as someone who joined and was later pardoned by RI, not as a petition signer.

### Correction
| Claim | Old Tag | New Tag | Reason |
|-------|---------|---------|--------|
| "John signed the 1663 CT petition" | 🟢 PROOF | 🟡 PLAUSIBLE | Cannot verify the petition. May be conflation with 1668. |
| "John adhered to CT jurisdiction" | — | 🟡 PLAUSIBLE | Pardon at Bartlett II p. 51 confirms CT adherence, not a specific petition. |

## WHAT THIS MEANS

### For John
John Greene is documented in RI colonial records as a Kings Province official (Bartlett II pp. 255–263, 391–393), in the CSP Colonial as a named RI purchaser, and in the Potuck sloop incident during KPW. He was pardoned for CT adherence (Bartlett II p. 51). He does NOT appear in CT or MA records as an officer or administrator of those governments.

### For Joan
Joan remains invisible. She is not named in any of the records where John appears. This is expected — wives are rarely named in colonial administrative records.

### For the Sachem Network
Anashuecot's kin group is invisible outside the 1672 deed. The broader Narragansett sachem world (Pessicus, Ninecraft, Pumham, Quaiapen, Canonchet) is well-documented in Bartlett, but Anashuecot's sub-lineage never appears in any colonial administrative record. This could mean:
1. They were too local (sub-regional sachems below the colonial radar)
2. Their records are in un-digitized local sources (NK town records)
3. They were destroyed/displaced by KPW and left no post-war documentary footprint
4. The spelling variants are so extreme that searches miss them

**Option 4 is the one we can still test.** The phonetic variant registry was applied but only against digitized/published sources. The physical manuscripts (Mass. Archives Collection 30, CT Historical Society Winthrop papers) remain unsearched.
