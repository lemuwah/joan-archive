## Attack 1: H14 — Bates Grantor Contradiction as Evidence

**The claim:** Bates read Joan as co-grantor on the Daniel deed; Worth abstracted only the James deed where John signs alone. The discrepancy might reflect two different instruments.

**Attack:** This is **plausible but unprovable with current evidence** — which makes it a hypothesis, not a finding. The project has a pattern of promoting hypotheses to findings (see: the mother clause). This one must be treated with the same discipline.

**Alternative explanations for Bates's wording:**
1. **Bates paraphrased loosely.** "Greene and his wife Joan deeded" could mean "Greene deeded, and his wife Joan was named in the deed." Bates is a secondary source writing in 1918 — her prose conventions are not legal-drafting precision.
2. **Bates followed F.L. Greene's lead.** If Bates read F.L. Greene 1894 (plausible, since both cover the same subject), she may have adopted Greene's framing ("he deeded... to his sons... thirty shillings... as long as their father or mother should live") and interpreted "father or mother" as implying joint grantors.
3. **Bates read the full manuscript.** In this case, Joan really IS a co-grantor on one or both instruments, and Worth's abstract drops it. This is the Explorer's theory and it is POSSIBLE — but it requires the manuscript to contain information that the abstract omits.

**The discipline required:** Until the manuscript is read, we CANNOT distinguish between "Bates read something Worth missed" and "Bates paraphrased carelessly." Both are equally likely. The project must hold BOTH possibilities open. Per Law 1, this is a contradiction, not a finding.

**Verdict:** CONTRADICTION — HELD OPEN. Not evidence for either model until the manuscript is transcribed.

## Attack 2: H15 — Single-Appearance Analysis

**The claim:** Joan's one-appearance-in-40-years documentary profile is itself the strongest surviving evidence for Model A.

**Attack:** This is **the strongest argument the project has — and also the most dangerous one.**

**Why it's strong:** The comparison set is clean. Four of six Quidnessett-area wives have documented maiden names. Joan doesn't. The methodology is testable: anyone can check the same records for the same women. This is not linguistic speculation or phonetic chain-building — it's a straight documentary comparison.

**Why it's dangerous:** Absence-of-evidence arguments are inherently unfalsifiable. If a record surfaces showing Joan as English, the hypothesis collapses. But if no record ever surfaces, the argument gets STRONGER over time without any new positive evidence. This is the structure of an argument from ignorance.

**The critical question the project must answer:** How many other colonial women in comparable situations (frontier wives, small landholders, outside major towns) have similarly thin documentary footprints? The project has compared Joan to Quidnessett wives — but has it compared her to wives in OTHER frontier settings? If frontier wives generally have thin records, Joan's silence isn't anomalous; it's normal.

**Specific demands:**
1. **Expand the comparison set.** Include wives of small freeholders in other Narragansett frontier zones (Point Judith, Tower Hill, Wickford) — not just the Quidnessett cluster. If those wives also have thin records, Model B becomes more plausible.
2. **Include wives who WERE Indigenous.** How documented are known English-Indigenous marriages in 1680s New England? If Mary (wife of documented cross-cultural marriages) also appears in only 1–2 documents, that SUPPORTS Model A. If known Indigenous wives are MORE documented than Joan, it UNDERMINES the erasure argument.
3. **Control for the 1869 fire.** North Kingstown records burned. How many wives lost their ENTIRE paper trail to that fire? If Joan is one of a dozen women whose records were destroyed, her silence is evidence of fire, not identity.

**Verdict:** STRONGEST SURVIVING ARGUMENT — but needs a larger comparison set and a fire-loss control before it can carry Model A's weight.

## Attack 3: H16 — F.L. Greene Date Contradiction

**The claim:** F.L. Greene 1894 has John Sr. alive in 1686 and 1692, contradicting Bates's "dead before 25 Sept 1685."

**Attack:** This is a **real contradiction that requires resolution, not exploration.** The Explorer treats it as a hypothesis-generator (maybe there's a sixth John!). The Hostile Reviewer says: **read the sources first.**

**Most likely explanation:** F.L. Greene conflated the two Johns.
- The 1686 "address to the King" could be signed by Lt. John² Greene (the son, who was politically active).
- The 1692 deed witness could be Lt. John² or another John Greene entirely.
- F.L. Greene was writing in 1894 — before Bates's more rigorous 1918 analysis. He may not have distinguished father from son carefully.

**Less likely but possible:** Bates's 1685 death date is wrong. The Joseph Clark deed says "now deceased" — but does it? The Bates p. 74 image has been pulled (`images/1685_bates_1918_p74_...jpg`). Someone should read it verbatim. If it says "now deceased," that's dispositive — you can't be alive and "now deceased" on the same date.

**The Hostile Reviewer's demand:**
1. Read F.L. Greene 1894 pp. 10–11 verbatim on archive.org RIGHT NOW. What do the 1686 and 1692 entries actually say? Do they say "John Greene Sr. of Quidnessett" or just "John Greene"?
2. Read the Bates p. 74 image. Does it say "now deceased"? Verbatim, not paraphrased.

**Do NOT generate hypotheses about a sixth John until the sources are read.** This is an archival question, not an exploratory one.

**Verdict:** FACTUAL QUESTION — route to Archivist for source verification, not Explorer for speculation.

## Attack 4: H17 — "Standard Language, Anomalous Circumstance"

**The claim:** Even though the deed language is standard, the CIRCUMSTANCE (standard language applied to a woman with zero documentation) is anomalous.

**Attack:** This is **honest but weak.** The project has gone from:
- ~~"The language itself is anomalous"~~ (mother clause) → KILLED
- "The language is normal but the woman receiving it is anomalous" → H17

This is a retreat. The argument now rests on one observation: Joan has no other records. Everything else is pattern-matching. "Standard deed + undocumented woman = anomaly" is true, but it's also true of every undocumented woman who appears in a deed. The question is WHY she's undocumented, and "she's Indigenous" is one explanation among several (fire, poverty, isolation, illiteracy, late marriage, early death).

**What would make this argument stronger:** A systematic search for ALL women who appear in exactly one deed in 1680s RI/Narragansett records. If Joan is one of three, the anomaly weakens. If Joan is the ONLY one, it strengthens. This is testable.

**Verdict:** HONEST RETREAT. The argument is weaker but not dead. Needs baseline data.

## Attack 5: H18 — 1659 Awashouse-Greene Co-Occurrence

**The claim:** Awashouse and "John Greene" co-witness the 1659 Nanaquakesett deed.

**Attack:** **In a small colonial community with ~30 active English landholders and ~20 Narragansett sachems, co-occurrence on a legal instrument is EXPECTED, not surprising.** The Greene name appears on dozens of deeds. Narragansett witnesses appear on dozens of deeds. Finding them on the same deed is like finding "Smith" and "Doe" on the same corporate filing — it tells you they were both alive and local, nothing more.

**The project knows this.** Proof piece #4 established that proximity ≠ kinship. The 1659 co-occurrence is proximity evidence. It tells us Awashouse and a John Greene were both active in Narragansett land transactions in the 1650s. It does NOT tell us:
- Which John Greene this is
- Whether Awashouse = Anashuecot (still low-confidence per Cycle #4 hostile review)
- Whether any personal relationship existed
- Whether either one later married the other (or any relative of the other)

**Verdict:** DATA POINT — not evidence. Record it; do not promote it. The deed is real. The inference is not.

## Attack 6: H19 — Joan's Missing Death/Probate

**The claim:** Joan's death should have generated a probate or legal notation, and its absence extends the silence pattern.

**Attack:** **This argument runs into the 1869 fire wall.** North Kingstown records burned. The archive acknowledges this for other documents. It cannot selectively invoke the fire to explain missing records for John's will while treating the absence of Joan's death record as evidence of identity.

**However:** If Joan died AFTER 1700, and North Kingstown records from that period DO survive, then the absence IS meaningful. The question is whether there's a gap in the NK record series between the fire and whatever survives.

**Specific demand:** Before arguing from Joan's missing death record, the Archivist must establish: what is the EARLIEST surviving North Kingstown record? If it's 1700 and Joan died in 1695, the fire explains everything. If it's 1690 and Joan died in 1710, the absence matters.

**Verdict:** PREMATURE — needs archival chronology before the argument can be assessed.

### What Model A NEEDS to become a positive case again:

3. (Possibly) the 1869 fire destroying records that would have documented her

**Model B is simpler than it was in Cycle #4, because the "special explanations" count has been honestly reduced.** But it still requires Joan to be uniquely undocumented among her peers, which remains unexplained.
