## H15: Joan's Annuity as Death Marker

**Attack:** The logic is sound but the test is narrow. The 1695/96 deed might not mention the annuity even if Joan is alive — annuities weren't always recited in subsequent conveyances. A clean-title sale without annuity mention could mean Joan is dead OR that the annuity was considered a separate obligation not running with the land.

**Shadow model:** Joan dies shortly after 1682. The annuity was never paid or was paid briefly. No enforcement record because she died before it became contentious.

**Verdict:** SURVIVES — testable. But a null result (no Joan mention) is ambiguous, not conclusive. Only an explicit "Joan deceased" or "annuity still payable" resolves it.

## H16: John's Brothers as Joan's Entry Point

**Attack:** The Explorer already identified the weakness. John GAVE land to his brothers in 1675. He's the senior landholder. The brothers are dependents, not facilitators. Joan's entry through brothers who arrived AFTER Joan was already in the household makes no sense chronologically.

**Additional problem:** We don't know when James and Thomas arrived. If they came to RI after John was already established at Quidnessett, they can't have brought Joan. If they came together as a family unit in the 1630s–40s, that's different — but we have no evidence for that.

**Verdict:** WEAKENED. Worth tracking the brothers for genealogical completeness. Not a Joan search path.

## H17: Anochetuckett River = Sachem Territory Marker

**Attack:** Place names persist after people die. "Anashuecot's river" doesn't mean Anashuecot was alive in 1687 or ever lived at that specific bend. It means the English associated the sachem name with the geography. Standard colonial practice — they named places after the Indigenous people they displaced.

**What it DOES prove:** The territorial association between Anashuecot's lineage and the Quidnessett/Cocumscussoc land is documented in English records as late as 1687. That's geographic continuity.

**What it does NOT prove:** That Joan = Anashuecot. That Anashuecot was alive. That the name indicates current occupation vs. historical memory.

**Verdict:** SURVIVES as geography. Fails as personal evidence.

## H18: Missing Baptisms

**Attack:** The Explorer correctly noted this is consistent with ALL models. It's not diagnostic. The hostile test: would we expect baptisms for ANY frontier family in this region in the 1640s–60s? If not, the absence tells us nothing about Joan specifically.

**Answer:** Richard Smith's own children were presumably not baptized at Cocumscussoc either (no church). The Warwick founder's children WERE baptized — but in Salisbury, England, before emigration. The comparison is apples and oranges.

**Verdict:** PARTIALLY SURVIVES — correct observation, zero diagnostic value. Don't use this to support any model.
