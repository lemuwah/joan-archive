## Q1: Where Is This Most Likely Wrong?

### H1 (Mark exists in digitized collection)
**Weak point:** The archive's own neg-space log says the 1682 home-place deed is in "Vol. IV" and the Pawtuxet deed is in "RI Land Evidence Vol. VII" (Records of RI & Providence Plantations). Neither of these is Volume 1 of the manuscript land evidences. The Explorer correctly flags the numbering confusion but may still lead a researcher to browse the wrong volume.

**Attack:** The Preservica Volume 1 scan may not contain these deeds at all. Start with the finding aid, not the page scans. Confirm which volume system applies before committing hours to page-by-page browsing.

**Verdict:** The hypothesis is *testable* but the volume-identification step must come first. This is a search task, not a hypothesis — it either succeeds or fails.

### H2 ("Or to her mother" clause is unusual)
**Weak point:** No baseline exists. The Explorer acknowledges this but still frames the clause as "uncommon." Without a baseline study of 1680s RI dower provisions, "uncommon" is an assertion, not a finding.

**Attack:** The clause might be a standard legal protection for aging dependents. In communities without institutional poor relief, private family deeds often named collateral dependents. The clause might reflect practical caregiving, not kinship structure.

**Shadow model:** If the "mother" clause is normal practice, Joan is simply a woman whose mother was elderly and needed financial protection. The clause is a caregiving provision, not a kinship marker. This null hypothesis survives without special pleading.

**Verdict:** CANNOT EVALUATE without baseline. Must remain UNVERIFIED.

### H3 (Comparing Joan's and Awawsunks's marks)
**Weak point:** The comparison assumes marks carry identity information. In 17th-century practice, marks were often standardized by scribes — the person "making their mark" might simply touch the pen while the scribe drew the X. The mark might reflect the scribe's handwriting, not Joan's.

**Attack:** If marks are scribe-drawn (documented majority practice), comparing two marks tells us about two scribes, not about two women. The only way to extract identity information from a mark is if it is clearly self-drawn and pictographic.

**Important nuance:** The archive precedent (Absolom mark reclassification) shows that some marks do carry real information. The correct methodology is: examine first, interpret second. The Explorer gets this right.

**Verdict:** SOUND methodology if applied correctly. The risk is interpreting before examining.

### H4 (Pawtuxet deed witnesses)
**Weak point:** Gorton and Wickes were among the most prominent men in the colony. They witnessed many deeds. Their presence on Joan's deed may be unremarkable.

**Attack:** Without a baseline of Gorton/Wickes witness patterns, treating their presence as significant is selection bias.

**Shadow model:** Gorton and Wickes witnessed the deed because they were neighbors and community leaders who routinely served as witnesses. No deeper connection required.

**Verdict:** CANNOT EVALUATE without baseline. Currently overweighted by the Explorer.

### H5 (1703 Fones Record entry)
**Weak point:** The Explorer asks "who filed it?" and "why in 1703?" without first reading the existing `primary_sources/fones.md` file. The answer may already be in the archive.

**Attack:** Valid question, but do the reading first.

**Verdict:** Check existing files before logging as a new hypothesis.
