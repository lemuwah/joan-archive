Correction — September 2026: direct manuscript read of the March 1682 deed

Everything on this page below "What We Know" has, until now, come from a secondary abstract (Worth) and a secondary narrative summary (Bates). This month the underlying leaf (RI Land Evidence, North Kingstown, DGS 008204949, image 10) was read directly for the first time. Several long-standing summaries did not survive contact with the manuscript.

This read is AI-assisted (transcription support only) and has not yet been independently paleographically verified. Per the La Mance Law and the No-Algorithmic-Contamination Law, it is logged here as a correction-in-progress, not a closed fact. Full write-up in the corrections log.

We're posting this half-finished on purpose. Every item below is a place where a better-trained eye than ours could confirm, correct, or overturn what we're currently seeing. If that's you, we want to hear from you.

  • Joan is a named beneficiary, not a co-grantor. The granting clause names John Greene alone ("Know all People..."); Joan appears only in the maintenance/life-estate provision. Bates's "Greene and his wife Joan deeded" was her paraphrase of the deed's effect, not its words.
  • Joan did not sign or mark the deed. Only John Greene signed. The prior firewall entry stating Joan "signs with her mark" is retracted — no mark, no signature, nothing from Joan in the execution block. Consistent with her role as beneficiary rather than party.
  • The "her mother" clause is SUSPENDED. The manuscript reads "...during his naturall life and [Joan] his Wife for and during her naturall life..." — no third-party maternal figure appears. The "her mother" phrasing traced back to F.L. Greene's "father or mother should live," where "mother" meant Joan herself, not Joan's mother. The matrilineal-kinship argument built on "her mother" as a separate person has no surviving source and is suspended pending a fresh look.
  • The deed names him "John Green(e), Senr." — confirmed from the manuscript, not present in the Worth abstract. This resolves which of the Five Johns this specific 1682 instrument belongs to.
  • The witness "H. Tibbits" is very likely Henry Tibbitts, per F.L. Greene — whose daughter Mary later married Edward² Greene, one of John and Joan's sons. If correct, the witness is family, not a stranger: a small, concrete data point for the archive's existing seven-name convergence thread. (Identification rests on a name match via a secondary source, not yet independently confirmed. If you have Tibbitts family research, this is a good place to jump in — get in touch.)
  • Benjamin Greene has independent documentary support for the first time. The same leaf records a separate transaction: George Havens of Newport, with his wife Mary's consent, selling land to "Beny Green of Kingstown" for thirty pounds — land held "near Quidnessett." Until now Benjamin appeared only on Bates's roster with no documentation of his own. (Newly proposed addition to the sons list — PROBABLE, not yet PROVEN. If Benjamin Greene of Kingstown appears anywhere in your own research, we'd like to know.)

What We Know

The core verified facts about Joan, updated to reflect the direct manuscript read above:

  1. Joan is named as wife of John Greene, Senr., in a March 1682 land deed at Quidnessett — as a named beneficiary of a life-estate provision, not as a co-grantor.
    Source: direct manuscript read, RI Land Evidence, North Kingstown (DGS 008204949, image 10); cross-checked against the Worth abstract. AI-assisted transcription, unverified pending paleographic review.
  2. A maintenance/life-estate provision is reserved to her "for and during her naturall life," alongside a parallel provision for John.
    Source: direct manuscript read. The exact figure is an open question, not a solved one — the Worth abstract gives 30 shillings/year; the direct read appears to show a different sum in the surrounding grant to their son Daniel. We're not resolving this ourselves. If you can read secretary hand and want to look at the leaf, start here.
  3. Joan did not sign or mark the deed. Only John Greene signed.
    Source: direct manuscript read (execution block).
  4. No other record of Joan has been located.
    Source: Methodological — negative search result (as of September 2026)

What We Do Not Know

  • Whether the deed references a separate "her mother" figure — now SUSPENDED as a likely misreading (see correction above), rather than simply undocumented
  • Joan's maiden name, parents, origin, birth, death, or burial — unknown
  • The exact maintenance sum reserved to Joan and John — the Worth abstract and the direct manuscript read disagree, and we're leaving that open rather than picking one
  • Whether "H. Tibbits" is in fact Henry Tibbitts, and whether "Beny Green" is a son of John and Joan — both are leads, not established facts

These are open invitations, not dead ends. If you can read secretary hand, if you have access to the Kingstown probate or vital records, or if a name here connects to something in your own research — the original manuscript and a list of what hasn't been searched yet are both public. We would rather have more eyes on the unresolved parts than a tidier page.

Who Was She? — Seven Competing Hypotheses

The archive holds seven hypotheses about Joan's identity. Six remain open. One has been eliminated. No model is privileged. The September 2026 manuscript read above does not confirm or eliminate any of the six open models — it corrects how Joan appears in the one document we do have, which is a separate question from who she was. Each model is tested against the same evidence on the Analysis page.

Model A — Narragansett Sachem-Line Woman

⚫ OPEN

Joan's 40-year documentary silence is unique among Quidnessett wives. The region was a documented site of English–Indigenous interaction.

Model B — English Woman, Undocumented

⚫ OPEN

"Joan" is an English name in an English legal instrument. Absence of records for colonial women is common, though Joan's silence is unusually deep.

Model C — Irish Origin

⚫ OPEN

Y-DNA results point to an Irish Sea genetic cluster. Irish immigration to New England in this period is documented but under-studied.

Model D — Indentured Servant

⚫ OPEN

Would explain absence of maiden name and family records. Post-indenture marriage to a landholder is documented in the colonial period.

Model E — Enslaved Person

⚫ OPEN

Post-1676 legal framework created conditions where enslaved persons entered English households. Would explain total absence of prior identity.

Model F — Widow of Prior Marriage

⚫ OPEN

Remarriage of widows was common in colonial New England. Joan may be known by a prior husband's name in other records.

Model G — Joan Beggarly / Alice Daniels (La Mance 1904)

🔴 ELIMINATED

La Mance conflated multiple women. Alice Daniels Beggerly died c. 1643/44. Joan is alive in 1682. Timeline impossible. Wrong John Greene. Details.

The Six Laws

  1. No Narrative Smoothing If a document doesn't say it, we don't say it.
  2. La Mance Law Every claim gets traced to its origin. If the chain breaks, the claim is suspended.
  3. No Premature Disqualification No hypothesis gets thrown out until the evidence says so — not because another hypothesis looks better.
  4. No Algorithmic Contamination When AI makes an error, the error is logged and kept on record — never silently corrected.
  5. No Jurisdictional Assumption Don't assume the answer is only in Rhode Island. Check everywhere.
  6. No Centering No favorite hypothesis. All models get equal space until evidence decides.

Indigenous Perspectives

This archive is authored by an independent researcher working from colonial-era English-language records — an inherently limited and outside vantage point. Contact has been initiated with the Tomaquag Museum regarding Narragansett scholarly review. As of September 2026, no formal review has occurred.

All descriptions of Narragansett governance or cultural practice on this site are derived from non-Indigenous academic sources and are tagged 📖 EXTERNAL INTERPRETATION. This archive defers to Narragansett authority on their own history, governance, and cultural practices. That space is held, not filled.

If you are from a Narragansett or other Indigenous research background, your input carries distinct and higher weight — not folded into general feedback. A private channel is available: with "Joan Archive — Indigenous Review" in the subject line. Your correspondence will not be shared publicly without permission.
