What the Record Says

Joan Greene, 1682

Joan appears in a single colonial record — named as the wife of John Greene of Quidnessett in a March 1682 land deed. A life annuity of 30 shillings per year is reserved to her. No other record of Joan has been located.

What we do not know: Whether Joan signed or marked the deed. Whether she gave consent as a legal party. Whether the deed contains a clause referencing her mother. These questions require examination of the original manuscript (FamilySearch DGS 008204949, images 9–12), which this project has not yet accessed.
Open Question

Who was Joan?

This archive holds competing hypotheses and tests each against available colonial records. It makes no identity claim. It maps what is known, what has been searched, and what remains in silence. No model is privileged.

Model A — Narragansett

Sachem-line woman. Phonetic, geographic, temporal overlap with Anashuecot (1672 Fones deed). Hypothesis

Model B — English

Undocumented English woman. Many colonial wives left no records. Not unusual for the period. Hypothesis

Model C — Irish

Y-DNA points to Irish Sea cluster (R-Z255). Could be patrilineal only or indicate broader origin. Hypothesis

Model D — Indentured

Would explain absence of family records. Requires checking indenture and court records. Hypothesis

Model E — Enslaved

Post-1676 enslavement of Narragansett people is documented. Requires checking disposition records. Hypothesis

Model F — Widow

Remarried widow with no surviving first-marriage records. Requires probate search. Hypothesis

Model G — Other

Something we haven’t considered. The archive keeps this slot open. Hypothesis

Each model is explored on the analysis page with equal evidentiary treatment. Law 6: No Centering.

A Note on Narragansett Perspectives

This archive is authored by an independent researcher working from colonial-era English-language records — an inherently limited and outside vantage point.

Contact has been initiated with the Tomaquag Museum regarding Narragansett scholarly review. As of September 2026, no formal review by Narragansett scholars has occurred.

All descriptions of Narragansett governance or cultural practice on this site are derived from non-Indigenous academic sources and are marked with a 📖 EXTERNAL INTERPRETATION tag. This archive defers to Narragansett authority on their own history, governance, and cultural practices. That space is held, not filled.

If you are from a Narragansett or other Indigenous research background, your input carries distinct and higher weight — not folded into general feedback. A private channel is available.

Governing Framework

The Six Laws

1. No Narrative Smoothing — All contradictions logged and held open. Two facts near each other do not become one inference.

2. La Mance Law — No claim traceable to La Mance (1904) accepted without independent primary corroboration.

3. No Premature Disqualification — No hypothesis eliminated until thoroughly tested. Applies to all models A–G equally.

4. No Algorithmic Contamination — AI can suggest directions. AI cannot provide evidence. AI-generated connections are flagged, never presented as findings.

5. No Jurisdictional Assumption — No colony or archive dismissed as unlikely. Extends to all identity models.

6. No Centering — The archive investigates who Joan was, not a preferred theory. No model receives more space than its evidence warrants.

The Joan Archive — Open access research library. Primary sources are public records.
