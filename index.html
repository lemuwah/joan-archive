What We Know

The complete list of verified facts about Joan:

  1. Joan is named as wife of John Greene in a March 1682 land deed at Quidnessett.
    Source: Worth abstract, RI Land Evidences Vol. I, p. 122
  2. A life annuity of 30 shillings per year is reserved to her.
    Source: Worth abstract
  3. No other record of Joan has been located.
    Source: Methodological — negative search result (as of September 2026)

What We Do Not Know

  • Whether Joan signed or marked the deed — not documented in any source we hold
  • Whether Joan gave consent as a legal party — not documented
  • Whether the deed references her mother — conflicting secondary accounts
  • Joan's maiden name, parents, origin, birth, death, or burial — unknown

These questions require examination of the original manuscript.

Who Was She? — Seven Competing Hypotheses

The archive holds seven hypotheses about Joan's identity. Six remain open. One has been eliminated. No model is privileged. Each is tested against the same evidence on the Analysis page.

Model A — Narragansett Sachem-Line Woman

⚫ OPEN

Joan's 40-year documentary silence is unique among Quidnessett wives. The region was a documented site of English–Indigenous interaction.

Model B — English Woman, Undocumented

⚫ OPEN

"Joan" is an English name in an English legal instrument. Absence of records for colonial women is common, though Joan's silence is unusually deep.

Model C — Irish Origin

⚫ OPEN

Y-DNA results point to an Irish Sea genetic cluster. Irish immigration to New England in this period is documented but under-studied.

Model D — Indentured Servant

⚫ OPEN

Would explain absence of maiden name and family records. Post-indenture marriage to a landholder is documented in the colonial period.

Model E — Enslaved Person

⚫ OPEN

Post-1676 legal framework created conditions where enslaved persons entered English households. Would explain total absence of prior identity.

Model F — Widow of Prior Marriage

⚫ OPEN

Remarriage of widows was common in colonial New England. Joan may be known by a prior husband's name in other records.

Model G — Joan Beggarly / Alice Daniels (La Mance 1904)

🔴 ELIMINATED

La Mance conflated multiple women. Alice Daniels Beggerly died c. 1643/44. Joan is alive in 1682. Timeline impossible. Wrong John Greene. Details.

The Six Laws

  1. No Narrative Smoothing If a document doesn't say it, we don't say it.
  2. La Mance Law Every claim gets traced to its origin. If the chain breaks, the claim is suspended.
  3. No Premature Disqualification No hypothesis gets thrown out until the evidence says so — not because another hypothesis looks better.
  4. No Algorithmic Contamination When AI makes an error, the error is logged and kept on record — never silently corrected.
  5. No Jurisdictional Assumption Don't assume the answer is only in Rhode Island. Check everywhere.
  6. No Centering No favorite hypothesis. All models get equal space until evidence decides.

Indigenous Perspectives

This archive is authored by an independent researcher working from colonial-era English-language records — an inherently limited and outside vantage point. Contact has been initiated with the Tomaquag Museum regarding Narragansett scholarly review. As of September 2026, no formal review has occurred.

All descriptions of Narragansett governance or cultural practice on this site are derived from non-Indigenous academic sources and are tagged 📖 EXTERNAL INTERPRETATION. This archive defers to Narragansett authority on their own history, governance, and cultural practices. That space is held, not filled.

If you are from a Narragansett or other Indigenous research background, your input carries distinct and higher weight — not folded into general feedback. A private channel is available: email wendyleagreen@yahoo.com with "Joan Archive — Indigenous Review" in the subject line. Your correspondence will not be shared publicly without permission.
