About This Archive

Methodology, corrections, AI disclosure, and how to cite.

Who Maintains This

The Joan Archive is maintained by Wendy Green, an independent historical researcher. This is a self-funded, unaffiliated project. The archive has no institutional backing, no grant funding, and no academic appointment behind it.

What it does have: primary sources, transparent methodology, public corrections, and a commitment to getting Joan's story right rather than fast.

How This Archive Reads Colonial Records

The documentary record for 17th-century Rhode Island is overwhelmingly colonial in origin. Deeds, probate files, court records, and town books were created to document English property, English inheritance, and English governance.

This archive treats colonial records as partial instruments:

  • Reliable for documenting land transactions, boundaries, and English legal procedure.
  • Unreliable for documenting Indigenous identity, kinship, or social structure.
  • Silent on many women, Indigenous persons, and unfree persons — by design, not by accident.

The absence of Indigenous women from these documents should not be interpreted as absence from history. See the full epistemological statement: Epistemology.md.

The Six Laws

All content in this archive — human-written and AI-assisted — is governed by six laws:

  1. No Narrative Smoothing — contradictions are logged, not resolved by invention.
  2. La Mance Law — no claim traceable to La Mance (1904) accepted without independent primary corroboration.
  3. No Premature Disqualification — no hypothesis eliminated until tested against primary sources.
  4. No Algorithmic Contamination — every AI error is logged, not silently corrected.
  5. No Jurisdictional Assumption — no repository dismissed without being checked.
  6. No Centering — no identity model privileged over others without evidence.

Full text: The Six Laws.

Corrections Policy

This archive makes mistakes. When we find them, we document the fix publicly — what was wrong, how we caught it, what changed, and what's still open. This is what peer-reviewed journals do. It's what keeps the archive credible.

Corrections are weighted by type — see the Contribute page for the full priority system. In brief: a correction from Narragansett or other Indigenous authority restructures the archive; a primary source contradiction triggers immediate review; methodology and general errors are fixed in order.

Current corrections: CORRECTIONS.md.

Indigenous Review Status

Contact has been initiated with the Tomaquag Museum regarding Narragansett scholarly review of this archive's content. As of September 2026, no formal review has occurred.

All descriptions of Narragansett governance, cultural practice, or political structure on this site are derived from non-Indigenous academic sources and are tagged 📖 EXTERNAL INTERPRETATION. This archive defers to Narragansett authority on their own history.

AI Methodology Disclosure

This archive uses AI tools (multiple models) as research assistants. AI is used for:

  • Searching and cross-referencing sources
  • Drafting structured analysis for human review
  • Running multi-agent research cycles (Explorer, Hostile Reviewer, Archivist, Synthesizer)
  • Identifying contradictions, contamination, and gaps

AI is never used as a source. Every AI-generated claim must be independently verified against primary sources. Every identified AI error is logged in the Contamination Log. See AI Hallucination Checklist.
