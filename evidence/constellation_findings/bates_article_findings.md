## Key Finding

**Joan is named only in Bates's genealogical reconstruction — NOT from an independent primary source quotation.**

Bates writes "John(1) and Joan Greene" when listing children (e.g., "Lieutenant John² Greene of Newport, son of John¹ and Joan Greene, was born probably about 1640"), but in the examined pages she never quotes a document that names Joan directly beyond what the 1682 deed provides.

**Implication:** Bates likely derived Joan's name from the 1682 homeplace deed (which we already have). This article does NOT constitute a second independent source for Joan's identity. The Bates citation chain points back to the same single document.

## New Leads Generated

### LEAD A: James Greene → George Wightman Deed (Feb 15, 1695/96)
- **Priority:** HIGH
- **Why:** This is the annuity trail. If Joan was still alive in 1695/96, the 30-shilling annual annuity from the 1682 deed would appear as an encumbrance on this transaction. If Joan was dead, it wouldn't. This gives us a potential death bracket.
- **Where to search:** NK Land Records, FamilySearch microfilm, or published abstracts
- **Roadmap item:** #32 (annuity trail — James)

### LEAD B: Caleb Carr → John Greene Deed (Jan 3, 1651)
- **Priority:** MEDIUM
- **Why:** This is the land acquisition chain — how John got the Quidnessett land. The 1651 deed may name parties, witnesses, or conditions. Was Joan already his wife in 1651? Does the deed mention a wife?
- **Where to search:** NK Land Records, published abstracts
- **Roadmap item:** #35 (John Greene acquisition deed)

### LEAD C: Lt. John² Greene's Nuncupative Will (Sept 4, 1694)
- **Priority:** LOW for Joan directly
- **Why:** This is the SON's will, not the father's. It does not name Joan. However, it may reference family property or surviving relatives. Worth reading for network context.

### LEAD D: East Greenwich House Lot Allotment (May 14, 1685)
- **Priority:** LOW
- **Why:** After John's death. May reference "heirs of John Greene" or "widow Greene." Worth checking.

Bates is a careful genealogist but she does not add a second primary source for Joan's name. Her value is in the citation chain — she points to specific documents we can now chase (especially the Wightman deed and the Carr deed).
