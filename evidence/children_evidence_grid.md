## WHAT EACH "SOURCE" ACTUALLY IS

These are cited as if they are birth registers. They are NOT.

- **Arnold's Vital Records of Rhode Island** — A finding aid compiled from town records. NOT a complete 17th-century civil registration. Useful for later marriages and deaths, NOT for proving early births.
- **Bartlett's Colony Records** — Colonial government proceedings: land, court, military. NOT a birth register.
- **Austin's Genealogical Dictionary** — Compressed inference from deeds/wills/marriages. Looks like a fact sheet. IS a compilation with no individual primary source citations per child.
- **Savage's Genealogical Dictionary** — Covers the Warwick founder primarily. Should NOT be used uncritically for Quidnessett children.
- **F.L. Greene** — Separates Warwick from other branches. Uses Salisbury parish for founder. For Quidnessett, uses inferred kinship.
- **Bates (1918)** — NK town history. Contains claims about Joan (mark, consent) that have been 🔴 DISCREDITED by Image 10.

**Rule:** A name in a compilation = 🟡 PLAUSIBLE. A name on a manuscript image = 🟢 PROOF — AI TRANSCRIPTION. No exceptions.

## THE MISSING BAPTISMS — SIGNIFICANCE

The Warwick founder's children were baptized in Salisbury. Our John's children have NO baptisms anywhere. This is not just a records gap:

- Quidnessett had no church in the 1640s–1660s
- Smith's trading post was a frontier outpost, not a parish
- If Joan was Indigenous → no baptism expected (Model A)
- If Joan was Irish Catholic → no Protestant minister would baptize (Model C)
- If Joan was English Protestant on the frontier → maybe no baptism due to isolation (Model B)
- If Joan was a servant or enslaved person → baptism unlikely (Models D/E)

The absence is consistent with multiple models. It does not prove any one of them. Held open.
