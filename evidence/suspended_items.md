## S-001: "Joan signed with a mark"

**Previously stated:** Joan signed the March 1682 deed with her mark.  
**Why suspended:** No source says this for the March 1682 deed. Worth's abstract does not mention Joan signing. Bates's "and his wife Joan deeded" is summary language, not a description of a mark. The May 1682 Pawtuxet deed (where "her mark" was claimed) is itself ⚫ SUSPENDED per CORRECTIONS.md #1.  
**Resolution:** Manuscript examination (images 9–12).  
**Date suspended:** 2026-09-02  
**Decision:** D-001

## S-004: "Or to her mother if she survive"

**Previously stated:** The annuity clause included reversion to Joan's mother.  
**Why suspended:** Not in any source the archive holds. F.L. Greene says "their father or mother" (= the sons' parents). Worth says "after his decease to Joane Greene, his wife." NK manuscript says "dureing her naturall life." See `contradictions/mother-clause-wording.md`.  
**Resolution:** Manuscript examination.  
**Date suspended:** 2026-08-31 (per contradiction investigation)  
**Decision:** D-001 (formalized Sep 2)

---

## S-005: Joan as co-grantor

**Previously stated:** Joan acted as co-grantor in the March 1682 deed.  
**Why suspended:** Bates's summary language ("Greene and his wife Joan deeded") does not confirm co-grantor status. Worth describes John Greene conveying land; Joan is named as wife and annuity beneficiary. Whether she was a legal party to the conveyance is not established.  
**Resolution:** Manuscript examination.  
**Date suspended:** 2026-09-02  
**Decision:** D-001

## S-006: Joan as legal actor

**Previously stated:** Joan's inclusion in the deed demonstrates legal agency.  
**Why suspended:** This inference rests on the co-grantor assumption (S-005). If Joan is a beneficiary only (as Worth's abstract suggests), her inclusion does not demonstrate independent legal action.  
**Resolution:** Depends on S-005.  
**Date suspended:** 2026-09-02  
**Decision:** D-001
