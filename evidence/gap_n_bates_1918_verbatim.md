# GAP-N: Bates 1918 pp. 74 + 76 — RESOLVED (2026-09-04)

## Source
Louise Prosser Bates, "John Greene of Newport and Narragansett," *Rhode Island Historical Society Collections*, vol. XI, no. 3 (July 1918), pp. 69–78.  
[Internet Archive full text](https://archive.org/stream/rhodeislandv11to14rhoduoft/rhodeislandv11to14rhoduoft_djvu.txt)

## Verbatim Extracts

### Page 74 — The 1685 Evidence
> "September 25, 1685, Joseph Clark, above, of Newport, sold the land which was bounded south by Caleb Carr's land which was formerly in possession of **'John Greene, sr., of Narragansett, now deceased,'** to Francis Brinley. [Jamestown Records.]"

### Page 76 — Bates's Conclusion
> "In 1682 he seems to have begun to settle up his affairs. On February 4, 1682, Edward Greenman acknowledged, for himself and brother David, the deed he had given Greene to land in Newport in 1647. **Greene and his wife Joan deeded to their sons, Daniel and James, land in Quidnisset in return for thirty shillings a year paid by each of them so long as either parent lived.** This was March 24, 1681/2. He left a will, as is shown by the deeds of his son Edward, but it was doubtless destroyed in the North Kingstown fire. **He died before September 25, 1685.**"

## 🚨 KEY FINDINGS

### 1. Bates's Evidence Is a Specific, Checkable Document
Bates cites the **Jamestown Records** — a September 25, 1685 land sale from Joseph Clark to Francis Brinley, in which a parcel is described as bounded by land "formerly in possession of **John Greene, sr., of Narragansett, now deceased**." 

This is NOT a vague assertion. It's a citation to a specific colonial record with a date, parties, and a repository. It can be verified.

### 2. Bates's "Deeded" Language — AGENT_GUARDRAILS §1 Applies
Bates writes: "**Greene and his wife Joan deeded** to their sons." This is Bates's summary, not a quote from the deed. The NK manuscript (Image 10, AI read) shows John alone in the granting clause, Joan in the life-estate/annuity provision only, and only John signing. Per AGENT_GUARDRAILS §1: transcribed narratives are not verbatim quotes. Bates's "deeded" = her description of the transaction, not Joan's legal role in the instrument.

### 4. Three Possible Resolutions

**Resolution A: Five-John Firewall.** The "John Greene, sr., of Narragansett" in the 1685 Jamestown deed is a DIFFERENT John Greene from the Quidnessett John. Multiple Johns operated in the Narragansett region. The "sr." designation alone doesn't uniquely identify our John.

**Resolution B: Bates is right, F.L. Greene is wrong.** The 1686 and 1692 appearances F.L. Greene cites are a different John, and our John really did die before September 1685. F.L. Greene's uncited sources may have conflated two Johns.

**Resolution C: F.L. Greene is right, Bates's interpretation is wrong.** The Jamestown deed's "now deceased" may refer to John's status relative to the Newport land only — i.e., he had divested himself of that property, making him legally "deceased" as to that parcel. (This is a stretch under colonial legal language, but held open per Law 3.)

**The archive cannot resolve this without reading both source documents:**
1. The Jamestown Town Records — the September 25, 1685 Clark-to-Brinley deed
2. The 1686 "address to the King" and the 1692 witnessed deed that F.L. Greene references

### 5. What This Means for Joan

If Bates is right (dead by 1685), Joan was a widow by age ~[unknown] and the thirty-shillings annuity from Daniel and James was her sole documented income after John's death.

If F.L. Greene is right (alive until ~1692–1696), John and Joan may have been alive together for another 10–14 years after the 1682 deed. The archive silence around Joan — "nothing further is known about her" — becomes even more striking: she left zero further documentary traces across an additional decade.

Either way, Joan's disappearance from the record after 1682 is total. Whether John died in 1685 or 1696, she is never mentioned again.
